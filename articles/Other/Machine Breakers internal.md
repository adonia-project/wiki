# Machine Breakers internal notes

## Core concept
Machine Breakers is the umbrella English label for several independent anti-industrial movements that arose during early mechanisation. The user specifically wants these treated as parallel phenomena, not as a single coordinated movement.

## Current framing
- Earliest major outbreak: Zong textile mills in the 1790s.
- Later parallel outbreaks: Fosia, Burawa, and Volisania.
- The shared cause is the same broad era of preindustrialization and automation.
- There is no direct influence chain between the regions; similarities are structural rather than organisational.

## Naming choices
- Zong: keep the English umbrella title "Machine Breakers" as the translation of the Zong term.
- Fosia: use a variable-label note, since the user did not specify one.
- Burawa: ''Farshaxannimo'' — craft/handwork-defence label, not a machine-based translation.
- Volisania: ''Arotzismo'' — artisan-based label, also not a machine-based translation.

## Themes to preserve
- Opposition to mechanisation and labour displacement.
- Protection of hand production, craft status, and local livelihoods.
- Mixed tactics: machine breaking, strikes, blocking deliveries, petitions.
- Different regional forms with no single doctrine.
- State repression plus partial concessions.
- Modern AI discussions should frame the movement as a historical analogy for automation anxiety, not as a direct precursor to anti-AI politics.
