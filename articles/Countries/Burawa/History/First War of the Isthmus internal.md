## Key Decisions (2026-08-20)

### Emperor Succession
- Emperor Tianzheng died June 1711 (before word of the attack reached the Zong homeland)
- Word of Barcelona peninsula capture sent mid-May 1711, arrived August 1711 (4-month voyage)
- Succeeded by adult son Tianli (天理, "Heavenly Principle") — regarded as "meek" replacement
- Tianli seized upon news of Barcelona capture (August 1711) as opportunity to prove himself
- Societal Assembly enthusiastically endorsed war; imperialist and anti-Volisania disposition
- Tianli: born c. 1670, reigned 1711–c. 1740, died c. 1740 (~age 70). Added to emperor list.
- Note: "Tianwei" (天威) was already used by a claimant emperor (1653, died 1671, never recognized) — could not reuse

### Theaters (confirmed by user)
1. Shendan Ocean theater (naval operations off Balboa coast) — primary
2. Sinchew coastal raid theater
3. Sinchew–Balboa border land theater — MOST DEVASTATING theater
- No fighting elsewhere

### Naval Dynamics (confirmed by user 2026-08-20, revised 2026-08-20)
- Naval war was heavily favoured for Zong in the opening phase
- Most of Volisania's Royal Navy was on the Aethiopean (eastern) side of the isthmus
- Shendan approaches were lightly defended → Zong established early naval dominance
- This is why Sant Cristòfor fell in October 1711 (relatively rapid capture)
- Volisania gradually redeployed naval forces from Aethiopean side to Shendan theater
  - Required sailing around the continent of Abyala — approximately 6 months voyage
  - Gradual arrival shifted balance over 1712-1713
  - Sufficient strength achieved by 1715 for counter-blockade
- The counter-blockade isolated the Zong expeditionary force within Sant Cristòfor

### Hybrid Outbreak (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- Ships incident was REAL but minor — trading ships were refueling, not impressed
- Admiral Lim Kiat-seng exaggerated the incident as pretext to send flotilla
- Admiral controlled information: forced Governor Tân Bûn-seng to halt ALL communications to Zong homeland and Louyang until a success was reported
- Communications blackout meant Zong court did not know about the attack until months later
- Word of Barcelona peninsula capture sent mid-May 1711; arrived August 1711 (4-month voyage)
- Tianzheng died June 1711 (before word arrived)
- Tianli received news August 1711, approved war, sent reinforcements
- Official approval came AFTER the attack was already underway

### Civilian-Military Tension (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- Civilian colonial governments (Louyang, Sinchew) wanted domestic economy development
- Royal Zong Navy controlled by central state, not colonial governments
- Navy's role had diminished since 1600s — mostly route protection and rebellion suppression
- Navy admirals wanted glory like 15th-16th century commanders
- Admirals saw Sant Cristòfor as chance to reclaim navy's martial prestige
- Louyang governor hesitant to commit forces without court authorisation
- 3rd, 8th, 12th Armadas stationed at Louyang; admirals acted on own initiative to support siege
- 1st, 2nd Armadas already at Tamsui (for planned Free Sinchew assault buildup)

### Military Buildup at Tamsui (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- Tianzheng authorised military preparations at Tamsui for planned assault on Free Sinchew
- 1st and 2nd Armadas stationed at Tamsui as part of buildup
- 3rd, 8th, 12th Armadas stationed at Louyang
- Original plan: land campaign against Free Sinchew + naval assault on Sant Cristòfor
- Admiral Lim Kiat-seng used the ships incident as pretext to launch the naval assault early
- The flotilla attack (18 April) preceded the planned Free Sinchew land campaign (August 1711)

### Land War Details (confirmed by user 2026-08-20, revised 2026-08-20)
- The land war was VERY devastating compared to the naval war
- Two distinct types of land fighting:
  1. FIELD BATTLES near Sant Cristòfor: open terrain, conventional line tactics
     - Volisania: dragoons, light cavalry for flanking and reconnaissance
     - Zong: infantry-heavy, naval artillery support from fleet in harbour
     - Two named battles: Battle of Varakí (1712), Battle of la Riera (1714)
  2. JUNGLE FIGHTING along Sinchew-Balboa border: chaotic, ambushes
     - Free Sinchew used shortened carbines and shorter-barrelled rifles from start (Volisanian-supplied)
     - Sinchewese initially used standard flintlock muskets (disadvantage in dense vegetation)
     - Sinchewese adapted later by shortening musket barrels → improvised carbines
     - Dense vegetation rendered conventional line tactics impractical

### Free Sinchew Land Campaign (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- August 1711: Sinchew Army Corps sent through Free Sinchew territory
  - Sinchew expected Free Sinchew to fold or let them pass
  - Free Sinchew attacked instead — first battle between 1st Forward Battalion and Free Sinchew forces
  - Major Lîm Tek-bûn (林德文) commanded 1st Forward Battalion
  - Sinchew saw naval attack on Sant Cristòfor as convenient excuse to invade Free Sinchew
  - "Looked like it was going to be easy after taking Sant Cristòfor"
- ~1 week after first battle: Free Sinchew sent correspondence to Volisanian contacts
  - Volisania granted free passage for arms
  - Volisania supplied heavy arms for the protracted jungle war
- Early battles: Sinchew battalions LOSING
  - Free Sinchew used guerrilla tactics + Volisanian heavy arms
- October 1711 (concurrent with fall of Sant Cristòfor):
  - Sinchew asked landowners near Free State to contribute slaves to fight
  - Promise of money after the war → ORIGIN of slave-backed loan system
  - State loans secured against value of enslaved labourers
  - Slave regiments deployed in most dangerous jungle sectors

### Field Battles (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- Battle of Varakí (1712): Volisanian counter-offensive along the Camí Ral
  - Fought at Varakí (existing place — city on Lake Jarumá, Cumará comarca, Estret Province)
  - Varakí name derives from Birú "warakí" ("peaceful water")
  - Volisanian cavalry (dragoons, light horse) outflanked Zong infantry
  - TURNING POINT: halted Zong expansion along the Camí Ral
  - Volisanians pushed Zong back to encampments at Sant Martí (now a ciutat/district of Sant Cristòfor)
  - Zong contracted perimeter closer to Sant Cristòfor
- Battle of la Riera (1714): Zong defensive victory near Sant Cristòfor
  - Named for creek crossing the battlefield
  - Zong entrenched behind field fortifications + naval artillery from fleet
  - Repelled repeated Volisanian cavalry and infantry assaults
  - Stabilised Zong perimeter but no breakout possible

### Fought primarily by Sinchew and Balboan forces (not Zong mainland troops)
- Balboan slaves were impressed into military service by Volisanian colonial administration
  - Used as labourers, porters, and front-line combatants
  - Placed in most exposed/dangerous positions
  - Disproportionately high casualties
- Disease ran rampant in the isthmian jungle
  - Malaria, dysentery, tropical fevers
  - Disease casualties exceeded combat casualties in many cases
  - Especially lethal to non-tropical troops and enslaved Balboans
- First large-scale use in Lurandian theater of new military technologies:
  - Flintlock muskets (replacing matchlocks — more reliable in humid jungle)
  - Socket bayonets (eliminating need for separate pike formations)
  - Shortened carbines and shorter-barrelled rifles (jungle adaptation)
  - These technologies increased lethality and contributed to high casualties

### Outbreak Timeline (comedy of errors — confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- 3 March 1711: Zong-flagged trading ships dock at harbor of Sant Cristòfor to refuel
- 18 March 1711: False report reaches Zong naval station at Tamsui alleging ships impressed
- 20 March 1711: Expeditionary vessel departs Tamsui to investigate
- 30 March 1711: Expeditionary vessel arrives Sant Cristòfor; sees ships at anchor; assumes detained; departs to report
- 3 April 1711: Trading ships complete refueling and depart Sant Cristòfor homeward
- 7 April 1711: Expeditionary vessel returns to Tamsui; Governor Tân Bûn-seng proposes diplomacy; Admiral Lim Kiat-seng overrides, orders flotilla deployment
- 8 April 1711: Zong flotilla departs Tamsui (2,600.749 km coastal voyage)
- 11 April 1711: Trading ships pass flotilla heading opposite direction (ships already safe)
- 17 April 1711: Volisanian spotters detect fleet approaching; warn Balboan colonial governor Jordi de Montclar
- 18 April 1711: Zong flotilla enters harbor; Volisanian forces open cannon fire; war begins

### Casus Belli (expanded 2026-08-20)

#### Zong's Land Invasion Plans
- Zong originally planned a LAND invasion to take the Camí Ral and reclaim Free Sinchew territory
- Military planner: Ong Tek-bûn (王德文) drafted the plans
- Required moving troops through Sinchew into the isthmian jungle toward Sant Cristòfor
- Thick jungle made this impassable for conventional military columns
- Zong started large-scale deforestation in the frontier zone to clear paths
- Resistance from:
  - Baiduist Sinchewese population (environmental order beliefs)
  - Karawã and Apyãwa peoples (territorial defence)
  - Free Sinchew confederation coordinated the resistance
- Land invasion blocked → Zong shifted to naval assault strategy

#### Free Sinchew
- Territory between Sinchew and Balboa effectively controlled by Free Sinchew since the 1660s
- Union of Sinchewese holdouts (from Sinchew War 1648-1656, didn't capitulate, retreated into forests) and native tribes
- Two indigenous tribes: Karawã and Apyãwa (Lurandian-style names)
- Governed by equal council of tribal chiefs and Sinchewese military leaders
- Council Speaker (first among equals): Tân Huat-seng (陳法生) — Hokkien-Baiduist name, second/third-generation Sinchewese born in the jungle
- Fighting Free Sinchew was a CO-EQUAL objective with taking the Camí Ral

#### Volisania's Diplomatic Strategy
- Wanted Sinchew to become a Volisanian ally
- Tried to court the Sinchewese, weaken economic links with Sant Cristòfor
- 1672: Volisania publicly declared support for Free Sinchewese Army → political freeze with Zong
- Waited ~50 years before acting — government was anti-full-out-war
- Preferred building trade links with Zong's neighbors like Yanbaru
- Yanbaru trade connected to Cinc Parets-Macaio galleon trade (1608-1850)
  - Galleons made landfall at Kerama islands in Yanbaru
  - Yanbaruan pearls traded at Cinc Parets fair
  - This trade network reduced Volisania's dependence on Zong cooperation

#### Free Sinchew Proclamation
- Issued after military collapse of Free Sinchew at the Battle of Karawãtyba in February 1715
- Presented as a proposed "peace" treaty — essentially an ultimatum
- "Give up or be wiped out"
- Those who capitulated were absorbed into Sinchew colonial population
- Those who refused were killed or captured
- All Free Sinchew leadership tried and executed
- Karawã and Apyãwa communities dispersed — mostly killed or captured
- Survivors absorbed into Sinchew, losing distinct political identity

### Counter-offensive / 1715 Stalemate (confirmed by user, revised 2026-08-20)
- After fall of Sant Cristòfor in October 1711, Volisania gradually redeployed naval forces from Aethiopean side to Shendan theater
- Redeployment required sailing around continent of Abyala (~6 months voyage); gradual arrival shifted balance over 1712-1713
- Battle of Varakí (1712): turning point — Volisanian counter-offensive halted Zong expansion along Camí Ral, pushed Zong back to Sant Martí encampments
- Battle of la Riera (1714): Zong defensive victory, stabilised perimeter
- By 1715, sufficient Volisanian naval strength for counter-blockade
- Volisanian Admiral Bernat de Vilanova blockaded Shendan approaches and surrounded Zong on Península de la Barcelona and inner city walls
- Zong navy maintained supply lines to mainland; resupplied the garrison by sea
- Volisanian forces conducted periodic raids every few weeks but could not dislodge Zong
- 6-month stalemate (approximately mid-1715 to early 1716)
- Burawi Admiral Xirsi Xaashi raided Zong supply lines
- General Oriol March led land assault attempts
- Neither side could achieve military decision → negotiations opened directly between commanders

### Maroon Corsairs (confirmed by user)
- Don Ismael el-Nabulsi led Maroon corsairs as privateers (not formal coalition members)
- Harassed Zong shipping in Shendan Ocean
- Operated semi-independently

### Treaty of Sant Cristòfor (confirmed by user)
1. Zong withdraws from Sant Cristòfor
2. Zong receives docking and passage rights through Volisanian ports
3. Camí Ral remains under Volisanian control
4. Isthmian corridor established as matter of international concern
5. Sant Cristòfor designated a free port
6. Status quo ante borders between Sinchew and Balboa restored
7. Reunification of Free Sinchew into Sinchew (by military conquest) recognised
- NO indemnity (user removed)
- NO Breisland/Merica port guarantees (user removed)
- Treaty signed 12 January 1716 at Sant Cristòfor (not at a neutral venue)

### Other Details
- Breisland and Merica: sold goods and weapons to Zong, shared intelligence — covert supporters, not belligerents
- Galwa: neutral (post-1709 Imperial Split, Sahra's regency)
- Maros/Sultanate of Maros: neutral
- Guajicutea: supported Volisanian war effort with shipbuilding at Waribá; post-war, cheap Zong goods killed local cotton industry

### Sinchewese Slavery and the War (confirmed by user 2026-08-20)
- Sinchewese state raised huge slave regiments by borrowing slaves from landowners (state loans secured against slave value)
- ~5,000 enslaved Sinchewese deployed, ~3,000 killed (~60% mortality rate)
- After the war, landowners demanded compensation for lost slaves
- Zong refused: slaves "did not legally exist" per Edict of 1500 (Secret Trade context)
  - Secret Trade: system of slavery in Sinchew from late 16th century
  - Enslaved Dagit and Marosian peoples
  - Zong Edict of 1500 banned slavery on mainland, ambiguous for colonies
  - Secret Trade article: https://talod.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Secret_Trade
- 1717: Landowners' rebellion — squashed (caused by Zong's refusal to compensate for lost slaves)
- 1718: Emperor Tianli banned slavery in ALL Zong lands (extended Edict of 1500 to colonies)
  - Refused to acknowledge slave-backed loans (no slaves = no loans)
  - Sinchew forced to emancipate all slaves
  - Economic crash in Sinchew (plantation economy collapsed)
  - Zong provided no financial assistance
- The economic devastation + political repression → Sinchewese Revolutionary War (1726–1733) → modern Sinchewese state
