# Moved to [[Farisnimo internal.md]]

The article and internal notes were renamed from Farism to Farisnimo.

## Intended chronology
- Late 1830s: emergence among dockworkers, miners, railway labourers, and shipyard employees.
- 1840s: growth of workers' associations, petitioning, and early union organisation.
- 1850s: stronger labour agitation, the Jowhar Riots, and the Free Women's Movement current.
- Late 1850s onward: decline as a distinct movement, with ideas absorbed into later union and reform politics.

## Main thematic points
- Rose Revolution reforms were not enough for wage labourers.
- The movement responded to the growth of a capitalist/merchant class in the 1840s and 1850s.
- It pushed labour protections and political liberalism.
- It initially supported universal suffrage in a male-coded form, but a women-led current later pressed for real universal suffrage.
- It had strong roots in mining, rail, dock, and shipbuilding labour.
- The name derives from Burawi ''faris'' meaning "seat" or "place of representation".

## Names used in the article
- [[Aadan Warsame]] — pamphleteer and labour writer.
- [[Suleymaan Dheer]] — wage-reform writer.
- [[Fadumo Warsame]] — women’s suffrage advocate, also a Farist pamphleteer.
- [[Maryan Cabdi]] — women’s movement organiser.

## Key institutions / events
- Burawa Workers Union: main organisational expression of the movement.
- Jowhar Riots: dockworker killed by hired security officer at Jowhar Shipbuilding Corporation; dispute over weekly payment escalated into riots.
- Free Women's Movement in Burawa: later label for the women-led Farist current in the 1850s.
