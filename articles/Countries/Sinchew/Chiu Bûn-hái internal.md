## 1. Dates — supplied by the user

**1 April 1474 – 9 June 1543, aged 69.** Born at [[Huwei]], died at [[Tamsui]].

**A near-miss in the transcript:** the user first wrote *"we can write about Sūn-heng Hâng who died in jun 9 1543, born april 1 1474"*, then corrected to **"sorry i meant Chiu Bûn-hái"**. The dates belong to **the man, not the company.**

**Why the misreading was tempting:** 1474–1543 is *exactly* the span of [[Colonial Zong]] — Anping founded 1474, colony divided 1543. The company's dates would have looked identical, and I nearly wrote the article for the wrong subject. **Lesson: when dates fit two candidate subjects equally, ask before writing.**

## 2. What the dates unlock — he was alive for everything

| year | age | event |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Apr 1474** | — | born — **the year [[Anping]] was founded** |
| by 1500s | 20s | a merchant of Huwei; the Sūn-heng Hâng established at some unrecorded point |
| **1511** | **37** | **takes the grant at Oktalwa for ten pigs** |
| **1521** | **47** | **marries his son to Chinowa** |
| 1527 | 53 | Hokta dies of plague; his son becomes miko and King of Okheng |
| **1535** | **61** | **watches his son sell Okheng for 4,000 taels of gold** |
| **9 Jun 1543** | **69** | **dies at Tamsui** — the year of the Tamsui Plot, his son's removal, and the colony's division |

**THE BIG ONE: he lived to see the kingdom sold.** I had earlier flagged "was his father still alive in 1535 to see the kingdom sold" as *the most powerful unwritten scene in the scenario*. **He was alive, and he was 61.** The land sold was the plot he took in 1511 for ten pigs; the territory was the one he spent twenty-four years enlarging. **He watched his son sign it away and died eight years later.**

**He also died in the same year as his son's fall.** 1543 brought the Tamsui Plot, CKG's removal, the colony's division, and his own death in June. He did not live to see the year out.

**And he was born the year Anping was founded** — his life spans the entire colonial project in the west, from its first settlement to its partition.

## 4. Register decisions
- **No motive attributed.** The article records what he did and what the administration wrote about it. It does **not** say he wanted a kingdom, though that is the user's framing — the evidence is the conduct, and the article says so in its closing section.
- **The record section is the strongest part:** *"No document written by Chiu Bûn-hái or at his direction is known… The evidence for his purposes is therefore the evidence of what he did, and the papers of the administration that declined to intervene in it."*
- **The Okheng naming point is now in his article** — 沃興 carries the company's name (興) inside that of the polity it made. His firm is in the name of the state he built.

## 5. Open questions
1. **When was the Sūn-heng Hâng founded?** Unknown; it appears in the papers only from 1511. **A founding date would be worth having** — and if it were 1474, the company and the colony would share a birth year.
2. **Whom did he marry?** Unrecorded, as is the date. CKG is his only recorded child.
3. **Did he approve of the sale in 1535?** He was 61, alive, and the head of the house that had built the territory. **The record does not say whether he was party to the decision to comply.**
4. **Did he live at Oktalwa/Tamsui throughout**, or move between there and Huwei? The article assumes his family removed to the settlement in 1511 because CKG was brought up there from age six.
5. **What happened to the Sūn-heng Hâng after him?** He died in 1543, the year his son lost office. Did the company continue, wind up, or pass to his grandchildren? **This is where a `Sūn-heng Hâng` article would begin.**
6. **Was he implicated in the Tamsui Plot?** His son was. He died in June of that year, and the record does not say whether he was involved or merely present.
