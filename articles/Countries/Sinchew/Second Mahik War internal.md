## 1. The user's framing — the material this was built from
*"the end of the second Mahik War that saw the coquest of the salowi and chidoha, which I think began in 1549 to 1558, the cause was actually by the start of the land grant system that was used to divide the new spoils from the first mahik war to anyone who paid their way to sinchew…"* — followed by a long account of the grants, the crops, the labour, the General Assembly, the fisheries, the mercenaries, and the secrecy.

**Answers to my five questions:**
1. **The Edict of 1500 DID ban slavery in all of Zong.** It was simply **not enforced** in the New World, and **the colonial governments were complicit**. *(Not a legal grey area — unimplemented law. The earlier articles say "grey area"; that phrasing may want revisiting.)*
2. **The crown probably knew and turned a blind eye** — because those enslaved were **not Zong**.
3. **The General Assembly of 1549 is the ANCESTOR of the Zong General Assembly**, which it probably inspired — but it **was not electoral; it was a forum**.
4. **The grant was like headright** — **100 acres initially, later 50**. *(The user first said 10 acres in the same message; 100→50 is the ruling.)*
5. **Not treated as a war in contemporary works** — also called **the Secret Sinchew War**. Modern historians recognise it as a war: the records show deaths and displacement, and **the Hahkina wrote that it was a war they were supporting the Zong in.**

## 2. The Hahkina price — a good detail
**25,000 warriors in exchange for pork and iron.** A barter arrangement between allies: no money, no treaty obligation, no Zong troops. **It gave the campaign its numbers while keeping the crown's name off it.**

## 3. The Sea Wolves were already in canon
`articles/unorganized/Sea Wolves.mediawiki` — Sanese pirates and maritime mercenaries, by the 16th century **"trans-oceanic shock troops"**, known to Illypnia as the **Shendan Demons**. `History of Zong (1606-1711)` already calls them **"the backbone of the colonial garrison system."**

**So the colony's deniable force was pre-established.** They are under **no sovereign**, which is precisely why the crown could not be said to have hired them.

**Combined 202,216 km².** Both low and littoral. **The Salowi hold the longest coastline in the country — which is why the fisheries made the first incursions.** The sequence is geographically determined: fish before land.

**And the Salowi's oral tradition of the Kawesa's destruction made them meet the colony with a hostility sharpened by that memory.** They had watched it happen to their neighbours.

## 5. Register notes for this article
The subject is a **denied war**, so the article has to hold two things at once: what the record shows, and what contemporaries were willing to call it. The **Naming** section carries this — contemporaries did not call it a war; the record is explicit; modern scholarship does call it a war.

**Watch for editorialising.** I wrote several passages that judged rather than described ("the origin of their divergence", "the practice by which the colony would obtain what it could not be given") and **softened them all** to encyclopedic register before pushing.

## 6. Open questions
1. **Did the crown ever respond?** Even a reprimand would be significant — and its absence is itself evidence.
2. **Was the General Assembly's establishment (1549) connected to the war's beginning (1549)?** The same year is suggestive: a landowners' forum created just as land ran out.
3. **What was the Salowi and Chidoha fate after 1558** — enslaved, dispersed, absorbed, or surviving as communities? The Salowi at least appear later.
4. **Were the Sea Wolves paid in land** as well as money?
5. **Did the Hahkina receive their pork and iron in full?** A war fought for provisions is a good detail to follow up.
6. **How did the colony's own settlers regard the campaign** — did they know where the captives came from?
