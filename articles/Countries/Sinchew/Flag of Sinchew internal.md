## Historical Context (from established lore)
- Sinchew colony established 1538 at Tamsui (penal/plantation colony)
- Baiduist Expulsion from Zong (1606–1650): over 1M Zong Baiduists transported to Sinchew
- Sinchew War (1648–1656): Zong mainland fought Baiduist colony
- First War of the Isthmus (1711–1716): Sinchew Colony participated, ~5,000 killed (incl. ~3,000 enslaved)
- 1717: Landowners' rebellion squashed (Zong refused to compensate for slaves lost in war)
- 1718: Emperor Tianli banned slavery in ALL Zong lands; Sinchew forced to emancipate → economic crash; Zong provided no help
- Sinchewese Revolutionary War (1726–1733) → modern Sinchewese state (independence from Zong)
- Flag adopted 1733 upon independence

## Colonial Flag (added 2026-09-03)
- User provided colonial flag SVG: `/Users/shubhamnaik/Downloads/Flag_of_Colonial_Sinchew (1).svg`
- Colonial flag design: jade green field (#418B80 — Zong imperial color) with white Zong imperial symbol (upper-left) and two mantas symbol of Sinchew (right side)
- Adopted c. 1538 upon founding of colony at Tamsui
- Used until independence in 1733
- The two mantas are the colonial emblem of Sinchew — derived from marine life of the Sinchewese coast
- Jade green = Zong imperial color, associated with House of Yuanlong
- The imperial symbol of Zong asserts crown sovereignty over the colony
- The two mantas acknowledge the colony's distinct maritime identity
- After independence, the two mantas remain a cultural symbol of Sinchew (civic/maritime contexts)
- Uploaded to wiki as `Flag of Colonial Sinchew.svg`

## Revolutionary Symbolism & Designer (added 2026-09-03)
- **Designer**: Lîm Huat-tiat (林法哲, c. 1690–1745) — Baiduist monk and revolutionary intellectual, born in Tamsui to family of expelled Baiduist settlers. Member of the revolutionary council during the Sinchewese Revolutionary War.
- **Knot revolutionary significance**: The Baiduist knot was used in revolutionary insignia during the Sinchewese Revolutionary War (1726–1733). Its endless, interlaced form represents the endless struggle for religious freedom — from the 1606 expulsion through the colonial hardships and the revolutionary war. The unbroken pattern = vigilance; the fight is never truly over.
- **Black triangles revolutionary meaning**: The two black triangles represent the twin forces of oppression overthrown by the revolution:
  1. Zong imperial authority (colonial subordination, denied representation)
  2. Tianist religious establishment (expulsion, religious persecution)
  - Diagonally opposed = two pillars pressing from opposite sides
  - White diagonal band between them = revolutionary space, freedom carved between empire and theocracy
- **Knot dual meaning**: Religious (Ocean Crossing, interconnectedness) + revolutionary (endless struggle for religious freedom)
