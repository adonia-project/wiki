# Native peoples of Sinchew — internal notes

Working notes, begun 2026-09-19. **Purpose: establish the indigenous peoples of Sinchew well enough that their languages can supply place names, ethnonyms, and vocabulary for the rest of the Sinchew material.**

No article exists yet for any of these peoples. These notes gather what is already scattered across other articles, mark what is unresolved, and list the questions that need answering before anything can be written.

---

## 1. What is already established

### The two isthmian peoples
Source: `articles/Countries/Burawa/History/First War of the Isthmus.mediawiki`, `articles/Countries/Balboa/People/Fosian Balboan people.mediawiki`, `articles/Countries/Zong/History/History of Zong (1606-1711).mediawiki`

Two peoples are named: the **Karawã** and the **Apyãwa**. Both are described as indigenous to the **isthmian jungle between the Zong colony of Sinchew and the Volisanian colony of Balboa** — the territory later governed by [[Free Sinchew]].

Established facts:
- They maintained "a deep cultural relationship with the forest ecosystem."
- Their resistance to Zong deforestation "was rooted in both territorial defence and a cosmological order that regarded the forest as sacred."
- When Zong began large-scale deforestation to clear a route for a planned land invasion, both peoples "mounted armed resistance against the deforestation parties and the Zong troops escorting them."
- The Baiduist Sinchewese frontier communities, whose tradition "emphasised harmony with the natural order," aligned with them against the colonial administration.
- After the Sinchew War (1648–1656), the Free Sinchewese Army retreated into the jungle, made contact with them, and built **Free Sinchew** as a confederation governed by "an equal council of tribal chiefs and Sinchewese military leaders," with a Council Speaker as first among equals (Tân Huat-seng, 陳法生).
- They fought in the First War of the Isthmus (1711–1716) alongside the Sinchewese holdouts.
- Defeated at the **Battle of Karawãtyba** (February 1715).
- After the defeat: "the Karawã and Apyãwa were dispersed — mostly killed or captured; survivors absorbed into Sinchew, losing their distinct political identity."

### An unnamed third group
Source: `articles/Countries/Zong/History/History of Zong (1606-1711).mediawiki`

The 1606–1650 Baiduist relocation to Sinchew is described as marked by "disease, starvation, and conflict with the existing colonial population and **the indigenous peoples of the northwestern coast**." No name is given. This suggests at least one further people in Sinchew proper, distinct from the two isthmian peoples.

### The isthmian naming pattern already in use
Source: `articles/Countries/Balboa/People/Birú people.mediawiki`

The neighbouring **Birú** of Balboa are described as "the indigenous people of the isthmus." Their language is **Birú Dale** (named from Birú *dale*, "speech" or "tongue"), and it has a documented vocabulary that drives isthmian toponymy:

| *dale* | speech, tongue | language name |
| *yala* | land, territory, mountain | **Jala** Province |

| *iwa* | clear | **Lake Iwarakí** |
| *rakí* | reflection, surface | **Lake Iwarakí** |
| *warakí* | peaceful water | **Varakí** |
| *tule* | person | — |
| *mola* | shirt, clothing | — |
| *sappi* | village | — |

Note the pattern: **volcano names, provinces, lakes and towns in the isthmus region are corruptions of indigenous words**, often via Volisanian. This is the precedent to follow for Sinchew's indigenous-derived names.

### Naming context for the colonial layer
- "Sinchew" is a Zong (Hokkien) name — the colonial layer.
- Sinchewese personal naming is Zong-derived surnames plus Baiduist dharma names (see `Sinchewese name.mediawiki`).
- The colonial flag carried "two mantas" as the colonial emblem of Sinchew, "derived from marine life of the Sinchewese coast" — this may or may not connect to an indigenous tradition.

## 2. Decisions taken 2026-09-19

### Language family
The user's brief was to switch Sinchew's indigenous languages toward **Mexican** rather than Tupí-Guarani inspiration, on the grounds that **Balisca (the Tupí-Guarani region) is geographically far away**.

However, investigation established two complications:
- The suffix **-tyba** in *Karawãtyba* is already recorded in `First War of the Isthmus internal.md` as **"Tupi suffix -tyba = place of the Karawã"**, under a heading marked *confirmed by user 2026-08-20*.
- **Apyãwa is a genuine Tupí-Guaraní word** — the self-designation of the Tapirapé people of Mato Grosso, Brazil, where the morpheme *-ãwa* means "people/person."

**Resolution:** the **Karawã and Apyãwa remain as they are**. They sit in the **north of Sinchew, closest to the Balboan isthmus**, which makes a Tupí-tinged frontier population defensible as an isthmian rather than a Sinchew-proper phenomenon. Their established names, the `-tyba` suffix, and the existing First War of the Isthmus text stand unchanged.

**Sinchew proper will have other peoples.** Given Sinchew's size (2.08 million km²) and its several distinct climate regions, it should have multiple indigenous groups — not just the two isthmian peoples. Those will draw on **Mexican language families** (Mixe-Zoquean, Mayan/Huastec, Totonacan and possibly Huave were identified as candidates; selection deferred).

### Sequencing agreed
1. **Cut Sinchew into geographical regions first.**
2. **Then work on the geography** of those regions.
3. **Then work on the peoples** — placing indigenous groups within regions once the regions and geography exist.
4. Language families and vocabulary come after that.

This supersedes the earlier "language first" framing. The questions in section 3 below are retained for when the peoples work resumes, but are **not** urgent.

## 3. Open questions (deferred — see sequencing above)

### Language
1. **Do the Karawã and Apyãwa speak related languages, or distinct ones?** Is there a shared family or areal name?
2. **Is there a Sinchew-specific language name** comparable to "Birú Dale"? Something like "<people> <word-for-speech>"?
3. **Is Birú Dale related to the Karawã/Apyãwa languages?** The Birú are also an isthmian people — are these one family, neighbouring families, or unrelated?

### Relationship between the peoples
4. **Are the Karawã and Apyãwa related to the Birú, or a separate group?** The Birú article says the Birú are "the indigenous people of the isthmus" (singular), while the First War article names two peoples in the isthmian jungle. Are these the same people under different names, related peoples, or distinct?
5. **Who are "the indigenous peoples of the northwestern coast"** mentioned in the Zong history? Named, or to be invented?
6. **Did the Karawã and Apyãwa survive as communities** after 1715, or were they wholly absorbed? This affects whether they appear in modern Sinchew at all.

### Etymology and naming
7. **What do *Karawã* and *Apyãwa* mean?** Are they self-designations (endonyms) or names given by others (exonyms)?
8. **Is "Karawãtyba" formed from *Karawã* + a locative suffix?** If so, what is the suffix and what does it mean? (A *-tyba* "place of abundance" pattern would suggest a Tupi-Guarani-like grammar.)
9. **What vocabulary should underpin Sinchewese place names?** This is the core deliverable — the words for water, river, mountain, forest, village, stone, sea, island, and so on, from which toponyms can be built consistently.

### Culture
10. **What is the sacred-forest cosmology called**, if it has a name? It is referenced but never named.
11. **What was the confederation's structure** in indigenous terms — did the "equal council of tribal chiefs" have a name?

## 4. Naming implications to resolve

Once the vocabulary exists, these Sinchewese features would want indigenous-derived names:
- Major rivers and lakes (currently unnamed or generically described)
- Named mountain peaks in the eastern highlands (referenced in the climate article only as "the eastern highlands")
- The isthmian jungle / frontier region itself
- Any surviving Karawã or Apyãwa communities
- The site of the Battle of Karawãtyba, if it is distinct from the settlement the name describes

Note that the Zong colonial layer would predominate in the settled coastal regions, with indigenous names surviving in the interior and the isthmian frontier — mirroring the Birú pattern in Balboa, where indigenous toponymy survives best in the remote highlands.

## 5. Language families — REVISED 2026-09-19 (Southeastern US)

### Superseding the Mexican decision
The earlier decision (section 2) to model Sinchew's indigenous languages on **Mexican** families (Mixe-Zoquean, Mayan/Huastec, Totonacan, Huave) is **superseded**. User, 2026-09-19: *"I think mississippi languages actually is more accurate, floridian/southern USA, given the large area maybe we can distribute a bunch around."*

This is a better fit for Sinchew specifically. The Southeastern US has the same structural properties as Sinchew — large area, wet subtropical-to-tropical climate gradient, a major river system, mountains along one side, long gulf coast — and supported **more distinct language families than almost anywhere in North America**.

The **Karawã and Apyãwa are unaffected** and remain Tupí-tinged (section 2). They are now formalised as the **ninth family**, appropriate because they sit in the north-east corner toward the Balboan isthmus rather than in Sinchew proper.

**Twenty languages in total.** Family 7, **Uwisa**, is a **language isolate** — genuinely unrelated to any of its neighbours, mirroring Yuchi in the real Southeast. That gives the family tree a puzzle for in-world linguistics and adds realistic texture.

### The twenty languages

**1. Kawesa family** (Muskogean-inspired; central plateau, interior valleys). Signature: -a and -i endings, hk/ch clusters, generally three or four syllables.
- **Kawesa** — the largest language of Sinchew; its name supplies the family name
- **Chinapa**
- **Talowa**
- **Hokchita**
- **Yakona**

**2. Nahola family** (Natchez/Tunica-inspired; the great river valleys). Signature: short, nasal-plus-stop sequences, hierarchical-speech registers.
- **Nahola**
- **Tahena**
- **Kawocha**

**3. Timara family** (Timucuan-inspired; northern lowlands and north coast). Signature: -ra and -na endings, open syllables.
- **Timara**
- **Sarowa**
- **Potina**

**4. Salowi family** (Calusa-inspired; western Gulf of Louyang coast). A single-language family; maritime and estuarine, non-agricultural.
- **Salowi**

**5. Chidoha family** (Caddoan-inspired; western interior). Signature: -ha and -osa endings.
- **Chidoha**
- **Nakoha**

**6. Yelani family** (Iroquoian/Cherokee-inspired; eastern highlands). A single language holding the largest single territory of any Sinchew people, occupying the mountain belt.
- **Yelani**

**7. Uwisa** (Yuchi-inspired; eastern uplands). **Language isolate.**
- **Uwisa**

**8. Hachina family** (Chitimacha/Atakapa-inspired; southern coast).
- **Hachina**
- **Hatako**

**9. Karawã–Apyãwa family** (Tupí-Guaraní; north-east corner). Already established; names unchanged.
- **Karawã**
- **Apyãwa**

### Distribution logic

The north-to-south climate gradient drives the mapping. Sinchew's north is tropical rainforest, so a Timucuan-analogue reads there much as southern Florida does; the south is humid subtropical, matching the Gulf coast families. The eastern highlands are Sinchew's Appalachians, which is where the Cherokee-analogue belongs.

Two further points carry over from the Birú precedent in Balboa:

- **Colonial toponymy will dominate the coasts and plains.** Zong settlement concentrated on the western lowlands and the northern coast, so indigenous-derived names should survive best in the **interior plateau, the eastern highlands and uplands, and the isthmian frontier**.
- **The eastern highlands are the strongest candidate for retained indigenous names**, because the Yelani hold the mountain belt and colonial settlement had least reason to penetrate it.

### Open questions
1. **Family names vs language names.** Several families take their name from their largest language (Kawesa, Nahola, Timara, Chidoha, Hachina). Is that acceptable, or should families be named separately?
2. **Which language supplies toponyms in each region?** Needed before the 416 rivers and lakes can be named.
3. **Names for families 4 and 7** as single-language families — are they properly families at all, or isolates/unnamed units?
4. **Are the Karawã and Apyãwa related to each other**, or two unrelated isthmian languages grouped geographically? Currently treated as one family.
5. **Which language survives into the modern era** as the most-spoken indigenous language of Sinchew? Kawesa is the default assumption given its extent.
6. **Vocabulary** — the core deliverable. Words for water, river, lake, mountain, forest, village, stone, sea, island, person, and speech (for language names in the Birú *dale* pattern).
