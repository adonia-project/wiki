# Chinowa (Chiu Chi-hôa) — internal notes

Begun 2026-09-21. Daughter of Hokta; wife of Chiu Khéng-gio̍k.

Article: `Chinowa.mediawiki`. Related: `Chiu Khéng-gio̍k internal.md`, `Hokta internal.md`, `Tamsui Company internal.md`.

---

## 1. Who she is

**Chinowa, about 1507 – 1589**, died at [[Tamsui]] aged about 82. **Zong name: Chiu Chi-hôa (周芝華)**, chosen by the user from three options. Daughter of **Hokta**, miko of the Akwesa. Sister of **[[Oktisa]]**. Wife of **[[Chiu Khéng-gio̍k]]** from 1521 (she was ~14, he 16). Two children, unnamed.

**She and Oktisa are the only named Kawesa women in the entire record** — and the only female Kawesa names that survive at all.

---

## 2. The paired names — a good structural detail
The two names mirror each other **in opposite directions**:

| person | their own name | the other people's rendering |
|---|---|---|
| Chiu Khéng-gio̍k | 周慶玉 (*Khèng-gio̍k*) | **Kengohka** — Akwesa, from the first element of 慶 |
| Chinowa | *Chinowa* | **芝華** (*Chi-hôa*) — Zong, 芝 from *Chi-*, 華 from *-wa* |

**Each name renders the other's bearer in the other's tongue**, and both were formed the same way — taking the first element of the original. **A Zong man and an Akwesa woman who each acquired a name in the other's language, by the same rule.** That is worth keeping; it makes the marriage legible as a genuine two-way absorption rather than one culture swallowing the other.

**She took the Chiu surname**, so she appears in the settlement's papers as Chiu Chi-hôa. **The Akwesa went on calling her Chinowa for the whole sixty-eight years.**

**Decision:** the article states the name was **given on her marriage**, and says the colonial papers use the Zong name while Akwesa usage (so far as reconstructable) used Chinowa throughout. **The user may want to confirm the timing.**

## 3. The shape of her life
| year | age | event |
|---|---|---|
| ~1507 | — | born in her father's chiefdom |
| 1521 | ~14 | **married**, and given the name Chi-hôa |
| 1527 | ~20 | **her father dies of plague**; her husband becomes miko and king |
| 1535 | ~28 | **her inheritance is sold to the colony** |
| 1543 | ~36 | her husband removed; the territory divided |
| 1564 | ~57 | the Great Disappointment |
| 1572–85 | 65–78 | the Great Peasant Revolt in Zong |
| **1589** | **~82** | **dies at Tamsui** — the year the Yuanlong Restoration completes |

**She lived at the bay for eighty-two of her eighty-two years**, from a chiefdom to a colony. Her husband outlived her by nine years.

**The marriage lasted longer than the chiefdom did, by a very long way** — married 68 years; the chiefdom ended six years in.

---

## 4. Register discipline
The article **does not attribute feeling to her**. It records what was done with her country and notes that she appears in the papers "in the course of recording what was done with the country she came from." It says **no document written by her or at her direction is known.**

**The strongest line, and it should stay:** *"The record of her life is therefore the record of the transactions conducted over her father's land."*

## 5. Open questions
1. **When exactly was she given the name Chi-hôa** — at the marriage (1521), or on formal entry into the family?
2. **Did she speak Zong?** Presumably yes by adulthood, having married in at fourteen — but did she speak Akwesa to her children, and did they grow up bilingual?
3. **Were the two children raised Zong or Akwesa?** This is the same question as for CKG and matters more for her, since she was the Akwesa parent.
4. **What happened to Oktisa?** Still a name in an infobox and nothing else. **Her marriage is unrecorded, which in a patriarchal chiefdom with no male heir is a strange gap.**
5. **Did she ever return to the Akwesa settlements**, or move between the Akwesa community and the Zong town at Tamsui? The colony's minority Akwesa community persisted after 1543 — **did she remain part of it?**
6. **Her mother is unrecorded.** Hokta had two daughters and no recorded wife.
