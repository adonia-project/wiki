### Tiengo and the Hachinawa — the six-province majority

Tiengo is the Baiduist heartland on the long lake, and its interests have not been
those of the smaller western provinces. Separating it from the rest of the west
produces the real political division:

| grouping | provinces | population (1736) | of country | Senate seats | of Senate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiengo and the former Hachinawa | 6 | 1,940,589 | 56.2% | 12 | 37.5% |
| The rest of the west and centre | 10 | 1,512,230 | 43.8% | 20 | 62.5% |

A grouping holding 56.2 per cent of the population holds 37.5 per cent of the Senate,
and a grouping holding 43.8 per cent holds 62.5 per cent. The six provinces are the
constituency of the Union of Free Peoples — the Hahkina and the Baiduists who made the
revolution — and the ten are the constituency of the Sinchew Liberal Assembly and the
class that drew the boundaries.

In the General Assembly, where seats are allocated at large, the same majority would
take about 56 per cent of the seats. The two chambers therefore answer to different
populations, and the Senate is the instrument by which the smaller one governs.

### The three groupings and the two-of-three requirement

The provinces fall into three political groupings rather than two: the Tianist-aligned
provinces of the west and centre, the Baiduist-aligned province of Tiengo together with
the Baiduist towns of the other provinces, and the native-aligned provinces of the
former Hachinawa, whose populations are Hahkina, Mahik and Apyãwa.

No grouping holds both chambers. The Baiduist and native groupings together hold about
56 per cent of the population and would carry the General Assembly; the Tianist-aligned
provinces hold twenty of the thirty-two Senate seats and control the Senate. Legislation
therefore requires the agreement of at least two of the three groupings.

**The requirement is not a provision of the constitution.** It is produced by the two
chambers' different electorates, and it has governed the state's legislation since 1736.
That is what the gerrymander was for: the boundaries alone gave the Tianists a blocking
minority, and the bicameral structure converts it into a share of power, because the
majority cannot legislate without them either.

**Still to decide:**

1. Are the groupings named in any instrument, or purely political description?
2. What happens when a grouping splits internally? The native grouping contains Hahkina,
   Mahik, Sitonglan and Apyãwa — not obviously one bloc.
3. Do the Sitonglan count into any grouping? They are 115,318, named in the state's title
   nowhere, and in none of the three groupings. They are the group that has to choose a
   side every time, which is a position with power and no security.

## The province names, and where they are misleading (user, 2026-09-23)

The user raised two problems with the names I coined:

**Akwesa is not where the Akwesa are.** *"the akwesa were near tamsui, but its ok maybe it
was named after the people from tamsui"* — so province H took a name belonging to a people
who lived somewhere else. The user's own reading is that it was **named after migrants**:
people from the Akwesa country who moved to the province and gave it their name. That is
a normal way for a place to be named and does not need changing.

**Oktalwa is not where the bay of Oktalwa is.** *"kotalwa isnt where the bay is, but it can
still be named that just because there is a bay there people also called the oktalwa"* —
province G has a bay, and took the name of a bay, but not necessarily the same bay. Again
the user's reading is that this is fine: the name is generic in the region, and more than
one bay was called Oktalwa.

**So both names stand, and both are records of how names travel** — one by people moving
and one by a name generalising. The article's Notes column describes G as "the bay of
Oktalwa" and H as "the Akwesa territory"; neither is exactly right, and neither is wrong
enough to change.

**If either is revised, the Zong and Hachina renderings in the constitution table must change
with it** — 沃塔瓦 / 烏克搭拉瓦 for Oktalwa, 阿圭沙 / 阿克衛沙 for Akwesa.
