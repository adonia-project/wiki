## 1. The mirror

**Hachina belongs to the Hachina family, inspired by Chitimacha and Atakapa** — the Gulf coast societies of Louisiana and Texas. (Note: **Muskogean** is the mirror for the *Kawesa* family, not this one.)

### Phonology to work within

**Consonants:** p, t, k, b, d, g, ch, ts, f, s, sh, h, m, n, w, y, l, and glottal stop (')
**Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u — short and long; nasalised vowels occur in Atakapa
**Syllables:** CV and CVC predominate; clusters are rare and usually stop-plus-glide
**Word shape:** open syllables, words commonly end in a vowel. Two or three syllables is typical; four is possible.

### RESOLVED — the mirror supplies phonology only (user, 2026-09-21)
User: *"well we just need the language as a base, the culture we can modify."*

**The Chitimacha/Atakapa mirror is a sound system, not a culture.** It governs phonology, syllable structure and word shape — and nothing else.

**Consequences:**
- **No swap is needed.** Kawesa stays Muskogean, Hachina stays Chitimacha/Atakapa.
- **Hachina's culture is invented freely** and is not constrained by what Chitimacha or Atakapa societies were actually like. If the state needs mound-building, chiefly grades, town squares, a priest-king or a temple hierarchy, it has them — drawn from wherever fits.
- **The mismatch noted above is therefore a non-issue.** The point stands as a curiosity (the real Chitimacha were small Gulf-coast societies; the real Muskogean were the mound-builders), but it imposes no constraint.

One thing worth keeping: **the lexicon should still be culturally honest to the state we're building.** A grain empire with irrigation, gold, firearms and a regnal council needs words for those things, whether or not its mirror language had them. Coin them.

## 3. Names built from it

**All of the following are CONFIRMED (user, 2026-09-21)** except Hatako.

| name | construction | meaning | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hachina** | *hachin* + agentive | the central people | confirmed |
| **Hachinawa** | *hachin* + *awa* | the middle realm — **the empire** | **confirmed** |
| **Welanawa** | *wela* + *nawa* ~ *awa* | the western realm — the Zong (formal) | confirmed |
| **Watasa** | *wata* + *sa* | sea-people — the Zong (colloquial) | confirmed |
| **Hachintala** | *hachin* + *tala* | the seat of the centre — **the capital** | **confirmed** |
| **Hatako** | — | the second Hachina-family language | etymology pending |

### Words exported to Sinchewese — pending
The Hachina gave their northern neighbours a name, which the Zong adopted. **That word is not yet determined**, and it is not "Kawesa" — see `Kawesa internal.md`, where "Kawesa" is established as a *modern coinage* derived from the clan name **Akwesa**.

When the Hachina term is set, it belongs here, and it will be the first documented case of a Hachina word entering the wider vocabulary of Sinchew — by way of the Zong.

### Place and feature names
| name | construction | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Tsuwasha** | *tsu* "south" + *washa* "water" | the southern river — southern limit of Hahkina territory |
| **Hachinawa** | *hachin* "centre" + *awa* "realm" | the middle realm — the empire |
| **Hachintala** | *hachin* + *tala* "city" | the capital |

With *tsu* "south" now attested in use, the remaining directionals (*wela* "west", *hasha* "east", *na* "north") stay unconfirmed.

### Attested personal names (1538)
| name | construction | bearer |
|---|---|---|
| **Tsulhachi** | *tsula* "lord" + *hachin* "centre" | King of the Hahkina, r. before 1538–after 1538 |
| **Nachiha** | *nachi* "heart" | His son; led the embassy to Anping 1538; later king |

Note that **Tsulhachi** is a *regnal* name — built from the same root as the ethnonym and the empire-name, which is what a king of "the central realm" would want to be called. **Nachiha** is a personal name, not a regnal one. The distinction is worth keeping: Hahkina kings take throne-names, and their birth names are not recorded by the Zong.

### The Zong names — external, not Hachina
These are **not** Hachina words. They are what the Zong called them, in Hokkien.

| period | name | characters | reading | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early (from 1458) | **Hâ-pang** | 遐邦 | Hâ-pang | "the distant state" — descriptive, diplomatic |
| later, once envoys were exchanged | **Hahkina** | 哈其那 | Hah-kî-ná | the Hachina's own name, rendered phonetically |

The shift from descriptive to phonetic marks the moment the Zong began treating Hachina as a specific polity rather than a place at the edge of the map. Note that the phonetic rendering dropped the *-wa* of **Hachinawa**, taking the ethnonym **Hachina** instead — so the Zong recorded *the people*, not the empire.

**Note the structure of the naming.** The ethnonym, the empire name and the self-conception all rest on one root (*hachin* "centre"), which is why the Hachina read the Zong as a counterpart rather than a marvel: someone who knows themselves as *the centre* has a place already prepared in their thinking for *the west*. The Zong named themselves out of other people's geography without noticing.

The Zong have **two Hachina names**, which is worth preserving: **Welanawa** is court vocabulary, the phrase a regnal council would use in treaty; **Watasa** is what ordinary people said — the sea-people, named for how they arrived. States use diplomacy; commoners use description.

## 4. Open questions

1. **Swap the mirrors, keep phonology, or leave as is?** Decides how much Muskogean material enters Hachina culture.
2. **Is *hachin* the true root of the ethnonym**, or did the Hachina call themselves something else and "Hachina" is an exonym? If it is the endonym, the "central" self-description is encoded in their own name — which is strong.
3. **Does *awa* and *wela* apply to the other Sinchewese families too**, or are they Hachina-specific? Consistency across families matters if we build lexicons for all nine.
4. **The language's own name** — does "Hachina" name the language, or is there a separate word (in the Birú *Dale* pattern)?
5. **Build **Hatako** as well**, or treat it as a minor sister language with a short lexicon?
6. **Numerals and directions** — worth establishing early, since they drive toponymy (e.g. "north river", "seven hills").
7. **Is there a script**, and if so does it relate to the lexicography — syllabary, logographic, or none?
8. **Loanwords from the Zong.** Forty-plus years of contact before 1538 would put some Zong words into Hachina — trade terms and weapon names especially. Worth a small borrowing list.
