## 1. The land they live in — measured

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| Area | **295,841 km²** |
| Extent | lat 8.05°S–2.34°S (≈635 km), lon 105.87°W–99.42°W (≈715 km) |
| Elevation | **median 567 m** — 70% in the 430–800 m band, 23% in 260–430 m |
| Rainfall | median **3,187 mm**, p10 1,918, p90 3,723 |
| Temperature | median **21 °C** |
| Köppen | **Am 71%, Af 15%** — tropical monsoon, with rainforest in the wetter parts |

**Nahola occupies the great river valleys of the centre-north**, and its terrain reflects that: high enough to be upland (567 m) but not mountainous, and more varied than Uwisa's plateau. The rainfall is high and the valleys are the reason.

## 2. Position within the empire

Nahola borders Hachina along **1,509 boundary cells** — the second longest frontier of any Hachina neighbour, after Uwisa's 1,906.

**But Nahola is not named as a gold source.** The tribute in gold is recorded from **Uwisa and Yelani**. Nahola's contribution to the empire, if it was tributary at all in the earlier period, is therefore unspecified — grain is implausible (the plain out-produces them), so the likely answers are labour, troops, timber or valley produce.

**1565: the Nahola states join the Hachina empire officially.** Note the plural — **"the Nahola states"** — which is consistent with the language framework giving the Nahola family **three languages**. So Nahola is not one polity but a group of them, and they entered the empire as such.

## 3. Language — Natchez / Tunica inspired

**Family 2 in the framework. Three languages.** Signature: **short, nasal-plus-stop sequences, and hierarchical-speech registers.**

**The hierarchical speech registers are the most promising element.** Natchez and Tunica in the real Southeast were associated with ranked societies and with speech forms that vary by the status of the person addressed. **A people with three languages, ranked registers and a tributary relationship to a centralising empire has an obvious cultural engine**: how you speak to whom, and what that says about where you stand.

## 4. Open questions

1. ~~Three languages, three polities?~~ **RESOLVED - one state per language, three in all.** Proposed names in `Nahola language internal.md`: **Nahola** (the river bend), **Waiyampa** (the mound place), **Tumpinsa** (the council house). **Names unconfirmed.** The plural "Nahola states" in the 1565 record is three polities entering together.
2. **Hierarchical registers — how far do they go?** Is there a court register, a register for the empire's officials, a common speech?
3. **Nahola's tribute.** If not gold, then what — labour, troops, timber, valley crops?
4. **Their relation to Uwisa.** Two upland neighbours of the same empire, one a plateau and one a valley country, one a language isolate and one a family of three. Are they rivals, allied, or indifferent?
5. **Their relation to Yelani.** The Yelani are distant and in the mountains. Is there contact at all?
6. **Their warfare.** The uplands are described as intermittently restive. Is Nahola the source of resistance, or the loyal tributary?
7. **Religion.** Do they share the Hachina semi-monotheism, and does the Centre mean anything to a valley people with their own rivers?
