## 1. The land they live in — measured

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| Area | **128,687 km²** — smallest of the three tributaries |
| Extent | lat 10.99°S–5.06°S (≈661 km), lon 106.84°W–94.54°W (**≈1,356 km**) |
| Elevation | **median 1,515 m** — **100% above 800 m**; p95 2,300 m, **max 2,697 m — the highest ground in Sinchew** |
| Rainfall | median **3,978 mm**, p10 2,929, p90 4,682 |
| Temperature | median **14 °C**, p10 10, p90 18 |
| Highest point | (−96.31, −11.02) |

**Yelani is the only people of Sinchew who occupy no lowland at all.** Every square kilometre is above 800 m. The temperature median of 14 °C is the coldest of the nine families by a wide margin — Hachina's is 21, Uwisa's 20, the coastal peoples' 24 to 26.

**And the territory is extraordinarily elongated: 1,356 km east to west against 661 km north to south.** The Yelani hold a mountain belt, not a mountain region, and the belt runs the length of the country's eastern side.

## 2. Position within the empire

Yelani borders Hachina along **929 boundary cells** — the shortest of the three, because the mountains themselves are the barrier.

**Yelani is one of the two named sources of tributary gold**, together with Uwisa. The Hachina–Yelani frontier has a median elevation of **1,055 m**, rising to a maximum of 1,492 m, and the gold in those highlands — alluvial or vein — is the classic setting for mountain gold.

**Yelani alone among the three is not described as joining the empire.** Nahola joined in 1565 and Uwisa in 1568. **Whether the Yelani ever formally entered, or remained a tributary without incorporation, is open** — and the mountains are a plausible reason: an empire can collect tribute from a mountain people more cheaply than it can govern them.

## 3. Language — Iroquoian (Cherokee) inspired

**Family 6 in the framework. A single language, holding the largest single territory of any Sinchew people** — a mountain belt 1,356 km long.

**One language across 1,356 km of mountain is a strong claim**, and Cherokee in the real Southeast is a fair model for it: a single speech community spread across a mountain range, politically fragmented into towns that share a language and a religion but not a government.

**The framework also notes that the eastern highlands are the strongest candidate for retained indigenous names**, because colonial settlement had least reason to penetrate the mountains. So **Yelani toponymy probably survives better than that of any other people of Sinchew** — which matters for the modern country, not just the pre-colonial period.

## 4. Open questions

1. **One language, how many polities?** A mountain belt 1,356 km long is unlikely to have been one state. Towns, valleys, a confederacy, or nothing?
2. ~~**What grows at 1,515 m and 14 °C with 3,978 mm of rain?**~~ **RESOLVED — tuber farmers, and no livestock.**
   The Yelani grow **highland tubers**. They keep **no domestic animals at all** — not the turkey, not any herd animal, nothing. **User, 2026-09-22: *"I think 2 probably tuber farmers, which is why also the hachina had a protein advantage from turkey"***

   **This is the most useful fact about the Yelani, and it reframes the whole empire.**

   **The Hachina had turkeys. Nobody in the mountains did.** Grain feeds more people per hectare than tubers, but neither grain nor tubers supply much protein, and **the only domesticated animal in Sinchew before the Zong was the turkey** — which the Hachina kept and their tributaries did not.

   **So the asymmetry between the plain and the mountains is not only one of calories.** Hachina had calories *and* animal protein; the uplands had calories. A diet of tubers without meat is a diet that is adequate in bulk and short in the things that build muscle, heal wounds and feed children.

   **Three consequences worth keeping:**
   1. **A military dimension.** Men raised on meat against men raised on tubers is a real advantage, and it stacks with the grain surplus, the metals and the numbers.
   2. **A trade dimension.** Turkeys become an exportable good — not merely a food, but something the empire can give that its clients cannot produce, and therefore a lever that does not depend on grain.
   3. **A settlement dimension.** A people without herds are a people who can move less, and who cannot simply drive their wealth away from a raid.
3. **Is Yelani where the Hachina's horses might have been useful** — or useless, because horses are poor on steep ground? The notes on the contact period say horses gave the empire reach into the valleys and passes but not the mountains.
4. **Did the Yelani ever join the empire formally?** Or pay tribute without incorporation, as a mountain people might?
5. **Gold: mined by Yelani, or collected by Hachina expeditions?** The frontier is mountainous and the empire's control of it is presumed rather than described.
6. **Population** — 128,687 km² of high mountain is unlikely to hold many people, and the Yelani may be the smallest of the three.
7. **When did the Yelani become tributary**, and was it gold that made them so?
