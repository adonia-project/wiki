## 1. The mirror

**Uwisa is inspired by Yuchi** — a real language of the American Southeast, and **a language isolate**: unrelated to any of its neighbours.

**Family 7 in the framework. A single language, and an isolate.** Every other people of Sinchew belongs to a family with relatives; Uwisa has none.

### Phonology to work within

**Consonants:** p, t, k, ch, s, sh, h, m, n, w, y, l, glottal stop (')
**Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u — short and long, **with nasalised forms** (*ą*, *ę*, *į*, *ǫ*, *ų*)
**Ejectives** (*k'*, *t'*, *ch'*) are characteristic of Yuchi and are available if wanted
**Syllable shape:** CV and CVC; clusters rare
**Word shape:** open syllables common, words often ending in a vowel

**Nasalised vowels are the family fingerprint**, where the Nahola family's is the nasal-plus-stop cluster.

## 3. The two rivers

**Both run east to west**, and they lie in opposite halves of the country with a watershed between them.

| river | course inside Uwisa | position | proposed name | construction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| northern | **344 km**, wholly within the territory | lat ≈ 5.7°S–7.3°S | **Hatso** | *hą* "water" + *tso* "great" — the great water |
| southern | **292 km** inside (596 km total, continuing beyond the frontier) | lat ≈ 9.6°S–10.6°S | **Kalihą** | *kala* + *hą* |

**The southern river is the longer and leaves the territory entirely**, which means its headwaters and its mouth both lie outside Uwisa. The northern river is shorter and wholly Uwisa's.

## 4. The two states

**Uwisa comprised two states, one on each river, and both were clients of the [[Hachinawa|Hachina]] empire.**

| state | river | position | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hatso** | Hatso | north | wholly within the country; the shorter river |
| **Kalihą** | Kalihą | south | on the longer river, whose course runs beyond Uwisa |

**Names proposed; both unconfirmed.**

**This changes the shape of the Uwisa problem, and for the better.** "The most powerful tributary, subdued over many generations" is easier to explain for **two states than for one**:

- **Two states can be beaten separately**, which is what makes a long contest possible rather than decisive.
- **While the empire campaigned against one, the other recovered.** A single kingdom, once broken, stays broken; two rivals give each other time.
- **And they could also fight each other**, which is where some of the "many wars" went.
- **They could also ally against the empire**, which is the danger the empire's diplomacy existed to prevent.

**So the relationship is not one subjection but a sequence of them**: the empire defeats one state, imposes tribute, turns to the other, and finds the first has stopped paying.

## 5. The gold

**The gold is in the highlands, and the highlands are the reason the two states are not the whole story.** The wealth the empire taxed was not produced on the two rivers; it came from the hill country behind and between them.

That raises a question the notes must leave open: **did the two states control the gold, or merely carry it?** If the mines were worked by upland peoples beyond the river states, the empire's real tributaries were three or more, and Hatso and Kalihą were the middlemen.
