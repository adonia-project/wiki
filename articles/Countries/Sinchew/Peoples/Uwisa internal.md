## 1. The land they live in — measured

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| Area | **326,760 km²** — the largest of the three tributaries, and larger than Hachina's other neighbours |
| Extent | lat 10.98°S–3.45°S (≈839 km), lon 101.90°W–94.21°W (≈849 km) |
| Elevation | **median 570 m** — **92% in the 430–800 m band** |
| Rainfall | median **2,297 mm**, p10 1,775, p90 4,177 |
| Temperature | median **20 °C** |
| Köppen | **Am 50%, Cfa 26%** — monsoon with a substantial humid-subtropical share |

**Uwisa is the most uniform territory of any Sinchew people after Hachina itself: 92% of it lies in a single 370 m elevation band.** That is a plateau, not a mountain range. It is also the driest of the three tributaries — 2,297 mm against Nahola's 3,187 and Yelani's 3,978 — and the presence of Cfa means parts of it have a cooler, more seasonal regime than the monsoon norm.

**This combination matters.** A high, uniformly flat, comparatively dry plateau is the best upland setting in Sinchew for cultivation and for grazing, and it is the only one of the three with the terrain to support a substantial population on its own agriculture.

## 1b. Two states, two rivers

**Uwisa was not one polity. It comprised two states, one on each of its two major rivers, and both were clients of [[Hachinawa|Hachina]].**

| state | river | river's course inside Uwisa | position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hatso** | Hatso | **344 km**, wholly within the territory | north, lat ≈ 5.7°S–7.3°S |
| **Kalihą** | Kalihą | **292 km** inside, 596 km total | south, lat ≈ 9.6°S–10.6°S |

**The two rivers run east to west and lie in opposite halves of the country with a watershed between them.** The northern river is shorter and entirely Uwisa's; the southern is longer and leaves the territory, so that both its headwaters and its mouth lie beyond the Uwisa frontier.

**Names proposed; unconfirmed.** The lexicon and the constructions are in `Uwisa language internal.md`.

### Rivals

**The two states were rivals, not allies. (User, 2026-09-22.)**

**This is the key to the whole relationship, and it changes the empire's role from conqueror to arbiter.**

**Divide and rule is the ordinary instrument of an empire against two neighbours who dislike each other more than they dislike it.** Hachinawa did not need to defeat Uwisa outright if it could be the power whose favour decided which state prevailed. The instruments are familiar: support to one state against the other, recognition, trade on favourable terms, and the withholding of any of these.

**And it makes the tribute intelligible in a way that conquest alone does not.** Part of what Hatso and Kalihą paid was not the price of defeat but **the price of imperial favour** - paid so that the empire would back them rather than their rival. A state that stops paying does not merely risk a campaign; **it risks finding the empire subsidising its neighbour.**

**That explains why the assessments could rise without producing revolt.** Refusing an increase was more dangerous than paying it, **because the rival would not refuse.**

**It also explains the length of the contest.** The empire's difficulty was never that Uwisa was too strong to beat. It was that **two rivals could each be raised as a counterweight to the other**, so that whichever state the empire weakened was the one whose rival then grew. **A single kingdom once broken stays broken. Two rivals give each other time** - and give the empire a reason to keep both alive.

## 2. Position within the empire

Uwisa borders Hachina along **1,906 boundary cells** — the longest frontier of any of the empire's neighbours. It is also, with Yelani, one of the **two named sources of tributary gold**.

**The gold is the defining fact of the relationship.** Uwisa is where the empire's revenue came from, and the pressure on it increased through the contact period: the Hachinawa records show **the gold demanded of the Uwisa rising while quotas elsewhere changed little.**

**1568: Uwisa joins the Hachina empire** — three years after Nahola, in the same period of unification of the native states under a single polity.

## 3. Language — Yuchi inspired, a language isolate

**Family 7 in the framework. A single language, and a LANGUAGE ISOLATE** — genuinely unrelated to any of its neighbours, mirroring Yuchi in the real Southeast.

**This is the most interesting single fact about Uwisa.** Every other people of Sinchew belongs to a family with relatives; Uwisa has none. It means the Uwisa are the region's anomaly — arrived from somewhere unrecorded, or the remnant of a family everyone else's ancestors displaced — and it gives in-world linguistics a genuine puzzle.

**And it has a political consequence worth considering.** A people with no relatives are a people with no natural allies. If the empire pressed hardest on the Uwisa, the Uwisa had nowhere to run and no kin to call on.

## 4. Open questions

1. ~~If Uwisa is a plateau with its own grain, why does it depend on Hachina?~~ **RESOLVED - it did not depend. It was defeated.**
   **User, 2026-09-22: *"the uwisa were the most powerful of the tributaries and they historically had many wars but the hachina had basically subduded them over many generations"***

   **The economic-dependence model applies to Nahola, not to Uwisa.** Uwisa had the plateau, the population and its own agriculture; **it did not come to Hachina for food.** It was **a rival state beaten over centuries, and the gold is a war indemnity that became permanent.**

   **And two states rather than one makes the long contest intelligible:**
   - **Two states can be beaten separately**, so the campaign is never decisive.
   - **While the empire fought one, the other recovered** - a single kingdom once broken stays broken; two give each other time.
   - **They could fight each other**, which is part of the "many wars".
   - **They could ally against the empire**, which is the danger imperial diplomacy existed to prevent.

   **So the relationship is not one subjection but a sequence of them:** the empire defeats one state, imposes tribute, turns to the other, and finds the first has stopped paying.

   **And firearms and horses ended the cycle** - for the first time the empire could enforce permanently what it had previously enforced temporarily. **That is why the gold assessments rise in the contact period.**
2. **Where did an isolate language come from?** A submerged family, a migration, a remnant. This is a good in-world scholarly controversy.
3. **Who did the gold actually belong to** — the two states, clans within them, or upland peoples beyond them? **The tribute registers name the Uwisa "house by house"**, which suggests the empire collected from many Uwisa houses rather than from one ruler. **And the gold is in the highlands, not on the two rivers** — so Hatso and Kalihą may have been middlemen rather than producers.
4. **Was there resistance?** The pressure rose across the period. Did it produce revolt, flight, or submission?
5. **Their relation to Nahola.** Two upland neighbours, one an isolate and one a family of three, both subject to the same empire.
6. **Population.** With 326,760 km² of plateau, Uwisa is a candidate for the largest population of the three tributaries — possibly comparable to the Hachina's dependants in aggregate.
7. **Religion**, and whether the Centre meant anything to a plateau people with no great river of their own.
8. **How did the rivalry end, and what does "joined the empire in 1568" mean for two rivals?** Both subsumed, one absorbing the other with imperial consent, or the distinction abolished by the empire?
9. **Did Hachinawa ever back one state openly against the other**, and is that recorded as an imperial action or only in Uwisa's own history?
