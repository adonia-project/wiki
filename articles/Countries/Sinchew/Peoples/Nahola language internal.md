## 1. The mirror

**Nahola belongs to the Nahola family, inspired by Natchez and Tunica** — the lower Mississippi valley societies of Louisiana and Mississippi.

**Family 2 in the framework, with three languages.** Signature as recorded there: **short, nasal-plus-stop sequences, and hierarchical-speech registers.**

### Phonology to work within

**Consonants:** p, t, k, b, d, g, m, n, w, y, h, s, sh, ch, ts, l, glottal stop (')
**Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u — short and long, **with nasalised forms** (written *aⁿ*, *eⁿ* and so on)
**Syllable shape:** CV and CVC; **nasal-plus-stop clusters are the signature** — *mp*, *nt*, *nk*, *ns*, *nts*
**Word shape:** two or three syllables typical; four possible

**The nasal-plus-stop cluster is the family's fingerprint.** A Nahola word should contain one wherever it can.

## 3. The three states

**Three languages, three polities, one per valley system.** Named below as proposals.

| state | construction | meaning | character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nahola** | *naha* + *hola* | the river bend | the principal state, in the largest valley, and the one the family is named for |
| **Waiyampa** | *waiya* + *mpa* | the mound place | the second state, named for its earthworks |
| **Tumpinsa** | *tumpa* + *insa* | the council house | the third state, named for its assembly and its great hall |

**The three entered the [[Hachinawa|Hachina]] empire together in 1565** — the plural "Nahola states" in the record is three polities, not one.

**Whether they were allies or rivals before 1565 is open.** Three neighbours sharing a family of languages but not a government is the ordinary condition of the region, and a common suzerain would have given them a common grievance.

## 4. Hierarchical speech registers

**The framework's most promising element, and it needs a system.** Proposal:

| register | used for | notes |
|---|---|---|
| **court speech** | the king, his house, and the great lineages | the highest form; archaic vocabulary |
| **valley speech** | ordinary adults of the same town | the everyday form |
| **tributary speech** | addressing an imperial official or a Hachina | the register in which the empire is spoken to |
| **name speech** | addressing a person by rank rather than by name | |

**The political reading:** in a system of three valley states under a distant empire, **how you speak to someone tells everyone present where you stand** — and the registers make status audible in the first sentence of any conversation.
