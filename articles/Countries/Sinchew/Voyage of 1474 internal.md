## 2. Proposed chronology — NEEDS USER CONFIRMATION

### Distance and speed
The west coast from Anping (16.9°S) north to the peninsula's tip (~9.6°S) is approximately **1,130 km** by the coastline, which bends progressively west along the way. At a plausible 15th-century average of 4–5 knots open sailing that is only ~5–7 days of pure passage — but a *survey* voyage stops to chart, sound, take on water, and make contact, so the elapsed time is far longer than the sailing time.

### The first voyage
| | proposal |
|---|---|
| **Departure** | **3 April 1474** |
| **Return** | **19 October 1474** (≈6.5 months) |
| Route | north from Anping, charting; Gulf of Louyang; Kawesa waters (attacked); Chidoha coast (peaceful); turn back at the westward trend; refuel at Hahkina; south to Anping |

**Gap between the voyages: ~17 months** — long enough to build, provision, and crew a larger expedition, and to act on the first voyage's report.

**All three dates are proposals.** The user asked specifically for when they left, when they returned, and when the next voyage started, so these are the numbers to settle.

## 3. The two voyages — what each established

### First voyage (1474)
- **Purpose:** find a sea route to [[Illypnia]] by rounding the north of Lurandia — a passage joining the Shendan and Aethiopean oceans
- Charted the coast north of Anping
- **First Zong contact with the Hahkina** — peaceful; the Hahkina came out, were amazed at the ships, gave offerings
- **Attacked by Kawesa clans** in their waters; fought through
- **Chidoha coast more peaceful**
- Running low on supplies, **and had realised the coast was carrying them west rather than east**
- Concluded there must be a **large peninsula** to round
- Returned via the **Hahkina coast to refuel**, then south to Anping

### Second voyage (1476)
- Larger, better resourced
- **Discovered Tamsui bay**
- **Made contact with the Akwesa** — the clan that later sold the site
- Turned back

**Note the sequence this creates:** the Zong met the Akwesa in **1476**, sixty-two years before the Treaty of Anping (1538). The relationship that produced the sale of Tamsui is therefore a **very long one** — two generations of trade and contact before the site was bought. That is worth building on.

## 4. The Sinchew Peninsula

The user: *"I think the peninsula was called the sinchew peninsula."*

So the landmass the first voyage concluded must be rounded is the **Sinchew Peninsula** — named by the sailors, in the Zong descriptive scheme.

**Geographic basis, measured:** at about 10.8°S the coastline's character changes sharply — west edge −150.9°, east edge −142.4° instead of −95.2° — that is, the landmass narrows dramatically. Sailing north from Anping, the west coast bends steadily west (16.9°S: −148.96 → 13.2°S: −149.73 → 12.0°S: −150.44 → 10.8°S: −150.90 → 9.6°S: −154.46), so a navigator would observe exactly the westward drift the user describes, and would correctly infer a peninsula to round.

**Open:** the exact limits of the Sinchew Peninsula, and whether the name is a Zong coinage or later.
