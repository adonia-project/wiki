# Galwa — Internal Notes

## Overview
Galwa is the western Burawi kingdom, the western successor state created by the 1709 Burawa Imperial Split. The kingdom is centred on the western coast at [[Hargiesa]] and developed separately from the eastern [[Kingdom of Burawa]], with a political culture shaped by the [[Burawi Catholic Church]], the [[Council of Regents (Galwa)]], and later the [[Parliament of Lords (Galwa)]].

## Key facts
- **Capital:** [[Hargiesa]]
- **Population:** 31,434,103 (modern estimate)
- **Ruling house:** [[Kadim-Galwa]]
- **Founding regent:** [[Sahra of Galwa]]
- **Early king:** [[Cali IV of Galwa]]
- **Later monarch:** [[Nuur VI of Galwa]]
- **Primary historic sea orientation:** [[Aethiopean Ocean]] trade routes
- **Main rival:** the eastern [[Kingdom of Burawa]]
- **Major regional concerns:** [[Bindorama]], [[Asikyira]], [[Kijura]], and North Kaftian holdings

## Historical arc

### 1709–1743: formation and regency
- Galwa emerged from the [[Burawa Imperial Split]] as the western kingdom.
- [[Sahra of Galwa]] ruled as regent during Cali IV's minority.
- The [[New Laws of 1710]] centralised administration and created the provincial system.
- Galwa stayed neutral in the [[First War of the Isthmus]].
- The [[Bindorama Civil War]] weakened Galwa's colonial position and ended with the creation of [[Asikyira]].
- In 1743, Cali IV created the [[Parliament of Lords (Galwa)]], ending the absolutist regency model.

### 1743–1822: commercial and colonial reorientation
- Galwa turned toward trade, naval links, and North Kaftian expansion.
- The kingdom built commercial ties with [[Zong]] and military ties with [[Breisland]].
- [[Nuur VI of Galwa]] expanded state finance, colonial administration, and education.
- The period ended with the [[Burawi War]] and the creation of [[Kijura]].

### 1822–1892: reform and liberalisation
- Galwa moved from feudal agrarian structures toward a more commercial state.
- Urban reform, land sales, and freer trade strengthened port cities and the merchant class.
- The [[Commoners' Act of 1865]] and the [[Free Trade Act of 1852]] are key markers of the period.
- The North Kaftian colony was incorporated as [[Laga]] in 1872.

## Politics
- Early Galwa was highly centralised and court-driven.
- The regency period was governed through the [[Council of Regents (Galwa)]].
- Later, the kingdom developed limited representative institutions through the [[Parliament of Lords (Galwa)]].
- The church retained influence throughout, but the crown increasingly balanced clerical power with state centralisation.
