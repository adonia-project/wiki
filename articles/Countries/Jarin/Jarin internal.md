# Jarin — Internal Notes

## Overview
Jarin is a major Mesogean-influenced state in southeastern [[Kaftia]]. The repo currently treats it as a modern sovereign country with a long historical lineage, including the ancient [[Kingdom of Jarin]] and the later [[United Kingdom of Jarin and Itegar]]. It is one of the larger countries in [[Southeastern Kaftia]], with a modern population of roughly 64 million and a strong role in regional politics, religion, and football.

Jarin is especially important as:
- a historic eastern Kaftian kingdom
- the birthplace and stronghold of [[Lathrianism]]
- a state with a long-running interaction with [[Orma]], [[Itegar]], and [[Burawa]]
- a Mesogean coastal power that hosted the [[1974 AFA World Cup]]

## Historical arc

### Ancient and classical foundations
The earliest deep history linked to Jarin comes from the river valleys of southern Jarin, where the [[Qush civilization]] emerged around 4000 BCE. The region is portrayed in the repository as one of the earliest civilized areas on [[Adonia]], with the first writing system on the planet developing there.

In later antiquity, the [[Kingdom of Jarin]] appears after the rebellion of '''General Theodosius''' around 100 CE, when he declared independence from the Mesogean Empire and established a state centered on the former province of Triphylia. This marks Jarin as an enduring political entity long before the modern state.

### Early modern rivalry and expansion
By the early modern period, Jarin had become a significant indigenous kingdom on the southeastern Kaftian coast. During the [[War of the Mesogean]], Jarin seized territory from [[Orma]], reshaping the regional balance and securing a more strategic position between inland and coastal powers.

The repository also describes Jarin as having significant Mesogean cultural influence, which suggests that the kingdom had deep historical contact with coastal and imperial traditions, even while preserving its own political identity.

### United Kingdom of Jarin and Itegar
In the early 19th century, Jarin was the home of the [[United Kingdom of Jarin and Itegar]]. This period is especially important for the history of religion in Jarin, because [[Lathrianism]] emerged there during a broader Christian awakening.

The Jarin–Itegar period is the clearest sign in the repository that modern Jarin should be presented not just as a territorial unit, but as a historically layered state with a distinct constitutional and religious development.

### Personal union with Burawa
During the [[Constitutional Era of Burawa]], Burawa entered into a personal union with the [[Kingdom of Jarin]] through the marriage of [[Nuur V]] to the Princess of Jarin. The union did not create formal political integration, but it aligned Jarin's foreign policy with Burawi interests and strengthened the eastern frontier balance.

This is one of the main historical hooks tying Jarin to the broader Burawi story.

## Geography and regional role
Jarin is part of the southern Mesogean coast of Southeastern Kaftia. In the regional geography article, Jarin is listed among the southern-coast states with a Mediterranean climate, and the region is associated with river valleys, coastal plains, and iron ore deposits.

Notable geographic associations in the repo include:
- the southern river-valley traditions of the [[Gash River]] and [[Baraka River]]
- iron ore deposits in Jarin's highland regions near the [[Enfolo]] border
- strong cultural and trade connections to the wider Mesogean coast

## Religion and culture
Jarin is the origin point of [[Lathrianism]], a nontrinitarian Christian movement founded in the early 19th century in the [[United Kingdom of Jarin and Itegar]]. The faith remains small today, but it is still strongest in Jarin and surrounding southern Kaftian regions.

The repository also suggests that Jarin influenced Burawa religious life through what it calls '''Jarinese theology''', which helped shape some strands of Burawi Protestant and forgiveness-oriented thought.

Other cultural notes:
- Jarin is strongly tied to Mesogean coastal civilization
- it has long-standing Christian traditions alongside broader Kaftian religious diversity
- the [[Jarani people]] form a recognized diaspora community in Burawa
