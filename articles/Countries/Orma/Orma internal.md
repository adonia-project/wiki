# Orma — Internal Notes

## Overview
Orma is a major eastern highland state in [[Southwestern Kaftia]] on the [[Mesogean Sea]] side of the region, situated between [[Itegar]] and [[Burawa]]. It is one of the most important historical kingdoms of the Orma highlands and has long acted as a buffer power between the inland highlands and the coastal sphere, especially [[Jarin]]. The modern country has a population of about 24,324,593 and is strongly tied to the highland corridor, trade through mountain passes, and the balance-of-power politics of the southern Kaftian interior.

The capital is [[Dirranmoticha]], which functions as the principal political and administrative centre of the country.

## Historical background

### Highland origins
Orma emerged from the eastern highland chiefdoms of Southwestern Kaftia. The Orma highlands were historically distinct from the Burawi core, with local elites controlling the mountain passes and trade routes linking the interior to the broader Kaftian mainland.

The early state developed around a mix of pastoral, agricultural, and transit-based communities. Its geography gave it strategic value despite the relatively rugged terrain, because the eastern passes created one of the key overland corridors between the southern highlands and the coast.

### Formation of the Kingdom of Orma
The medieval consolidation of the [[Kingdom of Orma]] was accelerated by the [[Northern Conquest of Burawa]] (893–925). During that period, an Orma ruler allied with Burawa against their shared enemy, the [[Kingdom of Ufyen]], and used the prestige of that alliance to consolidate authority over rival Orma chiefdoms.

This alliance helped transform Orma from a cluster of regional highland polities into a recognisable kingdom with a central court and a stronger identity.

### Independence and Christian development
Orma later developed into a durable buffer state. After the collapse of Ufyen, its relationship with Burawa alternated between alliance and rivalry, especially over the eastern frontier and adjacent borderlands.

A particularly important part of Orma's history is that it converted to [[Christianity]] independently of the [[Burawi Catholic Church]], through Mesogean trade contacts along the southeastern coast. This created a distinct religious tradition and helped Orma build ties with coastal powers that wanted to preserve it as a counterweight to Burawi expansion.

### War of the Mesogean and eastern loss
During the [[War of the Mesogean]], Orma aligned with [[Volisania]] against Burawa. That decision exposed Orma's eastern frontier, allowing [[Kingdom of Jarin|Jarin]] to seize a substantial eastern district. That territory later became [[Itegar]], which was organised as a buffer after the Kingdom of Jarin divided.

This was one of the most consequential turns in Orma's territorial history, because it permanently altered the eastern balance of power and deepened the Orma–Jarin frontier relationship.

### Modern era
Modern Orma remains a significant inland state with a strong highland identity and a continued role in regional politics. It is still defined by its frontier position, its mixed eastern districts, and its historic role as a state that survived through balancing larger neighbours rather than by conquering them.

## Geography and settlement
Orma is centred on the eastern highlands of Southwestern Kaftia and the Mesogean-facing frontier between [[Itegar]] and [[Burawa]]. The terrain is mountainous and strategically fragmented, with settlement concentrated in river valleys, pass towns, plateau markets, and coastal or near-coastal corridors.

[[Dirranmoticha]] is the capital and main administrative centre. The country's eastern districts are more linguistically mixed than the interior, reflecting centuries of trade and border contact with Jarin and the broader Mesogean east.

Notable geographic features for future expansion:
- highland passes linking Orma to the coast and interior
- border districts facing [[Jarin]]
- older Christian market towns in the central highlands
- eastern communities with stronger Beja and Itegarite influence

## Language and culture
Ormic is the dominant language in Orma and the main language of state, education, and public life. In the eastern districts, there are also communities speaking Beja/Jaranese and Hebrew/Itegarite, especially near the frontier and along older trade routes.

Culturally, Orma is best understood as a highland Christian kingdom with a frontier identity. It is not simply a neighbour of Burawa or Jarin; it is a separate highland civilisation with its own elite traditions, church history, and political memory.

## Economy
Orma's economy is likely to remain more highland and corridor-based than coastal or heavy-industrial. The country probably depends on:
- highland agriculture
- livestock and pastoral production
- mountain trade routes
- border markets
- administrative and ecclesiastical functions in Dirranmoticha

The eastern frontier also suggests strong local trade with Jarin and the Itegar corridor, especially in mixed-language districts.
