# Native fauna of Guajicutea

Guajicutea is an oceanic volcanic island at roughly 6°N, shaped by steep elevation gradients, trade winds, and isolation. Its native fauna is therefore a mix of:

- **Marine species** that use the island's reefs, seamount slopes, and pelagic waters.
- **Island-endemic land animals** descended from ancestors that crossed ocean gaps by air, rafting, or swimming.
- **Altitude specialists** sorted by the sharp climate transition from hot coast to cool upper slopes.

No human-introduced species are considered here.

## Marine and coastal

### Reef fish
The warm, shallow reefs and rocky volcanic shores support a typical tropical reef assemblage:

- **Parrotfish** (*Scarus* spp. and relatives) — scrape algae from coral and volcanic rock, important for reef erosion and sand production.
- **Damselfish and clownfish** — nest in sea anemones and coral heads.
- **Surgeonfish and tangs** — graze algae on reef flats.
- **Grouper and snapper** — larger predators around reef drop-offs and seamount slopes.
- **Wrasse** — diverse cleaners and invertebrate predators.
- **Trumpetfish and needlefish** — patrol reef edges and the surface.

### Pelagic and open-water fish
Beyond the reef, the island sits at the edge of a productive shelf break:

- **Flying fish** — abundant, launching from wave faces; a traditional food source.
- **Mackerel, jacks, and bonito** — schooling predators.
- **Tuna** — pass through seasonally in deeper water.
- **Marlin and sailfish** — occasional visitors offshore.
- **Barracuda** — patrol reef channels and drop-offs.

### Sharks, rays, and large marine life
- **Reef sharks** — whitetip and blacktip reef sharks along drop-offs.
- **Hammerhead sharks** — occasionally aggregate near seamounts off the northern point.
- **Stingrays and eagle rays** — over sand flats and seagrass patches.
- **Manta rays** — rare seasonal visitors to plankton-rich upwellings.
- **Sea turtles** — green turtles graze seagrass; hawksbills nest on remote beaches; leatherbacks pass offshore.

### Marine mammals
- **Spinner dolphins and spotted dolphins** — common in channels and along the shelf edge.
- **Humpback whales** — migrate past the island in winter, though calving is rare.
- **Sperm whales** — deep divers off the continental slope to the west.

### Coastal birds
- **Frigatebirds** — soar on trade winds, nest in mangroves and cliff trees.
- **Brown pelicans** — plunge-dive near shore.
- **Boobies** — red-footed and brown boobies nest on sea cliffs and islets.
- **Terns, noddies, and skimmers** — nest on beaches and rocky cays.
- **Herons and egrets** — hunt in mangrove creeks and tidal flats.

## Estuaries and mangroves

The low, muddy coasts and tidal river mouths support:

- **Mangrove crabs** — several species, including large land crabs that migrate to the sea to spawn.
- **Fiddler crabs** — on mudflats.
- **Mudskippers** — in tidal mangrove channels.
- **Snappers and mullet** — juvenile fish shelter in mangrove roots.
- **Tarpon** — enter brackish river mouths to hunt.

## Lowland forests and savanna

The hot lowlands are dominated by tropical seasonal forest on the windward side and drier forest/savanna woodland on the leeward west.

### Birds
- **Toucans and aracaris** — fruit-eaters in wetter forests.
- **Trogons and motmots** — sit-and-wait insect hunters.
- **Honeycreepers and tanagers** — nectar and fruit feeders, often brightly colored.
- **Parrots and parakeets** — nest in tree cavities; their loud flocks are audible across valleys.
- **Owls** — including a small endemic screech-owl in the dry forest.
- **Hummingbirds** — abundant on flowering shrubs and canopy trees.
- **Raptors** — broad-winged hawks, kites, and an endemic forest falcon.

### Reptiles
- **Anoles** — dozens of species, each occupying its own microhabitat (trunk, twig, grass).
- **Iguanas** — a large endemic rock iguana basks on volcanic outcrops; a smaller tree iguana lives in dry forest.
- **Skinks and whiptails** — ground-dwelling insectivores.
- **Snakes** — tree boas, racers, and a rear-fanged colubrid; no dangerously venomous land snakes.
- **Geckos** — nocturnal hunters on walls and tree trunks.

### Amphibians
- **Tree frogs** — several endemic species, including one that breeds in bromeliad pools.
- **Poison frogs** — small, brightly colored species in wet forest leaf litter.
- **Toads** — in drier lowlands.
- **Stream frogs** — along clean mountain rivers.

### Mammals
Native land mammals are limited by island isolation:

- **Bats** — the most diverse mammal group. Fruit bats pollinate figs and dispersed forest trees. Insectivorous bats hunt over rivers and clearings.
- **Rice rats and spiny rats** — descendants of rafting ancestors; occupy forest floor and savanna.
- **Opossums** — one or two arboreal species.

No large native predators or ungulates exist.

## Montane and cloud forests

Cool, wet upper slopes create a distinct community:

- **Quetzal relatives and trogons** — fruit-eaters of cloud forest canopy.
- **Solitaires and nightingale-thrushes** — songbirds of the misty mid-elevations.
- **Brush-finches and wrens** — dense understory birds.
- **Endemic cloud-forest frogs** — small, direct-developing species that skip the tadpole stage.
- **Giant land snails** — abundant in the leaf litter.
- **Orb-weaver spiders and stick insects** — large invertebrates of the cloud forest.

## Alpine and rocky summits

The highest volcanic peaks are cold, windy, and sparsely vegetated:

- **Volcano juncos and highland finches** — small birds adapted to cold nights.
- **Rock lizards** — dark, heat-absorbing lizards on sun-warmed lava.
- **Highland butterflies** — a few species that ride thermals up from lower slopes.
- **Scavenging beetles and flies** — around dead vegetation and animal remains.

## Freshwater rivers and streams

The island's rivers are short, steep, and rain-fed:

- **Freshwater shrimp** (*Atya* and *Macrobrachium*) — climb waterfalls and graze algae.
- **Gobies and sleeper gobies** — bottom-dwelling fish in lower reaches.
- **Mountain mullet** — in cleaner, cooler streams.
- **Stream crabs** — endemic freshwater crabs under rocks.
- **Aquatic insect larvae** — caddisflies, mayflies, and stoneflies; important food for fish.
- **Water striders and whirligig beetles** — on calm pools.

## A notable nearby fish: the Guajicutea flyingfish

The waters around Guajicutea are famous for a local population of **flyingfish** that launch in large schools at dawn and dusk. Islanders traditionally caught them with torches and woven nets from canoes. They are not a single endemic species, but a distinct regional population adapted to the island's shelf-break currents and the abundance of surface plankton. Their glides are longer here than in adjacent waters because of the steady northeast trades and the way the volcanic shelf funnels swell.

## Endemism and biogeography

Because Guajicutea is isolated, many of its land animals are endemic or nearly so:

- At least **three endemic anole lizards**, one per major habitat: dry forest, seasonal forest, and cloud forest.
- **One endemic rock iguana** restricted to the lava cliffs of the central caldera.
- **Two endemic hummingbirds**, one in the wet lowlands and one in the cloud forest.
- **Several endemic land snails and freshwater crabs**, limited to single watersheds.
- **One endemic fruit bat**, larger than its mainland relatives.

The fauna is most closely related to the broader Caribbean/West Indian region, with some Central American and South American influences carried by wind, currents, and occasional rafting events.
