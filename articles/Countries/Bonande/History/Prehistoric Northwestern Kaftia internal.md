# Prehistoric Northwestern Kaftia — internal notes

Article: `Prehistoric Northwestern Kaftia.mediawiki` (created 2026-11-02; retitled from "Prehistoric Bonande" the same day per user ruling — regional naming, matching `Prehistoric Southwestern Kaftia`). Working notes: `articles/Countries/Bonande/Bonande internal.md`.

## Canon basis (user-confirmed)

- Humans entered the Pfein Sea basin ~200,000 BP, overland from central Kaftia.
- Pfein Sea = end-Cretaceous meteor crater; marine, outlet via Strait of Sindewand; ~285,753 km²; max depth ~800–1,200 m, average ~300 m.
- Mediterranean climate with winter rains around the sea; semi-arid steppe east of the crescent.
- Green phase → desiccation of central Kaftia ~6,000–4,000 BP; central Kaftia now desert (Tamazgha north desert too); overland trade with southern Kaftia greatly reduced; basin reorients to the sea.
- Agricultural package mostly trade-derived from Dakare/Galwa wetlands (sorghum, pearl millet, zebu, domesticated c. 5000 BCE); wheat domesticated locally in the Bande River valley (~5–3 kya), independent of the eastern Mesogean domestication (wheat = twice-domesticated on Adonia); barley via later trade.
- Pre-1100 CE population = the **Bande peoples** (shore and river-valley groups, named for the Bande River, which forms the modern Bonande–Yaweka border) + the **Yaweka** (highland branch, offshoot of the founding population). Modern "Bonandian" identity arose ~1100 CE; prehistory articles must not call these people Bonandians.
- Canon for the future ancient/medieval article (removed from the prehistory article 2026-11-02 at user request — prehistory covers the peoples of the time, not modern identity): the name "Bonande" arose ~1100 CE when the shore and river-valley groups coalesced; the collective form preserves the name of the Bande River and of the pre-coalescence groups. **The Yaweka remained outside the coalescence and retain their own claim of descent from the founding population.**
- Yaweka = second-largest people group in modern Bonande; Bonandei–Yawekai family (Yawekai modeled linguistically on real-world Shambala; Bonandei no IRL model).

## Agent inventions (flagged, pending user review)

- **Sangwe tradition** (~200,000–40,000 BP) — prepared-core lithics; type site **Sangwe Bluff** (raised shore terrace, eastern Pfein coast).
- **Ndewe industry** (~40,000–10,000 BP) — microlithic Later Stone Age; type site **Ndewe Caves** (Yaweka foothills); stable shore settlements, reed watercraft, rock art sequence (hunters/megafauna → fishing/boats → cattle → fields/settlements).
- **Mbandezi culture** (~3000–1000 BCE) — food-producing complex; type site **Mbandezi** on the Bande River.
- Bow/composite tools attributed to the Ndewe industry (not explicitly dated by user).
- Zebu/sorghum/millet arriving "in the final centuries of the corridor" — inference from the desiccation timing (~6–4 kya) vs. southern domestication (c. 5000 BCE); dramatizes the "last chance trade" but is my construction.
- "Wheat is the crop for which Adonian archaeobotany recognises two independent domestication events" — restates the user's double-domestication canon as an in-world scholarly finding; the phrasing is mine.
- Genetic history section — inferred from canon (single entry, direct descent, desert isolation); all specific claims (sister clusters, minor southern Kaftian contribution) are my construction.
- Iron working "reached the basin around 1,000 BCE" — source/route of iron technology NOT established. Left vague deliberately.
- Wider-region section ("Beyond the Pfein basin") — per user canon 2026-11-02: sparse settlement outside the basin, hunter-gatherer/nomadic populations; northern-sea shells found at Pfein basin sites = long-distance trade. Details pending: the northern sea's name, which countries lie on it, the dating of the shell finds.
- Wider-region canon refined (2026-11-02): **Senara is inland, between Makombu and Bonande**; Alwana, Tategazi, Makombu are on the northern sea. **The Yaweka range divides the Pfein basin (south) from the northern sea (north); it is not tall — an impact byproduct — but enough to create natural divisions.** **No desert ever divided the north** — the northern route was open the whole time (the desiccation closed only the southern corridor). **Pfein glass shards flowed north** in exchange for the shells. "Probably continuous through the whole period" is the user's phrasing, rendered as such.
- Wider-region canon completed (2026-11-02, with political map): the northern sea is the **Sankani Sea, also known as the Sankani Gulf** — part of the Ethiopian Ocean, "more of a gulf than a sea". The **inner-ring states (Sindewand, Zetaba, Wolffrea, Yaweka, Bonande) cover the crater**; the **outer-ring states are split from them by the Yaweka ridge**, which was formed from the crater. The crater was excavated in **what was probably a peninsula of northwestern Kaftia** (in-world geological interpretation). The wider region's nomadic groups were **probably related to the basin's founding population — not confirmed in the historical record**; some **became pastoral nomads** (zebu herding) from the green phase onward.
- **Tamazgha placement corrected (2026-11-02)**: Tamazgha is in **central Kaftia** — south of northwestern Kaftia, north of southern Kaftia. The article's earlier "deserts of Tamazgha to the north" was wrong and has been fixed (the desert belt covering central Kaftia includes Tamazgha; it lies along the southern corridor, not north of the region).
- Map observations (political map, 2026-11-02, resolved same day): spelling is **Tategazi** (canonical). **Makombu lies between Tategazi and Olmora** (small, hidden on the map). **Olmora and Luongo are part of north-central Kaftia** — a different culture, outside the nine-country NW Kaftian Region; a partially-visible country beginning "La…" also appears east of the region. The "part of the Ethiopian Ocean / more of a gulf than a sea" description applies to the **Sankani Sea**, not the Pfein (user confirmed; the Pfein is the white cutout at the map's center).
