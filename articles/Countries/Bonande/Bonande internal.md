## People and language — confirmed

- [CANON 2026-11-01] The majority people of Bonande are the **Bonandian people**; the majority language is the **Bonandei language** (in-world form; "Bondei" was an Earth-style rendering). The Bonandians are the **direct descendants** of the ~200 kya settlers.
- [CANON 2026-11-01] The **Bonandei language** belongs to its own language family, the **Bonandei–Yawekai family**. The **Yawekai branch** is modeled (linguistically) on the real-world Shambala language; the Bonandei branch has no real-world model.
- [CANON 2026-11-01] The **second-largest people group in Bonande is the Yaweka peoples** — most likely a **separation/subgroup of the Bonandian peoples**, a population that **split off from the founding group at some point long ago** (presumably into the rim highlands). The Yaweka also claim traces/descent from the original settlers. The two branches of the Bonandei–Yawekai language family therefore mirror this single founding population splitting in two.

## The pre-1100 peoples — canon ruling and naming

- [CANON 2026-11-02] The modern construction of the **"Bonande people" arose around 1100 CE**. Before that period, the population must be referred to as **different groups** — regional shore, river-valley, and highland groups speaking Bonandei–Yawekai languages. Prehistory and ancient-history articles must NOT call these people "Bonandians".
- The **Yaweka** are the one branch that stayed outside the 1100 CE coalescence, keeping their distinct identity (while also claiming descent from the ancient settlers).
- Working implication: the 1100 CE coalescence united the **shore and river-valley groups** into "Bonande"; the highland Yaweka remained apart. What drove the coalescence is a question for the ancient/medieval history article.

### Naming — RESOLVED (user chose Option A, 2026-11-02)

The Yaweka stayed outside the coalescence.

## Agriculture — confirmed

- [CANON 2026-11-01] Ancient Bonande's agricultural package was a **mix, mostly trade-derived**: most agricultural crops/animals came via trade from the **Dakare/Galwa wetlands** (the established southern domestication center — sorghum, pearl millet, zebu, ~5000 BCE). The more resilient grains — **wheat and barley** — arrived via **later trades**.
- [CANON 2026-11-01] In Adonia, olives, figs, sheep, and pigs originated in the **southern part of the world** — so northwestern Kaftia had *different* grains and did not develop a Mediterranean-package analogue.
- [CANON 2026-11-01, user proposal] **Wheat was domesticated twice on Adonia**: once in **northwestern Kaftia** and once in the **eastern Mesogean**. The two domestications are independent. (In-world scientific finding; needs a dedicated article eventually.)
- [CANON 2026-11-02] The northwestern Kaftian wheat domestication center was the **valley of the Bonande–Yaweka border river**, roughly **~5,000–3,000 years ago** — local wild wheat taken into cultivation on the well-watered valley margins as the desiccation pushed populations riverward.

## Ancient history anchor — the desiccation of central Kaftia

- [CANON 2026-11-01] The defining event for ancient history: a **drying period turned the area between northwestern Kaftia and southern Kaftia into desert — central Kaftia is now desert**. Tamazgha in the north is desert as well. (User wrote "tamazga"; repo-established spelling is **Tamazgha** — confirm.)
- Consequence: overland trade between the Pfein basin and southern Kaftia was **greatly reduced** (low trade volumes across the desert belt). The basin's trade orientation shifted to the sea — coastal routes around the Pfein Sea and out through the Strait of Sindewand to the Ethiopian Ocean. Tamazgha and the desert belt form the northern/eastern arid frontier.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] Timing: **~6,000–4,000 years ago** — the same arid reversion that ended the green phase on the plains.

## The wider Northwest Kaftian Region — canon (2026-11-02)

- [CANON 2026-11-02] Outside the Pfein basin, there was **not much recorded (archaeological) human settlement** in the rest of the region — the wider region was occupied mostly by **hunter-gatherer and nomadic peoples**.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] There was **trade with other regions**: shells from **the northern sea** — the sea that [[Alwana]], [[Tategazi]], and [[Makombu]] face — have been **discovered at Pfein basin sites**, evidencing long-distance exchange between the basin and the northern coast.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] **Senara is inland, between Makombu and Bonande** (does not face the northern sea).
- [CANON 2026-11-02] The **Yaweka range divides the Pfein basin (south) from the northern sea (north)** — it is **not tall** (impact-crater byproduct) but enough to create natural divisions.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] **No desert ever divided the north**: the northern route over the crescent was open the whole time (the desiccation closed only the SOUTHERN corridor to southern Kaftia); northern trade probably continuous through all periods.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] **Pfein impact glass shards flowed north** in exchange for the northern-sea shells.
- [CANON 2026-11-02] The northern sea is the **Sankani Sea, also known as the Sankani Gulf** — part of the **Ethiopian Ocean**, "more of a gulf than a sea".
- [CANON 2026-11-02] The wider region's nomadic groups were **probably related to the basin's founding population** — not confirmed in the historical record. From the green phase onward, some **became pastoral nomads** (zebu herding spreading with them).

## Geological setting — for discussion

The Pfein impact is this world's end-Cretaceous extinction impact. Working inferences (to confirm with user):

- The Pfein Sea occupies the impact crater itself; the Yaweka Mountain Range is the eroded crater rim uplift, forming a crescent around the sea's eastern and northern margins.
- The Northwest Kaftian Depression is the broader crater basin — Bonande sits within it, on the eastern side of the sea (sea to the west, Yaweka crescent wrapping east and north).
- Likely impact-related geology: impact breccias, shocked quartz, an iridium-anomalous boundary layer (in-world scientists' evidence for the impact), and possibly fields of **impact glass** on the surrounding plains — a distinctive raw material for prehistoric toolmakers and a later gemstone/trade item.
- Crater-fill sediments could host evaporites, fertile lacustrine soils at the sea margins, and possibly hydrocarbons (modern economic potential).

## Prehistory — proposed framework

User anchor: humans migrated into the region around **200,000 years ago**, coming north from central Kaftia. Proposed sequence for discussion:

### Crater refugium model [PROPOSED]

The Pfein Sea is a permanent **marine** water body of ~286,000 km² — comparable to a small ocean basin set inside a continent — with a Mediterranean climate ringing its shores and drier country to the east. The sea, connected to the Ethiopian Ocean through the Strait of Sindewand, never desiccated: through every Pleistocene arid phase, the shore, its rivers, and the Yaweka springs retained water, game, and fish, while the surrounding plains oscillated between steppe and desert. The depression therefore functioned as a **long-term population refugium** — and since the Bonandians are the direct descendants of the ~200 kya settlers, the Bonandian lineage may be among the oldest continuously-occupied lines in Kaftia. This predicts:

- Near-continuous human occupation of the depression for ~200 kya (unusual continuity for a desert belt).
- Repeated population contractions to the shore and expansions back out onto the plains, tracking pluvial/arid cycles.
- Deep local cultural strata — lithic and later traditions with strong continuity and distinctive regional signatures.
- The salt sea gives the shore economy a **marine** character: coastal fisheries, shellfish gathering, sea mammals/turtles, and (later) watercraft — distinct from the freshwater fishing traditions of the Great Kaftian Lake basin to the south.

### Entry of humans [PROPOSED]

- The first humans entered the depression ~200 kya, **overland from central Kaftia to the south** — not via the coast.
- Proposed framing: the arid belt between central Kaftia and the depression was a barrier; the first entry occurred during a pluvial (wet) phase that opened a corridor north. After entry, the depression population was repeatedly isolated during arid phases.
- This is later than the settlement of the Southwestern Kaftian peninsula (~300 kya via two corridors from the main Kaftian continent to the east, per Prehistoric Southwestern Kaftia) — consistent with a broader pattern of early humans expanding outward from/through central Kaftia in different directions.

### Periodization sketch [PROPOSED — modelled on real-world African prehistory, to be reskinned]

| Period | Approx. span | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Early occupation | ~200 kya | First humans in the depression, arriving from central Kaftia. Large cutting tools / prepared-core lithics. |
| Middle Stone Age | ~200–40 kya | Prepared-core (Levallois-type) technology; beginnings of aquatic adaptation on the Pfein shore — fish, shellfish, sea turtles, waterfowl; use of impact glass as a raw material. |
| Later Stone Age | ~40–6 kya | Microlithic technology; bow; rock art in Yaweka caves and overhangs; stable shore settlements; distinctive Pfein fishery (reed/papyrus watercraft, harpoons, fish traps). |
| Green/pluvial phase | ~12–6 kya | Wet phase across the depression: grassland, rivers, marshes, megafauna. Pastoralism arrives; cattle-period rock art; early pottery. Population expansion away from the shore. |
| Arid reversion | ~6–4 kya | Plains dry out again; **central Kaftia desiccates into desert**, cutting the overland corridor to southern Kaftia. Populations contract back to the sea, mountain springs, and river oases; intensification of fishing and herding; beginnings of simple irrigation off seasonal rivers. |
| Food production | ~5–3 kya | Agricultural package arrives mostly via trade from the Dakare/Galwa wetlands (sorghum, pearl millet, zebu); **local wheat domestication** (the northwestern Kaftian domestication); wheat/barley also arriving via later trades; permanent shore villages; long-distance trade in impact glass, salt (evaporite pans), and dried fish. |
| Iron Age | ~3–2 kya | Iron smelting; village farming societies; population growth; proto-polities forming along the shore and river corridors → leads into Ancient history. |

### Distinctive worldbuilding hooks [PROPOSED]

- **Pfein impact glass** — prized blade material in the Stone Age, gemstone and export item later; a signature artifact of the region's archaeology.
- **Yaweka rock art sequence** — a datable art sequence in the rim caves: hunter/megafauna → aquatic/fishing → pastoral → boat/early-agricultural periods.
- **Paleo-shorelines** — the sea level has fluctuated over millennia; abandoned shore terraces are archaeological markers (settlement layers following the retreating waterline).
- **Marine endemics** — the Pfein Sea's restricted single-outlet geometry may have produced distinctive marine ecosystems.
