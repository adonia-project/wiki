# Balboa Canal — Internal Notes

## Overview
- 82 km lock-based canal across Isthmus of Balboa
- Connects Aethiopian Ocean (east) ↔ Shendan Ocean (west)
- One of the most important commercial waterways on Adonia
- Operated by AC Canal Commission since Treaty of Areza (1973)

## Engineering
- 3 lock sets, 6 chambers total: Aethiopian Locks, Alvarado Locks, Shendan Locks
- Lock chambers: 320m × 33.5m, 15.5m depth over sill
- "Balboa limits": max 300m length, 40m beam, 15.2m draft
- Lake Alvarado: artificial lake at ~26m elevation, central reach ~35km
- Route: Sant Llàtzer (Aethiopian/east) → Estret lowlands → locks → Lake Alvarado → locks → Portnou (Shendan/west)
- Portnou selected over Sant Cristòfor as western terminus (better harbour depth, shelter, expansion capacity)
- Sant Cristòfor is on the Shendan coast (major city but NOT the canal terminus)
- Sant Llàtzer is on the Aethiopian coast (Camí Ral eastern terminus, canal eastern terminus)

## History
- Pre-canal: Birú portage routes, 4–6 days crossing
- **Camí Ral** (Royal Road): Volisanian colonial highway, mid-16th century; connected Sant Llàtzer (Aethiopian) ↔ Sant Cristòfor (Shendan); reduced crossing to ~2 days; oxcart convoys, later stagecoaches
- **Sant Llàtzer** (Aethiopian/east coast) and **Sant Cristòfor** (Shendan/west coast): both established mid-16th century at the two termini of the portage route
- High transit/docking fees at Sant Cristòfor → one of the principal causes of the **First War of the Isthmus** (1711–1716)
- Treaty of Remoria (1716): Zong withdrew but got docking/passage rights through Volisanian ports
- **Isthmus Railway**: 1816, one of the earliest steam-powered commercial railways on Adonia; parallel to Camí Ral; between Sant Llàtzer and Sant Cristòfor; reduced crossing to ~4 hours; demonstrated transshipment bottleneck
- **Isthmus Railway Company**: Volisanian-owned; paid only land rent to Balboa; bulk of profits went to Volisanian shareholders

### Political context for construction
- Post-abolition (1854 independence): plantation economy collapsed; National Party + planter elite dominated
- **Expansió Party**: centre-right, national expansion + economic revival; opposed Volisanian private monopoly
- **Escàndol Ferroviari** (Railway Scandal): mid-1860s; Isthmus Railway Company + Zong shipping bribed National Party legislators to reduce fees and block canal legislation → destroyed National Party credibility
- 1868: Expansió Party won majority + presidency
- Expansió tried to nationalise railway → Volisania threatened embargo → retreated
- Turned to canal project instead; approached Zong + Nakamizu for financing
- Volisania alarmed by Zong interest → offered to finance/engineer itself to block Zong expansion
- **Balboa-Volisanian Company**: joint venture; 60% Volisania, 20% domestic bonds, 20% Nakamizu
- Nakamizu took on large debt → contributed to early 20th century economic malaise; loss of canal to Zong (1899) ended all repayments
- Construction relatively straightforward: already deforested + level; single continuous programme
- Opened 14 September 1885
- Expansió Party lost power in 1888 Sant Bart's Coup → Balboa Independence Party → led to Second War of the Isthmus

### Governance
- **Owner**: Republic of Balboa
- **Operator**: Balboa Canal Authority (Balboan state agency)
- **AC Canal Commission**: oversight/monitoring body (not operator); ensures Treaty of Areza compliance
- Treaty of Areza (1973): canal = international waterway, non-discriminatory access, Balboa cannot grant preferential access
- 1870s: Volisanian private consortium failed (cost, disease)
- 1878: Volisanian government took over; military engineers
- 1885: Completed, opened 14 September
- 1893–1899: Second War of the Isthmus → transferred to Zong
- Zong period: expanded locks, deepened channel, naval base, discriminatory tolls
- GAW: canal closed to commercial traffic, infrastructure damaged
- 1950: AC mandate; Canal Commission established
- 1950–1967: Reconstruction programme; reopened 1958
- 1973: Treaty of Areza → Balboan sovereignty, AC Canal Commission retains operational authority

## Operations
- ~14,000 transits/year; ~5% of world seaborne trade by volume
- Standard transit: 10–12 hours
- 24/7 operation except scheduled maintenance (1–2 weeks/year)
- Licensed pilot mandatory; satellite tracking + automated scheduling (since 1990s)
- Tolls: tonnage + transit type; average ~$450,000/container vessel (2026)
- Annual revenue: ~$6.5B; split between Commission, Balboan government, AC general fund
- Capacity approaching design limit; expansion studies underway but not approved

## Governance
- AC Canal Commission: AC + Balboa + 5 rotating maritime nations
- Permanent neutrality; non-discriminatory access
- Commission authority supersedes Balboan law within canal zone (~10km either side)
- Areza: within canal zone but governed separately under Treaty of Areza

## Environmental Impact
- Lake Alvarado: inundated ~420 km² of forest + agricultural land
- Fragmented forest corridor between Aethiopian and Shendan slopes
- Ballast water: invasive species in Lake Alvarado + canal corridor
- Water pollution, shoreline erosion, marine mammal disruption
- Regulations: ballast exchange, speed limits, discharge restrictions
- Proposed expansion: significant dredging/construction; environmental impact assessment underway
