# Balboa–Zong Balboa Cross-Border Trafficking — Internal Notes

## Article Info
- **File**: `articles/Countries/Balboa/History/Balboa–Zong Balboa cross-border trafficking.mediawiki`
- **Date written**: 2026-05-22
- **Scope**: 1899–1973 (Treaty of Sant Llàtzer through reunification); NOT modern trafficking

## Key Flows
1. **South → North (Balboa → Zong Balboa)**: Human trafficking (migrants, sex workers, skilled labourers) + narcotics (marijuana, cocaine)
2. **North → South (Zong Balboa → Balboa)**: Luxury goods, manufactured items, capital (Zong currency, gold, negotiable instruments)

## Origins
- Displaced soldiers after Second War of the Isthmus (1899) — discharged without pensions, turned to smuggling
- Displaced plantation workers — economic disparity between Zong Balboa (higher wages, legal equality) and Balboa interior (racial codes, subsistence wages)
- Tightly controlled border created demand for illegal crossing

## Narcotics Production
- **Produced in**: Tramuntana and Nurra hinterlands (Yalabirga highland valleys, isolated from constabulary)
- **Substances**: Marijuana and cocaine
- **Banned in both nations** by GAW
- **Demand**: Zong/Sinchewese elite in Zong Balboa; later broader consumer base during AC period
- **Price deflation during AC period**: Vehicular smuggling (cars/trucks) increased volume, reduced prices

## Smuggling Methods
| Method | Era | Details |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Overland (foot, night) | Pre-war | 3-5 persons guided; Yalabirga highlands; 1-3 nights |
| Coastal (small boats) | Pre-war | Shendan coast; isolated beaches; Zong naval presence not oriented to customs |
| Concealment in legitimate cargo | Pre-war | Agricultural produce, construction materials; bribed constabulary |
| Vehicular (cars/trucks) | AC period | Concealed at authorised checkpoints; more permissive customs |

## GAW Disruption (1935–1947)
- Border became active military front
- Constabulary destroyed as coherent force
- Production continued at reduced levels but distribution severed
- Human trafficking ceased entirely
- Population movements during war were different character (refugees, forced labour, famine)

## AC Mandate Period (1950–1973)
- Border more permissive than pre-war
- ICPF oriented toward Twelve Guarantees enforcement, not customs/immigration
- Narcotics trade re-emerged first; vehicular smuggling caused price deflation
- Human trafficking re-emerged more slowly; AC mandate oversight reduced incidence vs pre-war
- Pre-war Zong period: human trafficking "much more blatant"

## Provincial Government Complicity
- **Tramuntana and Nurra** provincial governments funded themselves from drug manufacture
- Officials diverted constabulary patrols, provided transport licences, ensured perfunctory customs inspections
- Provincial funds directly invested in cultivation/processing
- Revenue supplemented insufficient provincial budgets
- **Cabinet ministers** in Balboa national government were aware of the conspiracy
- Exposed during **reunification inquisitions** (after AC period ended)
- Major political scandal → contributed to provincial governance reform and modern anti-corruption framework
