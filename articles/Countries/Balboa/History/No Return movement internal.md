# No Return Movement — Internal Notes

## Quick Reference
- Creole: Mouvman No Vuelta
- Volisanian: Moviment No Retorn
- Active: 1952–1960
- HQ: Sant Cristòfor
- Methods: Mass demonstrations, press campaigns, civil resistance, silent protests
- Key result: Did NOT prevent return, but forced the Twelve Guarantees
- Rallying cry: "Nou la, nou rest la" ("We are here, we are staying")
- Annual commemoration: 14 March (silent vigil on harbour boulevard)

## Key People
- **Miquela Aldana**: Editor-in-chief of La Voz del Istmo; movement's intellectual voice; "The Language of the Crossing" essays
- **Djanèt Ousmane**: Labour correspondent; organised community/labour associations; mass base organiser
- **Oriol Cardona**: Cultural correspondent; linked movement to Second Star cultural institutions
- **Elena Vargas**: Poet; pamphlets distributed at demonstrations; "The Walls" read at 1957 demonstration

## Demonstrations (all 14 March)
| Year | Attendance | Key Feature |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1953 | 30,000 | First major demonstration; speeches from Municipal Gallery steps; petition of 15,000 signatures |
| 1955 | 50,000 | Emphasised race codes (Labour Preservation Act 1952); first silent protest component (5 min) |
| 1957 | 75,000 | Consultative Assembly endorsed; Vargas read "The Walls"; virtually every community org attended |
| 1958 | 100,000 | Silent protest (30 min); triggered by AC entering negotiations with Balboa; iconic photograph; handmade "No Vuelta" and "Independencia" signs |

## Internal Divisions
- **Radical wing**: Barri Negre + labour associations → outright independence
- **Moderate wing**: Commercial class + pragmatic leaders → binding guarantees as condition of return
- Independence movement overlapped but was distinct; Consultative Assembly passed independence resolution 1956
- Divergence never resolved; independence weakened by AC refusal + no international support

## Outcome
- Did NOT prevent return (Treaty of Balboa Harbour, 14 September 1960)
- DID force the Twelve Guarantees as non-negotiable condition
- 50,000 stood in silent protest at transfer ceremony (echoing 1958 demonstration)
- Movement leadership did not attend ceremony

## Legacy
- Celebrated as force that secured the Guarantees
- 14 March annual commemoration: silent vigil on harbour boulevard
- Ambivalent relationship with BPP (some organisers joined; independence advocates saw acceptance as betrayal)
- "Nou la, nou rest la" inscribed on monument at 1958 demonstration site

## Key Context
- Race codes in Balboa interior: Internal Movement Act (1948), Public Order Act (1950), Labour Preservation Act (1952)
- Mandate period: relative freedom + economic advancement for Black Balboans
- Second Star cultural flowering provided movement's cultural infrastructure
- La Voz del Istmo was movement's principal platform
