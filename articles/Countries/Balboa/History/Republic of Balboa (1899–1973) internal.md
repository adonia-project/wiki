# Republic of Balboa (1899–1973) / Balboa Boba — Internal Notes

## Overview
The rump Balboan state after losing Sant Cristòfor + canal zone to Zong (1899 Treaty of Sant Llàtzer). Official name: Republic of Balboa. Colloquial/period name: **Balboa Boba** ("Foolish Balboa") — pejorative reflecting perception that Balboan leadership brought ruin upon itself by impressing Zong vessels. Capital: Sant Bart. Oligarchic republic under white planter elite. 1899–1973 (74 years). Ended with Treaty of Areza returning canal zone.

## Key Dynamics
- **Economic**: Lost only viable city (Sant Cristòfor) + canal revenue → reduced to agricultural export economy (sugar, coffee, bananas) through minor ports
- **Political**: Oligarchic republic; franchise restricted to literate property-owning men → excluded Black majority; planter elite controlled state
- **Racial**: Systematic repression of Black majority; sharecropping/debt peonage/contract labour = de facto slavery; pass laws, curfews, land ownership restrictions; constabulary as enforcement tool
- **Demographic**: Area: 287,086 km²; Population: ~16M (1899) → ~16.8M (1910) → ~17.2M (1920) → ~18M (1935) → ~11.7M (1947, ~35% GAW loss) → ~12M (1950) → ~15.4M (1960, incl. ~2.4M from Sant Cristòfor mandate) → ~19.5M (1970, baby boom) → ~20.9M (1973, incl. ~400K from canal zone)
- Interior alone did not recover to pre-war peak of 18M; total exceeds 1935 figure due to mandate + canal zone additions
- Racial composition shifts (4 groups: white, Black, Fosian, mixed): 1899 (35% white, 35% Black, 10% Fosian, 20% mixed) → 1935 (45% white, 25% Black, 8% Fosian, 22% mixed — peak white share from Volisanian immigration) → 1947 (40% white, 28% Black, 7% Fosian, 25% mixed — GAW losses) → 1950 (35% white, 32% Black, 8% Fosian, 25% mixed — Volisanian exodus) → 1960 (25% white, 42% Black, 13% Fosian, 20% mixed — mandate return) → 1973 (25% white, 40% Black, 15% Fosian, 20% mixed — full reunification; Black plurality; white + Fosian stable minorities)
- Sant Bart: ~200K (1899) → ~500K (1960); predominantly rural republic
- Urbanisation: integration era saw beginning of rural-to-urban shift; Sant Cristòfor return (~2.4M) created large urban centre
- **Irredentism**: Recovery of Sant Cristòfor + canal = primary national goal; aligned with Volisanian fascism; culminated in GAW Balboa Campaign (1935)

## Presidents
1. **Rodrigo Velásquez** (1888–1912) — BIP; installed by Sant Bart's Coup; led Balboa into Second War of the Isthmus; survived by redirecting anger outward; consolidated planter oligarchy; extended presidential term, restricted franchise, emergency powers
2. **Arturo Mendez** (1912–1943) — BIP; planter/former constabulary officer; aligned with Volisanian fascism; Volisania re-equipped constabulary (violation of Treaty of Sant Llàtzer); intensified racial repression; **1943**: Zong captured Sant Bart → Mendez fled to Volisania; constabulary assumed de facto control; **1947**: handed over to AC by Volisanian provisional government after fascist collapse; convicted of war crimes by AC Tribunal 1949; sentenced to life imprisonment
3. **Efraín Solís** (1948–1956) — PBU; moderate planter, former provincial governor; AC-organised elections 1948 (franchise extended to all literate men); AC banned parties with racist/nationalist ties; PBU = centrist reconstruction party; attempted to balance planter demands with AC requirements; enacted harshest race codes (Internal Movement Act, Public Order Act, Labour Preservation Act)
4. **Cecilio Ríos** (1956–1962) — FeB; Sant Bart lawyer, former minister of justice; pragmatic; FeB split from PBU over pace of reform and negotiation strategy; negotiated Twelve Guarantees; secured ratification by appealing to moderate commercial interests; signed Treaty of Balboa Harbour (1960); **economic liberalisation programme (1956–1959)**: reduced export tariffs, permitted foreign investment in coastal ports, relaxed commercial restrictions; intended to strengthen negotiating position with AC but came too late to alter AC's position; framework later expanded by Guerrero for services-sector development; voted out 1962 by newly enfranchised Black majority
5. **Tomás Guerrero** (1962–1968) — BPP; Black Balboan industrialist; acquired former Zong textile complex in Sant Cristòfor during mandate; elected in first post-Guarantees elections; implemented Guarantees, dismantled racial state, expanded Ríos liberalisation framework for services-sector development; did not seek re-election 1968
6. **Darío Caballero** (1968–1973) — BPP; Black Balboan economist, former minister of finance under Guerrero; consolidation of new order; services-sector boom; negotiated Treaty of Areza (1973); oversaw canal zone return; formal reunification ceremony 1 July 1973 in Sant Llàtzer

## GAW Role
- Co-belligerent with Volisania; Balboa Campaign opened war (1935)
- Constabulary provided ground forces for canal zone assault (poorly trained, poorly led)
- Volisanian forces seized canal zone; Sant Cristòfor held out (walled city)
- Black conscripts + forced labour on military construction; high desertion rates
- War ended 1947; Balboa retained sovereignty (not occupied)

## Post-War (1947–1960)
- Population boom: 1.2M (1935) → 1.5M (1950) → 1.8M (1960); returning soldiers/labourers, high birth rates, influx from canal zone
- Sant Bart overwhelmed by population growth; infrastructure inadequate
- Black population bore brunt of post-war hardship; racial codes still in force

### Harsher Race Codes (1948–1955)
- **Internal Movement Act** (1948): ID documents for all Black Balboans; restricted inter-provincial travel without work permit
- **Public Order Act** (1950): Assemblies of 10+ Black Balboans banned without permit; political organisations for racial equality banned; 30-day detention without charge
- **Labour Preservation Act** (1952): Black Balboans cannot leave plantation employment without employer consent; criminal penalties for breach; employer-managed savings accounts workers can't withdraw from
- International condemnation; AC cited codes as evidence Balboa couldn't guarantee civil rights; No Return movement used codes as primary argument

### Negotiations
- AC position: return conditional on Twelve Guarantees
- Hardline faction (rural provinces + constabulary): reject conditions, press for unconditional return
- Moderate faction (Sant Bart commercial/professional): accept Guarantees in principle, resist in practice
- Resolved in favour of acceptance; Treaty of Balboa Harbour (1960)

## GAW (1935–1947)
- Isthmus = principal battleground of the war
- Two axes: siege of Sant Cristòfor (Zong held walled city via naval supply lines) + canal zone (changed hands opening weeks, contested throughout)
- Balboan interior = logistical base for coalition forces; roads from Sant Bart to front; agricultural output diverted to troops
- Zong naval aircraft bombed Sant Bart + road/rail infrastructure (not decisive but disruptive)
- By final years: Balboan interior exhausted; agricultural output declined; constabulary depleted; Balboan forces no longer capable of offensive operations
- Black conscripts: high desertion; forced labour on military construction

## Integration Era (1960–1973)
- Political: 1962 elections → Balboan People's Party landslide; planter oligarchy lost power
- Economic: plantations broken up/cooperativised; Sant Cristòfor harbour + manufacturing reintegrated; border infrastructure inadequate
- **Transition to services**: Interior had no industrial base; Sant Cristòfor's port + proximity to canal = natural trade/logistics/financial centre; new government favoured services over plantation rehabilitation
- Rural decline: cooperative farms less productive; rural workforce migrated to Sant Cristòfor; housing shortage + infrastructure strain but supplied labour for services sector
- **Late 1960s economic boom**: Services sector (trade, logistics, financial services, tourism) became largest contributor to GDP; first sustained growth since 1899; Sant Cristòfor prosperous again; canal zone indirect activity
- Violence:
  - Rural: white landowners resisted land redistribution; 1963 central highlands standoff (7 deaths, 3 days)
  - Sant Bart: constabulary resisted reform; harassed Black Balboans; ignored racial violence complaints
  - Border region: returning Black mandate population vs. white interior population; harassment, intimidation, physical violence; ICPF increased presence mid-1960s
  - Violence peaked late 1960s/early 1970s; isolated incidents as late as 1973
  - By 1970: plantations broken up, constabulary reformed, Black political dominance established
- Canal zone: returned 1973 (Treaty of Areza); less controversial than Sant Cristòfor return; Guarantees already in force 13 years
- Formal reunification: 1 July 1973, ceremony in Sant Llàtzer

## Economy Details
- Pre-1899: Sant Cristòfor port + canal revenue = principal government income
- Post-1899: Agricultural exports through minor Aethiopean/Shendan ports; inadequate for large-scale traffic; higher shipping costs reduced competitiveness
- Persistent trade deficits; dependent on Volisanian credit
- Mild Depression (1901–1908) compounded difficulties; planter elite intensified labour extraction
- Currency: Balboan peso
