# Balboa security, policing, and military reform — Internal Notes

## Core context
- In the 1990s, the outer hills of northern Estret became a hotbed of religious communities and survivalist communes.
- Local provincial and comarca governments were looking for new revenue streams, so land sales, leases, and tax receipts made isolated settlement more attractive.
- The Hope and Survival Happiness Group was one of several groups that moved into that landscape.

## International image and tourism
- Balboa had just hosted the World Cup successfully.
- The government was eager to project stability and modernity abroad.
- It launched tourism and promotional materials in Fosia and Illypnia to attract visitors, so rural unrest was politically awkward and often downplayed.

## Rural policing structure in the 1990s
- Outside major cities, policing was largely handled by the military.
- This was tied to the war on drug manufacturing in the northern regions.
- The usual pattern was military units burning down plantations or labs, only for production to reappear elsewhere.
- The military was decentralized and unevenly managed.
- Pay differed sharply by branch and role: the navy and central forces were better resourced than the patchwork army units that often functioned like local police.
- That structure produced weak coordination, inconsistent enforcement, and poor command oversight in the interior.

## Reform pressure
- The Hope and Survival Happiness Group standoff was one of the events that pushed the state toward reform.
- The raid on Manuel Sueza's compound is another key example.
- These cases helped justify:
  - creating a national police force for issues outside the major cities
  - refocusing the military on national defense rather than domestic policing
  - reducing troop numbers because of budget constraints

## Longer historical background
- From the 1960s through the 1980s, the Balboan People's Party pushed to modernize the military and distance it from its corrupt and racist past.
- The 1990s are the main visible reform decade, when those long-running efforts became more concrete.
- A national police force had existed before reunification, but it was disbanded during the Twelve Guarantees because it had been associated with extrajudicial killings and state violence against minorities.
- Many senior personnel from that force simply transitioned into the military or later security structures.
