# Balboa Airports Authority — Internal Notes

## Overview
- Government agency under the Ministry of Transport
- Established 1973 (Treaty of Areza)
- Owns and operates all 23 public airports in Balboa
- Also provides air traffic control (approach, aerodrome, en-route) via air navigation services division
- Balboa Area Control Centre for en-route ATC across Balboan airspace
- Headquartered in Sant Cristòfor

## Key Facts
- 23 airports (6 international, 17 domestic/regional)
- Total passengers: 52,297,000 (2025)
- Total cargo: ~660,000 tonnes (612K SCI + 48K PNU)
- Budget: ~$582 million (2024)
  - Aeronautical: ~$399M (68.6%) — landing fees, PFCs, aircraft parking
  - Non-aeronautical: ~$143M (24.6%) — retail, car parking, property leasing, cargo facility leasing
  - Air navigation charges: ~$40M (6.9%) — en-route charges (~$50/flight) + approach fees (~$30/flight)
  - No government subsidies — cross-subsidy model (SCI surplus funds small airports + ATC deficit)
  - SCI alone generates ~$357M (61% of total)
  - ATC operating cost: ~$69M/year; ATC revenue: ~$40M/year; ATC net deficit: ~$30M (covered by airport surplus)
- Employees: ~3,200 (including ~360 ATC staff)
- ICAO naming: IATA + "IB" suffix (e.g., SCI → SCIB)

## Governance
- Director-general appointed by Minister of Transport, confirmed by National Assembly
- 5-year term
- Revenue from airport charges (landing fees, parking, retail, concessions) + government appropriations
- Essential services subsidies for remote/low-traffic airports via Ministry of Transport

## History
- Pre-1973: airports owned by municipal governments, provincial administrations, AC mandate administration
- 1973: Treaty of Areza → BAA established, consolidating all public airports
- 1975-1985: Planned and built SCI (opened 15 March 1985)
- 1989-1992: Built PNU (opened 15 June 1992, in time for 1994 AFA World Cup)
- 2003: Terminal C at SCI (including Areza Concourse)
- 2008: Third runway (06/24) at SCI for cargo separation
