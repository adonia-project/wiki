# Primera Ordre de Balboa — Internal Notes

## Key facts
- Founded: c. 1967
- Banned: late 1970 (after Novara race riots)
- Destroyed: 1975–1976 (La Guàrdia Campaign)
- Founder/leader: Francesc Alemany i Bosch (1944–1976)
- Type: White nationalist terrorist organisation
- HQ: Altbirú (clandestine, 1971–1974), La Guàrdia comarca (clandestine, 1974–1976)
- Regions: Tramuntana, Jala, Nurra highlands

## Founder: Francesc Alemany i Bosch
- Born 1944
- Son of Lluís Alemany i Sala, wealthy Tramuntana planter
- Father's sugar plantation expropriated by BLRA in mid-1960s
- Father accepted compensation (government bonds), lived comfortably in Portblanc
- Family was "left pretty fine" — not impoverished by redistribution
- Francesc was radicalised as student at Universitat de Sant Bart
- Regarded father's acceptance of compensation as betrayal
- Motivation was ideological and racial, not economic
- Had access to family compensation funds — used to buy arms and fund POB
- Killed in siege of La Guàrdia headquarters, late 1975

## Father: Lluís Alemany i Sala
- Wealthy Tramuntana planter, 4-generation sugar plantation on savanna
- Expropriated by BLRA c. 1965
- Accepted compensation at assessed valuation
- Relocated to Portblanc, lived comfortably
- Did not participate in POB activities

## Timeline of activities

### Early period (c. 1967–1970)
- Political rallies in Portblanc (Tramuntana), Nurra, and smaller Tramuntana towns
- Attracted white planter community and white working class
- Alemany i Bosch was charismatic speaker
- Combined planter grievance with broader racial narrative
- Name "Primera Ordre" invoked colonial-era planter order
- Stockpiled weapons (hunting rifles, shotguns, some military-grade via constabulary contacts)
- Paramilitary training on private land in Tramuntana highlands

### 1970 Novara race riots
- Date: 1 July 1970
- Location: Novara, Nurra Province
- Triggered by fabricated story: false claim that a white woman was attacked and raped by a gang of Black men hiding in a Black neighbourhood
- Story published in Novara's main newspaper through POB connections with editorial staff
- Deliberately published on Emancipation Day (1 July) — anniversary of abolition of slavery in 1848
  - Black Balboans observe as holy day of rest, remembrance, church services
  - Black community at home for holiday, white community not observing
  - Timing chosen to maximise Black neighbourhood vulnerability and white mob mobilisation
- Story spread rapidly through white community
- POB used fabricated story as pretext for coordinated attacks
- POB members and sympathisers deployed from surrounding towns
- Targeted: Black-owned businesses, homes of Black families, integrated public schools
- Several days of violence, numerous injuries, significant property damage
- Investigation identified POB role
- POB banned by Balboan government, late 1970
- Forced organisation underground

### Emancipation Day (1 July)
- Anniversary of the abolition of slavery in Balboa, 1 July 1848
- Observed by Black Balboans as a holy day of rest, remembrance, and church services
- Not observed by the white community
- Families gather at home, attend church services, community feasts
- The POB exploited this day to publish the fabricated rape story that triggered the Novara race riots

### Underground period (1970–1974)
- Alemany i Bosch and leadership went underground
- Funding dwindling (donations from sympathetic planters + Alemany i Bosch's family funds)
- Turned to drug manufacturing to finance operations
- Relocated to highlands of Jala and Tramuntana
- First HQ: Altbirú, remote highland settlement in Jala Province
- Clashes with local Biru population over POB occupation of Biru territory
- Forced to relocate from Altbirú to La Guàrdia comarca (more remote)
- Drug distribution through criminal gangs in Sant Bart and Portblanc
- Drugs concealed in legitimate agricultural exports

### La Guàrdia and escalation (1974–1975)
- Reunification 1973 accelerated land redistribution in interior
- Siege of Murado Ranch (March 1974) perceived as declaration of war
- Campaign of violence targeting Biru and non-white Balboans in highlands:
  - Kidnapping and killing random Biru individuals
  - Killing non-white travellers
  - Driving through Biru communities terrorising residents
  - Stealing money and property
  - Ransom kidnappings for funding
  - Exact death toll unknown (many victims were remote highland residents, unreported)
- Planned "something big" — the Sant Bart University bombing

### Sant Bart University bombing (12 February 1975)
- Car bomb, ANFO, outside Faculty of Law, Universitat de Sant Bart
- 42 killed, 78 injured
- Deadliest terrorist attack in Balboan history
- Planned and authorised by Alemany i Bosch and senior leadership at La Guàrdia base
- Operatives: Joan Ballester i Font, Rafel Soler i Mas, Pere Vidal i Renom

### Assassination of deputy mayor of Sant Bart (May 1975)
- Bartomeu Riera i Solà, Black Balboan BPP politician
- Vocal advocate for Guarantees and land redistribution
- Shot outside his home in Sant Bart by POB operative
- Intended to demonstrate POB could reach prominent political figures
- Intimidate BPP officials in interior provinces

### La Guàrdia Campaign and destruction (1975–1976)
- Largest domestic military operation in Balboan history
- Named after La Guàrdia comarca
- Thousands of troops and police deployed
- Sustained incursions into highland areas around La Guàrdia
- Firefights and sieges with POB armed cells
- Alemany i Bosch and senior leadership killed in siege of La Guàrdia HQ, late 1975
- Remaining members arrested in raids through early 1976 or dispersed
- Some dispersed members later linked to independent drug operations
- Campaign also produced Biru special arms licenses → 1977 Aeroístmus hijacking

## Legacy
- Highland drug trade: established by POB, taken over by Biru groups then independent operators
- White nationalist ideological current persisted
- Segon Ordre de Balboa: modern group, formed 2000s, internet-based, no direct continuity
  - Loosely organised, geographically scattered
  - Online forums and social media
  - Online propaganda, occasional demonstrations, isolated violence
  - Identified as domestic extremist threat by security services
  - Decentralised structure makes monitoring difficult

- **Lluís Alemany i Sala** — father, wealthy planter, accepted compensation, did not participate
- **Joan Ballester i Font** — bombing operative, arrested 1975, convicted 1976
