# Lliure Moviment de Treballadors - Internal Notes

## Quick Reference
- **Name**: Lliure Moviment de Treballadors (LMT) — "Free Workers' Movement"
- **Period**: Mid-1870s – ? (survived beyond canal completion)
- **Location**: Canal corridor labour camps; Sant Cristòfor Zongosphere neighbourhoods
- **Membership**: No formal membership; network of autonomous circles (10–20 workers each)
- **Ideology**: Anarchist, syndicalist, workers' self-organisation, abolition of wage system, direct action, internationalism
- **Key event**: Association with assassination of Rafael Crespo i Soler (1880)

## Origins
- Emerged mid-1870s among Sinchewese workers in Balboa Canal construction camps
- Influenced by anarchist/syndicalist traditions from Zong Commonwealth (Jade Peninsula, Shendan Ocean network)
- Response to: harsh discipline by Volisanian engineering corps, displacement of local workers, low wages, no legal protections
- Name "Free Workers' Movement" reflects anarchist rejection of hierarchies (capitalist + union)

## Organisation
- **NOT a formal party** — loose network of autonomous circles
- No central leadership, no official platform, no membership cards
- Circles (10–20 workers) operate independently, connected through:
  - Circulation of publications
  - Personal contacts among workers moving between camps
  - Occasional meetings in Sant Cristòfor
- Decentralised structure made LMT hard to infiltrate/suppress

## Activities
1. **Discussion circles**: Read/debate publications, plan actions
2. **Mutual aid**: Financial support, medical care, assistance in disputes — essential in environment with no legal protections
3. **Strike organisation**: Several strikes in late 1870s (higher wages, shorter hours, safety); typically broken by Volisanian security
4. **Publications**: Pamphlets/manuscripts on portable presses; mixture of Balboan Creole + Zong; distributed clandestinely

## Ideology (Core Principles)
1. **Workers' self-organisation**: Autonomous circles, direct democracy; reject hierarchical unions
2. **Abolition of wage system**: Wage labour = bondage differing from slavery only in degree; workers should collectively own/manage enterprises
3. **Direct action**: Reject parliamentary politics; change through strikes, sabotage, confrontation
4. **Internationalism**: Solidarity across national/racial lines (Fosian + Black Balboan + mixed-race workers share common interest in overthrowing oligarchy)

## Publications
- Produced on portable presses in canal camps
- Mixture of Balboan Creole + Zong
- Circulated clandestinely
- Frequently denounced Crespo i Soler + Expansió Party as representatives of commercial class
- Rhetoric often violent (destruction of existing order, workers' republic)
- NOT统一 on all points — different circles emphasised different aspects; debate on strategy/tactics

## Relationship to Assassination (14 March 1880)
- **Tàn Hui-chye** was participant in LMT discussion circles; influenced by publications
- **Investigation conclusion**: Tàn acted on own initiative; LMT did NOT plan/authorise assassination
- No evidence of broader conspiracy; handgun traced to black-market dealer; no correspondence/documentation linking Tàn to plot
- LMT ideology inspired Tàn but did not direct him

## Suppression & Survival
- Post-assassination crackdown: increased constabulary surveillance, some circles dispersed, some workers detained
- Decentralised structure made LMT hard to suppress — dispersed circles re-formed in new locations
- Activities shifted away from strike organising (attracted attention) → mutual aid + discussion (more discreet)
- Survived remainder of canal construction + political upheavals
- Publications continued to circulate at reduced volume
- Ideas persisted among Fosian workforce in canal zone

## Black Balboan Collaboration
- Late 1870s onward: LMT established contacts with Black Balboan left-wing groups
- Black Balboan groups: organised around churches, mutual aid societies, sharecropper networks; traditions of resistance rooted in slavery/debt peonage/exclusion
- Points of contact: Sant Cristòfor circles (Black Balboan workers in warehousing/provisioning trades)
- LMT publications in Zong + Balboan Creole accessible to literate Black Balboan workers
- Forms of collaboration:
  - LMT mutual aid extended to Black Balboan workers injured/dismissed from port facilities
  - Black Balboan groups provided intelligence about constabulary activities in interior
  - LMT publications circulated among Black Balboan groups
  - Black Balboan oral traditions (work songs, sermons, resistance stories) adapted into LMT repertoire
- Limitations: racial/social barriers; planter elite actively discouraged cross-racial contact; canal camps segregated; constabulary monitored inter-racial meetings; Velásquez anti-Fosian policies designed partly to prevent Fosian-Black alliances
- Collaboration remained informal/localised rather than systematic
- Contacts maintained (attenuated) through Velásquez era + Second War of the Isthmus
- Revived in Balboa Boba + Zong Balboa periods

## Velásquez Era Repression
- Sant Bart's Coup (1888) → new phase of repression
- LMT characterised as "Zong-aligned subversive organisation" (inaccurate but politically convenient)
- LMT's anarchist ideology conflated with loyalty to Kingdom of Zong
- Anti-leftwing policies: any organisation challenging planter elite targeted
- Publications banned; possession of LMT materials = criminal offence
- Constabulary raids on meeting places in Sant Cristòfor Zongosphere neighbourhoods
- Workers detained, deported, conscripted into labour battalions
- Mutual aid networks disrupted; financial contributions interrupted
- LMT forced into hibernation: public activities ceased, publications intermittent, organisational capacity severely diminished
- Ideas continued to circulate in manuscript form; remaining circles maintained contact through personal networks

## Second War of the Isthmus & Dissolution
- War (1893–1899) scattered remaining circles
- Fosian workers conscripted, forcibly relocated, detained on suspicion of disloyalty
- Sant Cristòfor Zongosphere neighbourhoods (LMT's base) disrupted → Zong military occupation 1893
- Participants: fled to Zong-controlled territory, conscripted, detained, or disappeared into broader population
- Publications ceased entirely; mutual aid networks broken
- By Treaty of Sant Llàtzer (1899), LMT as organised force had effectively ceased to exist
- Ideas survived: publications preserved + circulated clandestinely → theoretical foundation for later left-wing organisations

## Legacy
- One of earliest left-wing workers' organisations on Isthmus of Balboa
- First systematic working-class critique of planter-commercial oligarchy
- Organisational practices (workers' circles) replicated by later organisations
- Publications rediscovered + reprinted in early 20th century → historical precedent for Balboan left
- Internationalism → model for coalition politics in 20th-century Balboan labour movement
- Multi-racial character of Balboan left traced to LMT's insistence on Fosian-Black solidarity
- One of the origins of left-wing movements during Balboa Boba + Zong Balboa eras
