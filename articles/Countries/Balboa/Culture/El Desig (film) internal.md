# El Desig (film) — Internal Notes

## Article location
`articles/Countries/Balboa/Culture/El Desig (film).mediawiki`

## Established facts (from existing wiki)
- Title: *El Desig* ("The Desire"), 1987
- Director: Montserrat Riera i Sola (born 1948, Sant Bart; moved to Sant Cristòfor early 1970s; former Cinema Lliure scriptwriter)
- Second film (first was *Les Dones del Port*, 1981, a documentary)
- Produced outside mainstream studio system
- Financed through personal funds + small contributions from feminist organisations and individual supporters
- Depicted sexual and economic lives of three women in Sant Cristòfor during the real-estate bubble of the 1980s
- Content: female desire, same-sex attraction, abortion
- First frank portrayal of female sexuality in a Balboan film
- Screened at Sant Cristòfor Cultural Centre in late 1987 (~200 attendees)
- One actor was a Portblanc resident (established jurisdiction for re-filing)
- Triggered prosecution under Public Decency Act (1976) — first prosecution under the Act
- Film preserved at Arxiu Nacional de Cinema de Balboa (added 1995)
- UAC retrospective for Riera i Sola in 2002

### Production details (invented)
- Screenplay written 1985–1986 during peak of real-estate bubble
- Filmed over 6 weeks in early 1987
- 16mm film, natural light, borrowed camera
- Locations: actual harbour district bars, financial district offices, residential apartments
- Edited by Riera i Sola on borrowed editing suite, spring–summer 1987
- Runtime: ~82 minutes
- No studio, no distributor, no budget figure
- Final demolition sequence filmed at actual demolition site in harbour district

### Plot structure
- Three interlocking stories, each centred on one woman
- Women's lives intersect through harbour district and financial district
- Brief, often wordless encounters (shared bus ride, glance in building lobby, conversation in bar)
- Stories run concurrently over ~one year
- Final sequence: Joana's bar demolished; all three women present as spectators; no resolution
- Final shot: empty lot, construction equipment moving in

### Key themes
- Female desire as inseparable from economic precarity
- Reproductive autonomy dependent on economic position
- Displacement of working-class communities by bubble-era development
- Sexuality of older women (challenging ageist assumptions)
- Economic complicity (Rosa works for the real-estate company causing displacement)
- Interconnectedness of women's economic and sexual lives under the bubble economy

### Reception (invented)
- Pre-trial: ~200 viewers; reviewed by Eulàlia Camps i Verdaguer in El Faro del Istmo (positive)
- During trial: focus shifted to constitutional questions, not film's artistic merits
- Post-trial: circulated through underground screening circuit (community centres, feminist meetings, university campuses)
- Never received mainstream distribution
- Directly influenced *Els Amagats* (1989)
- 2002 UAC retrospective included first mainstream cinema screening of the film
