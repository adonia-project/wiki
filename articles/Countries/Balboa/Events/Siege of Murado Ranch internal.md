# Siege of Murado Ranch — Internal Notes

## Key facts
- Date: 11–29 March 1974 (18-day siege)
- Location: Murado Ranch, Tramuntana savanna, Tramuntana Province
- Expropriation order served: 5 March 1974
- Military deployment ordered: 9 March 1974
- Military arrived: 11 March 1974
- Surrender: 29 March 1974
- Family departed: 28 April 1974
- Casualties: 0 (ended without bloodshed)

## Key people
- **Bernat Murado i Sala** — head of the Murado family, great-grandson of ranch founder, former provincial deputy in rump state legislature, outspoken opponent of the Guarantees, relocated to Sant Bart after siege, died 1989
- **Miquel Torrent i Alvarado** — President, ordered military deployment
- **Captain Jordi Vilanova i Reig** — military commander at the siege, ordered to secure compound and enforce expropriation without force if possible
- **Local priest** — one of the mediators during negotiations (unnamed)
- **BLRA official** — co-mediator during negotiations (unnamed)

## The Murado family
- One of the oldest planter dynasties in Tramuntana, roots in colonial period
- Great-grandson of ranch founder was head at time of siege
- Large cattle and sugar plantation on Tramuntana savanna
- Built on enslaved labour, maintained through sharecropping and debt peonage after 1848 abolition
- Several hundred workers (predominantly Black Balboans) living on property
- Conditions at time of reunification differed little from pre-Guarantees era
- Bernat Murado i Sala was prominent in Tramuntana white political establishment
- Had served as provincial deputy in rump state legislature
- Publicly stated he would not surrender the ranch to the BLRA

## The militia
- Approximately 30 armed men
- Drawn from remnants of planter militias from rump state period
- Primarily relatives, neighbours, political allies of Murado family
- Armed with rifles, shotguns, agricultural tools
- Barricaded main compound (family residence, sugar processing facility, workers' quarters)
- Barricades made from farm equipment, timber, vehicles
- Some militia members later linked to Primera Ordre de Balboa and highland drug trade

## The military
- Company of approximately 200 soldiers
- From Balboan Army's Sant Bart garrison
- Commander: Captain Jordi Vilanova i Reig
- Orders: secure compound, enforce expropriation, avoid force if possible
- Established perimeter around compound
- First use of military for domestic enforcement — unprecedented (rump state armed forces restricted to constabulary under Treaty of Sant Llàtzer)

## The BLRA process
- Expropriation initiated late 1973, shortly after reunification
- Process: formal assessment, valuation, expropriation order, compensation offer
- BLRA valuation substantially below family's own assessment
- Compensation payable in government bonds, not cash, subject to lengthy claims process
- Among first cases processed in Tramuntana after reunification

## Negotiations
- Began 14 March, mediated by local priest and BLRA official
- Murado demands: military withdrawal, annulment of expropriation order, no criminal charges
- BLRA position: expropriation lawful and non-negotiable, willing to negotiate terms of departure, compensation, treatment of militia
- Resolution factors: family realisation military would not withdraw, political allies' pressure, BLRA concessions

## Terms of surrender
- Family departure within 30 days
- Compensation at BLRA valuation, with independent appraisal review
- No criminal charges against militia members, provided weapons surrendered and dispersed

## Aftermath
- BLRA took possession 28 April 1974
- Ranch reorganised as state-owned agricultural enterprise
- Later incorporated into Corporació Agrícola de l'Istme (CAI) in 1976
- Cattle operations continued under CAI management
- Sugar processing facility modernised in early 1980s
- Subsequent expropriations in Tramuntana and other interior provinces proceeded without comparable armed resistance
- Planter class continued resistance through legal and political channels

## Escalation of violence
- Sant Bart University bombing (1975) — POB retaliation, 42 killed
- La Guàrdia Campaign (1975–1976) — largest domestic military operation
- Bernat Murado i Sala did not participate in violence
- Some militia members later linked to POB and highland drug trade

## Political significance
- First armed challenge to Twelve Guarantees after reunification
- First federal military deployment to enforce expropriation in interior provinces
- Established precedent: federal government will use military force to enforce land redistribution
- Symbol for Black/mixed Balboans: government commitment to Guarantees
- Symbol for white Balboans: federal overreach, dispossession of planter class
- BPP and successors mark anniversary as day of remembrance for land reform
- White nationalist groups use Murado resistance as symbol of opposition to post-reunification order

## Key dates
- Late 1973 — BLRA initiates expropriation process
- 5 March 1974 — Expropriation order served
- 9 March 1974 — Military deployment ordered
- 11 March 1974 — Military arrives, siege begins
- 14 March 1974 — Negotiations begin
- 29 March 1974 — Murado family surrenders
- 28 April 1974 — Family departs, BLRA takes possession
- 1975 — Sant Bart University bombing (POB retaliation)
- 1975–1976 — La Guàrdia Campaign
