# Assassination of Rafael Crespo i Soler - Internal Notes

## Quick Reference
- **Date**: 14 March 1880
- **Location**: Sant Cristòfor port
- **Victim**: Rafael Crespo i Soler (president of Balboa)
- **Assassin**: Tàn Hui-chye (Sinchewese canal worker, member of Lliure Moviment de Treballadors)
- **Weapon**: Handgun
- **Outcome**: Tàn executed 28 April 1880; Muntaner i Rius succeeded as president

## Key Entities

### Rafael Crespo i Soler
- President 1868–1880
- Sant Cristòfor merchant; first non-planter president
- Championed canal project; secured Volisanian financing
- Re-elected 1872, 1876
- Represented commercial class of Kendriyan coastal cities

### Tàn Hui-chye
- Sinchewese canal worker (Zong name, Catalanized spelling)
- Member of Lliure Moviment de Treballadors
- Radicalised during labour disputes in canal project's final phase
- Acted alone (investigation found no broader conspiracy)
- Executed 28 April 1880

### Lliure Moviment de Treballadors
- "Free Workers' Movement" — one of earliest left-wing workers' organisations on isthmus
- Emerged mid-1870s among Fosian workforce in canal camps
- Anarchist/syndicalist traditions from Sinchew/Zong Commonwealth
- Not a formal party — loose network of workers' circles, discussion groups, mutual aid societies
- Operated clandestinely in canal camps + Zongosphere neighbourhoods of Sant Cristòfor
- Advocated workers' self-organisation, abolition of wage system, overthrow of planter-commercial oligarchy
- Did NOT plan/authorise assassination (Tàn acted on own initiative)
- Subjected to increased surveillance after assassination; some circles dispersed
- Continued at reduced level of activity

## Social Context
- Canal construction brought substantial Fosian workforce to isthmus
- Local workers (Black/mixed Balboans) displaced from construction jobs by Fosian recruits accepting lower wages
- Wealth concentrated in Sant Cristòfor commercial class (port fees, warehousing, financial services)
- Fosian workers housed in overcrowded barracks; harsh discipline by Volisanian engineering corps
- Constabulary focused on Black population in interior — paid little attention to Fosian labour organising

## Assassination Details
- **Event**: Crespo i Soler inspecting newly completed warehousing facilities at port
- **Security**: Minimal — Balboan political establishment not accustomed to political violence
- **Method**: Tàn approached at close range; single shot to chest
- **Statement**: Tàn declared he acted "for the workers"; called Crespo i Soler a tyrant who grew rich from their labour
- **Immediate aftermath**: Tàn seized by crowd + constabulary; made no attempt to flee

## Investigation & Trial
- Conducted by Balboan constabulary + Volisanian military intelligence
- Sought to determine if broader conspiracy (Lliure Moviment or foreign powers)
- **Findings**: Tàn was member of Lliure Moviment; no evidence of organised plot; handgun traced to black-market dealer in Sant Cristòfor docks; no correspondence/documentation linking Tàn to broader conspiracy
- **Conclusion**: Tàn acted on own initiative, inspired by Lliure Moviment ideology but not directed by organisation

### Trial Controversies
- Military tribunal convened within 2 weeks; concluded in 3 days
- Defence counsel had limited opportunity to interview witnesses/examine evidence
- Prosecution relied on testimony from detained witnesses (later recanted; alleged beatings/deprivation)
- Convicted 20 April 1880: murder + treason
- ''El Transístmico'' editorial questioned fairness; no official response

## Succession
- Carles Muntaner i Rius (vice president) succeeded automatically
- Civil engineer; closely involved in canal project
- Oversaw canal completion 1885
- Presidency marked by political conflicts → Sant Bart's Coup 1888

## Open Questions

- What happened to the Lliure Moviment in later decades?
- Did the assassination affect the canal's completion timeline or politics?
- Were there other assassination attempts on Balboan presidents?
