# Unification generation (Balboa) — Internal Notes

## Concept
- Demographic cohort: born 1968–1982
- First generation to grow up entirely in reunified Balboa (post-1973 Treaty of Areza)
- Named after the TV series Generació Unitat (1999, BalboaVisió)
- Term subsequently adopted in Balboan sociological/cultural commentary

## Key formative events (by age during event)
- 1973 reunification: oldest members were 5, youngest not born yet
- 1974 Siege of Murado Ranch: ages -8 to 6
- 1975 Sant Bart University bombing: ages -7 to 7
- 1975–76 La Guàrdia Campaign: ages -7 to 8
- 1978–87 real-estate bubble: ages -4 to 19
- 1982 Dàrsena steel mill closes: ages 0 to 14
- 1982–84 Portblanc school crisis: ages 0 to 16
- 1980s Hill Wars: childhood/adolescence throughout
- 1985–91 asset crisis: ages 3 to 23
- 1988 El Desig trial: ages 6 to 20
- 1991 Tân Hông-un convicted: ages 9 to 23
- 1992 Grup Financer Central acquires Banc Balboa: ages 10 to 24
- 1993 First Pride march: ages 11 to 25
- 1994 spot recession: ages 12 to 26
- 1998 BTPMEU established: ages 16 to 30
- 1998 BalboaVisió founded: ages 16 to 30
- 1999 Generació Unitat TV series premieres: ages 17 to 31
- 2020 Adonian pandemic: ages 38 to 52

## Key characteristics (discussed with user, approved)
1. No memory of pre-reunification division — unified Balboa is the default
2. Multiculturalism as default, not policy — code-switching Volisanian/Creole is natural
3. Gilded Age cinema as formative pop culture
4. Economic cynicism — witnessed bubble inflate and collapse
5. Racial integration as lived experience, not ideology
6. Political disillusionment with BPP — internal divisions, corruption scandals
7. Urban identity — Sant Cristòfor, canal corridor, post-industrial city
8. Generació Unitat TV show validated their generational identity
9. Bridge generation between analog and digital
10. By 2026: professional/cultural establishment, ages 44–58
