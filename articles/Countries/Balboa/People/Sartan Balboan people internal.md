## Key design decisions
- No colonial settler population (unlike Volisanian Balboans). Sarta's colonial ambitions were directed toward Guajicutea, not Balboa.
- Main waves: civil war refugees + Redilution farmers. The user specified "most came during the sartan civil war time and the redilution period."
- Guajicutea-originated Sartans: small wave (~1,000) from Guajicutea after the Virtuous Revolution (1824) displaced the Sartan privileged class established under the Northern Confederation occupation's race codes. Culturally distinct — descendants of Sartan colonial community in Guajicutea (1609–1723), shaped by Sartan-Guaji cultural fusion.
- Sartisanian language: Sartan-Volisanian contact language spoken by a minority (<5% of Sartan Balboans), emerged in 19th century in urban centres. Guajicutea-originated Sartans contributed to its linguistic substrate (brought elements of Sartan-Guaji contact language).
- Sartan language largely lost due to assimilation into Volisanian-speaking majority. Survives in family names, religious terminology, folk songs.
- Religion: Petran Catholic (shared with Volisanian Balboans), but with distinct Sartan liturgical customs/devotional practices.
- Class division: civil war-era urban community (commerce/professions) vs. Redilution-era farming community (northern provinces, small-scale agriculture). Guajicutea-originated community is a third, culturally distinct urban sub-community.
- Redilution-era community most affected by post-reunification land redistribution.
