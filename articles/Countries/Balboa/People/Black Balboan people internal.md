# Black Balboan People — Internal Notes

## Quick Reference
- Population: 12,684,000 (36% of national)
- Volisanian: Negre Balboà (m), Negra Balboà (f), plural Negres Balboans
- Creole: Nwa Balboyen (m), Nway Balboyen (f), plural Nwayen Balboyen
- Alternative: Kafto-Balboan (preferred in rest of Abyala, less common in Balboa itself)
- Core constituency of BPP

## Nomenclature Key Points
- **Kafto/Kafto-Balboan**: Pan-Abyalan term, emphasises Kaftian heritage, used in other Abyalan countries, used in Balboa mainly in academic/international contexts
- **Black Balboan (Negre Balboà)**: The standard self-designation within Balboa; reclaimed from rump-state term of subordination; used in Twelve Guarantees official text; gained prominence during No Return movement
- **Historical**: esclau (slave) → negre lliure (free Black) → negre (rump state racial category) → negre balboà (reclaimed)
- **Reclamation**: Followed pattern of other post-slavery societies; BPP founding documents used negre balboà; contested by some who preferred Kafto

## Socioeconomic Data
- National median: $6,500 (≈ national median of $6,700)
- Estret median: $13,200 (close to provincial $15,400)
- Outside Estret median: $4,200
- Cooperative farm system: land security but low income growth; central political debate

## History Summary
1. **Origins**: Enslaved from Burawa, Dakare, Galwa, Sarfone regions via trans-Kaftian slave trade
2. **Colonial slavery**: Sugar, coffee, banana plantations; not self-sustaining; depended on continued importation
3. **Emancipation (1848)**: Second-to-last Volisanian colony; debt peonage continued
4. **Rump state**: Redilution/Birthing programmes; racial legislation; excluded from power
5. **Zong Balboa**: Lîm Bûn-cheng's anti-discrimination laws (1912-1918); benefits concentrated in canal corridor
6. **GAW**: Disproportionate losses; siege of Sant Cristòfor
7. **Mandate (Second Star)**: Black capitalist class; cultural flowering; No Return movement
8. **Post-reunification**: Guarantees, BPP, land reform, reparations; persistent geographic disparities

## Cultural Elements
- **Language**: Balboan Creole (primary); Volisanian (formal); Kaftian languages largely lost
- **Religion**: Catholicism (majority, with syncretic elements); Protestantism (growing); indigenous/syncretic traditions (rural)
- **Music**: istmo son genre (mandate period, corridor); hip hop (Sant Bart, late 1980s/early 1990s, interior)
- **Hip hop**: Emerged from Sant Bart's working-class Black neighbourhoods; Creole-language lyrics; critique of post-reunification order; Sant Bart Collective first recordings 1991; most popular among young Black Balboans by 2000s
- **Literature**: Elena Vargas, Raúl Mendez; Hái-khiⁿ journal (1956); La Voz del Istmo (1953)
- **Cuisine**: Rice/beans, plantains, cassava, seafood; Kaftian spice blends; Fosian/Volisanian influences in urban areas

## Cultural Divide: Corridor vs Interior
The 74-year separation (1899-1973) created two distinct Black Balboan subcultures:
- **Corridor** (Sant Cristòfor, Portnou, Estret): Zong anti-discrimination law; mandate egalitarianism; multilingual (Volisanian/Creole/Zong); istmo son; syncretic Catholicism; stronger BPP alignment; views reunification as culmination of civil rights struggle
- **Interior** (Sant Bart, Costabella, highlands): Rump state racial legislation; planter oligarchy; predominantly Volisanian-speaking; hip hop + folk traditions; traditional Catholicism; more willing to support Socialist Front/Democratic Alliance; views reunification with gratitude + scepticism
