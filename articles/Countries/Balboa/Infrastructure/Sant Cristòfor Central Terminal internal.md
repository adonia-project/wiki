# Sant Cristòfor Central Terminal — Internal Notes

## Key Facts
- 30 at-grade tracks served by 20 platforms (mix of side and island platforms; rail services only: CristoCarril, long-distance, HSR)
- 4 underground terminating platforms (Metro Lines 1, 2, 4 — west/east alignment, upper underground level)
  - Lines 1 and 2 use tracks 5 and 6 (confirmed by Line 1 extension article)
  - Line 4 uses remaining 2 tracks on upper level
- 4 underground through platforms with passing tracks (Metro Lines 3, 5, 7 — north/south alignment, deeper underground level)
- Total: 28 platforms (20 at-grade + 8 underground), 38 tracks (30 at-grade + 8 underground)
- Built 1906–1911, opened 16 February 1911
- Same site as original 1868–1872 Isthmus Railway terminus
- Architecture: Volisanian colonial (arcades, ironwork, ornate facades) + Zong imperial (upturned eaves, decorative tilework, courtyard concepts)

## Station Names
- Rail station: "Sant Cristòfor Central Terminal" (English) / "Estació Terminal Central de Sant Cristòfor" (Volisanian)
- Metro station: also "Sant Cristòfor Central Terminal" (user corrected 2026-09-02 — previously "Estació Terminal Central" was used in metro context, but user said this is incorrect)
- Both refer to the same station complex

## Governors
- Commissioned by: Tan Tek-chiu (陳德秋), Governor 1899–1910 (military officer)
- Opened under: Lîm Bûn-cheng (林文正), Governor 1910–1925 (civilian reformer)

## Underground Station Box Structure
From the Line 1 extension to Barcelona article:
- Upper level: Lines 1 and 2 tracks (5 and 6) — terminating, west/east
- Deeper level: Lines 3 and 5 — through, north/south
- Line 4 added to upper level in 1968
- Line 7 added to deeper level in 1990s
- Construction: upper level = cut-and-cover (shallow depth); deeper level = bored tunnels (greater depth)
- Line 1 extension requires threading through layered infrastructure without disrupting lower lines

## Metro Line History
- Lines 1, 2: Shining Star era (Zong Balboa), first metro lines, originate under terminal, run eastward
- Line 3: Planned before GAW, halted by war, completed 1964 (north-south connector)
- Line 4: 1968 (east to suburbs)
- Line 5: 1971 (northern bay shore to peninsula)
- Line 7: 1990s (outer ciutats expansion)

## CristoCarril Services
- Lines B, C, D, E (documented with articles)
- Lines F, G (mentioned in city article, no articles yet)
- All share first 5 stations south: Central Terminal → Portal de Sant Cristòfor → Sant Pere → La Font → Mas Blanc
- Line B diverges at La Font to Portnou Line corridor
- Line C continues on Varakí Line corridor
- Line D diverges at Mas Blanc to Old Grand Subdivision
- Line E continues on Sant Bart Line

## Long-Distance Rail
- 6 destinations: Sant Bart, Haicang, Bellver, Sant Llàtzer, Campdària, Portblanc
- Connects to every province

## HSR
- Sant Cristòfor–Portnou HSR line (opened 11 March 2009, 99 km, 250 km/h)
- Sant Cristòfor–Areza HSR line (opened 2008)
- Common trunk: Central Terminal → Varakí TDV (57.4 km)
- Portnou HSR serves Portal de Sant Cristòfor during events at Estadi de Balboa

## Historical Events
- 1866: Escàndol Ferroviari (Isthmus Railway Company + Zong shipping bribed National Party)
- 1868–1872: Original station built (western terminus of Isthmus Railway)
- 1886: Mayor Joan Ballester i Forteza assassinated on steps of original terminal
- 1899: Treaty of Sant Llàtzer — Zong takes over Sant Cristòfor
- 1906–1911: Terminal reconstructed (Tan Tek-chiu commissioned, Lîm Bûn-cheng opened)
- 1935–1947: GAW siege — terminal damaged, underground metro used as shelter
- 1953: Restoration under Mayor Elena Vargas i Aldana
- 1960: Sant Cristòfor returned to Balboa
- 2008: Sant Cristòfor–Areza HSR opens
- 2009: Sant Cristòfor–Portnou HSR opens
- 2026: Line 1 extension to Barcelona under construction (modifications to station box)
