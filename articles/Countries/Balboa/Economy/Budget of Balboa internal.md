### 4. Land Value Tax (PROPOSED — pending confirmation)

**National land value tax, established 1993 — transitioned from property transaction taxes to LVT**

**Context:** The 1978–1987 real estate bubble and crash was driven by land speculation. The post-bubble government initially relied on property transaction taxes and stamp duties, but these were volatile (surging during booms, collapsing during busts). In 1993, the government transitioned to a land value tax (LVT) — taxing the unimproved value of land annually rather than taxing transactions. This shifted the tax base from volatile transaction volumes to stable land values and discouraged speculative land holding.

**Structure:**
- National tax — revenue collected centrally, distributed to provinces/municipalities through the budget process
- Provinces and municipalities may levy additional local taxes, but the majority of their funding comes from central state transfers
- Tax base: unimproved land value (cadastral assessment, excluding buildings/improvements)
- Valuations updated periodically

**Zoned rate structure:**

| Zone | Share of Land Value | Rate | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sant Cristòfor / Estret corridor | 40% ($204B) | 2.5% | $5.1B |
| Other urban (Sant Bart, etc.) | 20% ($102B) | 1.5% | $1.53B |
| Agricultural (cooperatives) | 25% ($128B) | 0.5% | $0.64B |
| Rural/interior (Tramuntana, Migjorn) | 10% ($51B) | 0.3% | $0.15B |
| Exempt (Birú communal lands, govt, parks) | 5% ($26B) | 0% | $0 |
| **Total** | **100% ($510B)** | | **~$7.4B** |

**Total estimated land value: ~$510B** (60% of total property value of ~$850B, which is ~250% of GDP)

**Political fit:**
- BPP/Socialist Front: support LVT as anti-speculation, progressive
- Democratic Alliance: oppose (represents property-owning class in Sant Cristòfor)
- Tramuntana Regionalist Party: benefits (low rates for interior)
- Cooperative agricultural land at reduced rate (0.5%) — political commitment to cooperative farm system
- Birú communal lands exempt — land-rights settlements

**Historical note:** The 1993 transition from property transaction taxes to LVT fully replaced the old bubble-era federal revenue system. Federal property transaction taxes and stamp duties were abolished; the LVT is the sole federal property-related tax. Provinces may levy their own property transfer taxes as a local revenue source, but there is no federal transfer tax. A nominal recording fee for property transfers exists at the provincial level for administrative cost recovery.

**Revenue: ~$7.4B/year** (2.2% of GDP)

| **TOTAL ABOVE THRESHOLD** | **4,005,000** | **600,750** | **3,404,250** | | | **$13.93B** |

**Key assumptions behind the estimates:**
- Total employed: ~17.8M (35M population, ~55% labor force participation, 7.4% unemployment)
- Total labor income: ~$150B (~44% of $340B nominal GDP)
- Bottom 40% of earners receive 12% of income → 7.12M earners, avg $2,528 (all exempt)
- Middle 50% of earners receive 46% of income → 8.9M earners, avg $7,753 (mostly exempt)
- Top 10% of earners receive 42% of income → 1.78M earners, avg $35,393 (spread across all taxable brackets)
- 15% nonpay rate above the threshold — mostly informal-sector, self-employed, and undeclared cooperative income

**Interpretation (UPDATED for $6,000 threshold):**
- **54.2% of employed earners are exempt** because they earn below $6,000
- **38.9% of the workforce** actually pays income tax (~6.9M people)
- The top bracket ($100K+, 0.85% of workforce) generates **42% of all income tax revenue** (~$7.1B of $16.8B)
- The tax base is broader than under the $12K threshold but still concentrated in the Estret corridor / Sant Cristòfor financial sector
- **Effective income tax revenue: ~$16.8 billion** (about 4.9% of nominal GDP)
- Revenue increase from threshold change: +$2.92B ($13.9B → $16.8B)
- Existing payers pay ~$600 more (10% on $6,000–$12,000 = $600 additional)
- ~3.5M new payers at ~$250 average (10% on income between $6K–$12K)

**Who actually pays:**
- **Exempt**: agricultural workers, informal sector, subsistence farmers, low-wage services, most of Tramuntana/Migjorn/Nurra
- **$12K–$20K**: Estret working class, junior clerks, logistics workers, lower-level government employees
- **$20K–$35K**: mid-level professionals, teachers, nurses, skilled industrial workers at Badia Curta
- **$35K–$60K**: senior professionals, engineers, managers, mid-level finance
- **$60K–$100K**: senior finance professionals, executives, lawyers, doctors
- **$100K+**: top financial sector, C-suite executives, large business owners, investment fund partners

**Open questions:**
- Is the $12,000 threshold per individual filer or per household?
- Capital gains treatment? Is financial-sector income taxed as ordinary income or separately?
- Are there special exemptions for cooperative farm income, Birú communities, reparations recipients?
- Is the Sant Cristòfor Free Trade Zone personal income taxed at a reduced rate too?

- Excise rate: $0.35/litre
- Revenue: ~$3.5B/year
- Balboa has domestic refining (CIEA Badia Curta); excise applied at refinery gate / import point

**Alcohol excise:**
- Annual consumption: ~210M litres pure alcohol (6L per capita)
- Beer (60%): 126M L @ $0.50/L = $63M
- Spirits (30%): 63M L @ $5.00/L = $315M
- Wine (10%): 21M L @ $2.00/L = $42M
- Revenue: ~$420M/year

**Tobacco excise ($5/pack sin tax, introduced 2014):**
- Excise: $5.00/pack of 20 = $0.25/cigarette
- Pre-tax pack price: ~$4.00; post-tax: ~$9.00
- 2014: Smoking rate ~20%, ~4.4M smokers, ~1,100 cig/smoker → peak revenue ~$1.2B
- 2026: Smoking rate declined to ~7%, ~1.5M smokers, ~400 cig/smoker → revenue ~$154M
- Revenue has declined ~87% from peak as sin tax successfully reduced smoking
- 2026 revenue: ~$154M

**Sugar-sweetened beverage tax:**
- Annual consumption: ~2.8B litres (80L per capita)
- Tax rate: $0.10/litre
- Revenue: ~$280M/year
- Politically contentious: Balboa has powerful sugar lobby (Hirocorp, CAI, cooperative farms) + Coccola bottling

**Total excise revenue: ~$4.4B/year**

## Key Observations

1. **Taxation is the primary revenue source** — income tax ($16.8B) + VAT ($8.31B) + LVT ($7.4B) + corporate tax ($6.4B) + tariffs ($6.0B) + excise ($4.4B) = ~$49.3B, dwarfing canal revenue ($6.5B, mostly earmarked).

2. **The tax base is narrow** — only 38.9% of the workforce pays income tax (~6.9M people). The top income bracket ($100K+, 0.85% of workforce) generates 42% of income tax revenue. The fiscal system is heavily dependent on the Estret corridor and Sant Cristòfor financial sector.

3. **Canal revenue is fully allocated** — negative tariff ($1.1B) + agricultural supports ($1.2B) + canal O&M ($1.5B) + FIDB ($0.3B) + other ($0.4B) + debt service redirect ($2.0B) = $6.5B. Canal revenue now contributes directly to debt service, reducing borrowing needs.

4. **The "low taxation" financial hub paradox** — Sant Cristòfor's financial sector pays a low effective corporate rate (~14%), yet the government needs substantial tax revenue. The tax burden falls disproportionately on domestic manufacturing, petroleum, and the narrow income tax base.

5. **Education is the largest expenditure** at ~$14.8B (revised from $20.8B) — ~4.4% of GDP. Revised using provincial salary multipliers (teacher base $10K × provincial multiplier) and university endowment offsets ($352M/year from $7B endowment pool). Original $20.8B figure in wiki article needs updating.

6. **Healthcare (NHS) is the second largest expenditure** at ~$13.6B — ~4% of GDP. Beveridge model: government-owned hospitals, government-employed healthcare workers, free at point of use, universal coverage. 238,000 healthcare workers with 3-tier salary system (provincial scale + global rate for super-specialists).

7. **Provincial salary multiplier framework** — applies to ALL public sector employees (education, healthcare, police, civil service, etc.):
   - Estret 1.40x | Jala 1.10x | Migjorn 0.90x | Nurra 0.85x | Tramuntana 0.80x
   - Super-specialists (doctors, nurses) paid at global rates ($100K–$350K doctors, $60K–$100K nurses) — no provincial multiplier

8. **Total general fund revenue: ~$58.1B** — total spending: education ($14.8B) + healthcare ($13.6B) + BDF ($2.4B) + police ($5.1B) + civil service ($5.1B) + provincial transfers ($2.0B) + social welfare ($1.6B) + canal earmarks ($4.5B, excluding debt service redirect) + debt service ($5.3B, of which $2.0B from canal + $3.3B from borrowing) + infrastructure ($3.0B) + contingency ($1.5B) + ministry discretionary programs ($1.4B, 20 line items) + SOE subsidies ($285M) + AC membership dues ($100M) + hurricane fund gov contribution ($150M) + R&D/AC ($0.5B) = ~$61.5B. Annual deficit: ~$3.4B, financed by borrowing ($6.5B annual capacity). Debt-to-GDP: 35% ($119B), debt service 9.1% of revenue (comfortable).
