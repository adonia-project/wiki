# Península del Verger — Internal Notes

## Overview
Dedicated article for the flat, blocky limestone peninsula in northeastern Migjorn Province. Created 2026-09-02.

## Key Concepts

### Geology
- Uplifted Miocene coral reef limestone platform
- Raised by tectonic compression during isthmus formation (continental plates closing ancient seaway)
- Flat surface = preserved reef platform (reefs grow flat in shallow water)
- Blocky outline = harder limestone resisted erosion, softer surrounding sediments worn away
- Mature karst landscape: underground drainage, dolines, caves, no surface rivers

### The Water Paradox
- High rainfall (1,500–2,500 mm/year) but dry surface
- Rain infiltrates limestone immediately → underground drainage
- Surface is hydrologically dry despite being climatically wet
- Water table deep, accessible only via wells and dolines
- This is the KEY reason the peninsula is not more populated despite being flat

### Settlement Pattern
- Population concentrated on coast (within 10 km)
- Interior largely agricultural (sugar cane) or uninhabited
- Three comarcas: La Punta (362/km²), El Verger (134/km²), El Cap (23/km²)
- Total: ~1,065,000 across 7,880 km² = 135/km²
- Much lower density than canal corridor (El Delta = 2,055/km²)

### Birú Connection
- Several municipalities have Birú names: Yarawakí, Pukakí, Yapurá
- Caves as archaeological sites — Birú occupation pre-colonisation
- Dolines as natural water reservoirs — focal points for indigenous settlement
- Birú place names associated with karst features

### Zong Connection
- Lamhai (La Badia) and Tekkang (L'Espigó) — Zong-derived names
- Fosian community from Zong Balboa period (1899-1950)
- Sinchewese workers brought to canal and plantations
