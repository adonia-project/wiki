# Hotel Senador — Internal Notes

## Key Facts
- **Name**: Hotel Senador (Catalan)
- **Original**: Built 1858, four-story stone colonial building on Embarcador CR
- **Destroyed**: 1944 (GAW bombing + fire)
- **Rebuilt**: 1962, floors 18–35 of International Finance Building (64 stories)
- **Address**: 2 Embarcador CR, Sant Cristòfor, Balboa
- **Rating**: 5-star
- **Rooms**: 312
- **Restaurants**: Magpalibog (32nd floor), Terrassa del Senador (19th floor), El Mirador (35th floor cocktail bar)

## Original Hotel (1858–1944)
- Built during early industrial expansion of Sant Cristòfor
- Four-story stone, Volisanian colonial style, central courtyard, arched galleries, terracotta roof
- Initially for merchants and government officials
- Position on Embarcador CR = harbourfront promenade = centre of public life

## Zong Era Intellectual Hub (1899–1944)
- **Cafè del Senador**: Ground-floor café, informal salon
- **Cercle del Port** ("Harbour Circle"): Zong-Balboan + Sinchewese writers, Saturday evening gatherings
  - Lîm Chheng-hong: Zong-Balboan poet, 海邊的聲 ("Voices from the Shore", 1928)
  - Nor Pham: Sinchewese journalist, underground newspaper L'Estret
  - Elena Vargas: Balboan Creole novelist, The Walls (1954) — set in hotel during siege
- **Grup Modernista**: Visual artists + architects, second-floor gallery exhibitions 1915–1944
  - First significant body of work synthesizing Zong, Volisanian, Balboan visual traditions
  - Exhibitions were rare spaces where Zong, Balboan, Sinchewese socialized as equals
- **Political role**: Basement meeting point for resistance during GAW siege; negotiations for dissolution of Zong Balboa conducted in private rooms
- Hotel owned by Zong family that valued neutral space reputation → light colonial surveillance

## Destruction & Rebuilding
- Destroyed 1944: bombing + fire gutted the building, courtyard and café entirely lost
- Ruins stood for over a decade
- 1962: Site incorporated into International Finance Building (64-story skyscraper)
- Hotel rebuilt on floors 18–35, no architectural resemblance to original
- Controversial: critics said commercial development erased cultural heritage; supporters said preserving name/location was better than losing it entirely
