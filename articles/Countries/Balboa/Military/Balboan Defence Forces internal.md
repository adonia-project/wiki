# Balboan Defence Forces — Internal Notes

## Overview
The Balboan Defence Forces (BDF; Volisanian: Forces Armades de Balboa, FAB) is the unified military of the Republic of Balboa. Established 1974 after the Treaty of Areza (1973) lifted demilitarization. Built from scratch — not from the old constabulary. All-volunteer professional force. ~30,000 active personnel.

## User-Confirmed Facts

### Structure
- 2 branches: Army, Naval and Air Service (includes Coast Guard and Air Wing)
- Air Force was never a separate branch — Air Wing was always part of the Naval and Air Service
- No 1980s Air Force creation — Air Wing equipped with helicopters/scouting aircraft from the start, using donated GAW surplus
- Personnel: ~12K Army, ~18K Naval and Air Service
- All-volunteer force (no conscription)
- Civilian workforce managed by Ministry of Defence: cafeteria/food service, janitorial/facilities maintenance, administrative clerks, legal advisors/paralegals, procurement/contract specialists, financial analysts, HR personnel, IT support

### Command
- President is Commander-in-Chief
- Minister of Defence: civilian, appointed by president, reports to president, day-to-day admin
- Joint Military Council (Consell Militar Conjunt): comprises branch commanders of Army, Navy, Air Force; chaired by chief of defence staff; coordinates operations, develops doctrine, provides unified military advice to minister
- Chief of defence staff: senior military officer, chairs the Joint Military Council, reports to minister
- Each branch headed by a branch commander reporting to chief of defence staff
- War powers: Constitution requires National Assembly approval for act of war; president has leeway to direct operations once approved; National Assembly can also withdraw from war; president cannot unilaterally end/withdraw from war but can act as agent for treaties (subject to assembly ratification)

### Budget
- ~0.7% of GDP (~$2.4B based on ~$340B nominal GDP)
- Lowered from initial estimate because Balboa relies on AC coalition forces for real military protection
- Budget constrained since 1990s reforms (troop reductions)

### Equipment
- Much of early equipment was donated GAW surplus from coalition forces at the Areza military base
- Donated equipment (small arms, vehicles, vessels, aircraft) formed basis of inventory through 1970s–1980s
- Gradually supplemented by purchases from foreign suppliers
- **2001 modernisation**: comprehensive programme partially funded by AC loan; replaced remaining GAW surplus with modern equipment from Balisca, Okaiken, and Volisania
- Post-2001 principal foreign suppliers: Volisania, Balisca, Okaiken (replaced Zong as supplier)
- Domestic industry: repair/services focused
- One domestic small watercraft manufacturer: Drassanes de Haicang (invented)
- No significant domestic arms industry

### Defence Strategy
- Balboa relies on AC coalition forces for real military protection, by being the host country
- BDF provides domestic security, canal defence, support to civilian law enforcement
- AC coalition forces provide principal deterrent against external aggression
- Two key foreign military installations:
  - Areza joint military base (International City of Areza) — coalition installation, AC member state forces
  - AC Haicang Navy Station — leased to AC for docking/maintenance of foreign military ships, co-located with BDF naval base at Haicang

### Treaty Status
- Treaty of Sant Llàtzer (1899): demilitarized Balboa, constabulary only
- Treaty of Areza (1973): fully lifted demilitarization
- No remaining treaty restrictions

### Twelve Guarantees
- Apply to BDF: non-discrimination, ethnic representation in recruitment and promotion
- BDF recruitment base predominantly Black and mixed Balboan (BPP political base)
- Integration of Guarantees was gradual (1960s–1980s modernization effort)

### ICPF Relationship
- BDF and ICPF are separate but coordinate
- ICPF mandate in Balboa proper terminated in 1990s (phased reduction)
- ICPF mandate in Areza is perpetual

### Peacekeeping
- Yes, AC peacekeeping contributor
- Small deployments: few dozen personnel (observers, medical, engineering, small infantry)

### Navy Role
- Coast guard duties, managing ship traffic
- Canal approach security
- Main base: Haicang (Estret Province, Aethiopian coast)
- Organised into 6 naval divisions + 3 air wing divisions = 9 total:
  1. **Coast Guard / Maritime Patrol** (Guardacostes i Patrulla Marítima) ~5,500 — core operational unit, patrol boats and coastal vessels, manage ship traffic, routine patrols, vessel inspections, maritime law enforcement
  2. **Canal Defence Unit** (Unitat de Defensa del Canal) ~2,500 — naval side of canal security, monitors vessel traffic through canal, coordinates with Balboa Canal Authority, responds to canal zone incidents
  3. **Search and Rescue** (Recerca i Rescat) ~2,800 — combined naval+air SAR, coastal waters, canal approaches, overland; operates helicopters and rescue boats
  4. **Naval Intelligence** (Intel·ligència Naval) ~1,000 — monitors maritime threats, tracks suspicious vessels, coordinates with Army Intelligence on sea-route smuggling
  5. **Naval Logistics and Transport** (Logística i Transport Naval) ~1,000 — manages vessel fleet, fuel supplies, maintenance schedules, transport support, supply lines for naval bases
  6. **Naval Military Police** (Policia Militar Naval) ~500 — internal discipline within Naval and Air Service, security for naval installations including Haicang and AC Haicang Navy Station coordination

### Air Wing Role
- Component of the Naval and Air Service, not a separate branch
- Support to Army, search and rescue, reconnaissance
- Mainly helicopters and scouting aircraft
- No fighter jets
- 3 divisions:
  1. **Helicopter / Transport Wing** (Ala d'Helicòpters i Transport) ~2,500 — core of the Air Wing, helicopter fleet for transport, medical evacuation, airlift support to Army and naval operations
  2. **Reconnaissance Squadron** (Esquadró de Reconeixement) ~1,200 — scouting aircraft, aerial observation for Army Intelligence (SIGINT/IMINT), border surveillance
  3. **Ground Support and Logistics** (Suport Terrestre i Logística) ~1,000 — maintenance, logistics, ground support, airbase operations, supply lines
- SAR merged into naval Search and Rescue division (~2,800 combined)

### Army Role (current)
- Organised into 7 divisions:
  1. **Land Forces** (Forces Terrestres) — traditional army, base stations, training/drills, support to police in high-risk situations (drug smugglers/crime) but not primary force, called in only when requested
  2. **Army Intelligence** (Intel·ligència de l'Exèrcit) — military-specific threats (armed groups in highlands, threats to military installations); border intelligence (remote crossings, cross-border smuggling networks); SIGINT/IMINT using Air Force reconnaissance aircraft and military-grade surveillance equipment; counter-intelligence within BDF (personnel vetting, protection of military communications); shares products with civilian agencies
  3. **Border Forces** (Forces de Frontera) — formal border patrol, works with civilian immigration authority for border services
  4. **Engineering Corps** (Cos d'Enginyers) — infrastructure construction, road maintenance in remote areas, disaster response (floods, landslides), canal infrastructure support
  5. **Logistics and Transport** (Logística i Transport) — manages vehicle fleet, transport support to police/government agencies, supply lines for remote bases
  6. **Military Police** (Policia Militar) — internal discipline within BDF, security for military installations, works alongside civilian police at military facilities
  7. **Ceremonial and Protocol** (Ceremonial i Protocol) — honour guard, state ceremonies, presidential security detail, small but visible unit

### Bases
- BDF HQ: Sant Bart (Jala Province)
- Army: 5 bases (one in each province) + HQ in Sant Bart
- Naval and Air Service: 3 primary naval bases (Haicang, Badia Curta, Campdària) + ~28 naval stations along the coast (forward patrol posts, rescue stations, refuelling points — specific locations TBD)
- Haicang also serves as principal ship repair and maintenance facility
- AC facilities: Areza joint military base, AC Haicang Navy Station
- Staffing breakdown: see `BDF staffing internal.csv` in same directory
  - Army: 12,000 military across HQ + 5 provincial bases
  - Naval and Air Service: 11,900 naval + 6,000 air wing = 17,900 military across 3 primary bases + 28 coastal stations
  - Civilian: ~3,375 across all installations
  - Total: ~29,900 military + ~3,375 civilian = ~33,275 total

### Ranks
- Volisanian-style: Capità, Tinent, Major, Coronel, General, etc.
- Army and Air Force share similar structure
- Navy uses separate naval rank system

## History (from existing wiki articles)

### Pre-BDF (1899–1973)
- Treaty of Sant Llàtzer (1899): demilitarized, constabulary only
- Constabulary expanded by planter oligarchy as internal control instrument
- Volisania secretly re-equipped constabulary in 1920s (treaty violation)
- **1930s**: Volisania secretly funded and organised the Balboa Revolutionary Army (Exèrcit Revolucionari de Balboa) — a parallel military force distinct from the constabulary, to support the planned invasion of Zong Balboa
- Revolutionary Army was the primary Balboan combatant against Zong military during the GAW
- Constabulary was relegated to supporting role during GAW
- Both Revolutionary Army and constabulary destroyed during Zong occupation (1943)
- Post-war: neither force reconstituted; no Balboan military existed for nearly 3 decades
- **1947–1952**: Military/security provided by the Unified Support Forces — coalition of Zong, Louyang, Sinchew, and liberal Volisanian forces; ad hoc post-war arrangement; maintained order, disarmed former belligerents, managed canal zone reconstruction
- **1952–1974**: Military/security provided by AC Unified Forces and ICPF; transition from ad hoc coalition to formal multilateral AC authority; ICPF was principal security presence
- Pre-reunification national police force disbanded during Twelve Guarantees (associated with extrajudicial killings, state violence against minorities)
- Senior personnel from disbanded police transitioned into military or later security structures
- **1974**: BDF established; AC Unified Forces withdrew; ICPF transitioned to training/support role for the new BDF

### Establishment (1973–1974)
- Treaty of Areza (1 July 1973): lifted demilitarization fully
- BDF established 1974, built from scratch (NOT from constabulary, Revolutionary Army, or pre-reunification police remnants)
- First recruits from Black and mixed Balboan communities (BPP base)
- Early challenges: no institutional memory, no trained officer corps, no equipment
- Ministry of Defence established simultaneously
- **ICPF training and support**: The International Cooperative Police Force provided substantial training, instructors, doctrine, and institutional guidance to the BDF in its early years — the new force had no institutional memory and relied on ICPF support
- **Early dual structure**: The BDF functioned more as a security force than a conventional military in its early years:
  - **Army (localised)**: functioned largely as a jobs programme, providing employment to young men in rural areas while assisting with policing in the interior provinces
  - **Navy (professionalised)**: oriented primarily toward coast guard duties around the Balboa Canal and coastal waters
- **Policing over military action**: In the early years, the BDF was employed principally in policing and internal security, not military action — reflecting absence of external threats and need for rural security presence
- The Army's localised/jobs-programme character evolved into the default policing arrangement that persisted through the 1980s

### Early Deployments (1974–1976)
- Siege of Murado Ranch (March 1974): first military deployment, enforce land expropriation, 18-day siege, no bloodshed
- Sant Bart University bombing (February 1975): POB terrorist attack, 42 killed
- La Guàrdia Campaign (1975–1976): largest domestic military operation, counter-terrorism against POB in Jala highlands
- Controversial: special arms licenses to Biru communities → 1977 Aeroístmus hijacking

### Hill Wars and Domestic Policing (1980s)
- Drug manufacturing in Tramuntana and Jala highlands
- Hill Wars: intermittent violence between rival drug factions
- BDF deployed for drug interdiction
- UTT (United Terrorism Taskforce) established 1983: joint military-police-intelligence
- Protected corridors created for secured transportation routes
- BDF served as default policing force outside major cities
- Military was decentralized and unevenly managed
- Pay differed by branch: navy and central forces better resourced than army units
- BPP pushed to modernize military from 1960s through 1980s

### Security Reform (1990s–2000s)
- 1990s: main reform decade
- ICPF mandate in Balboa proper terminated (phased reduction)
- Hope and Survival Happiness Group standoff (1999): botched military raid, 3 dead, demonstrated military's lack of policing capacity
- Catalyst for National Police force of Balboa creation
- Reforms: refocused BDF on national defense, reduced troop numbers, separated military from domestic policing
- Also: raid on Manuel Sueza's compound (mentioned in internal notes, not yet a full article)

## Invented Elements

### Drassanes de Haicang
- Domestic small watercraft manufacturer
- Based at Haicang, Estret Province
- Builds: patrol boats, harbour launches, coastal patrol vessels
- Also provides ship repair and maintenance services
- Only domestic naval vessel manufacturer
- Name derived from Volisanian (Catalan-inspired) "drassanes" (shipyards) + Haicang (place name)
- Consistent with existing wiki usage: "Les Drassanes" appears in Foneria General de Balboa article
