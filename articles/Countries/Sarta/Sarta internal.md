# Sarta — Internal Notes

## Overview
- Sarta is the unification of Sartan-speaking polities in Illypnia in 1197 under Ramiro I of Fuertelon.
- The monarchy follows primogeniture through the House of Fuertelon.
- The early state wars run through the Sierra Madre frontier and toward the Kingdom of Hirona.
- The Sartan-Tamarin War (1247–1273) ends with Tamarin annexed and a treaty with Jacan.
- The Great Catholic Schism in 1313 grows out of disputes over church authority, taxation, and jurisdiction in Illypnia.
- Kastruneto is landlocked and more isolated from papal revenue networks, which helps explain its criticism of indulgences, purgatory, and Curial corruption.
- After the death of the sitting pope, the conclave elects the Kastrunetean Augustin V, who establishes court at Zamarra on Lake Zamarra.
- The Two Centuries War follows, with Kastruneto, Breisland, and Nabia supporting Zamarra and Volisania, Sarta, and Jacan supporting the papal states.
- In Sarta, the schism reinforces rather than creates north-south divisions.

## Naming / terminology
- Zamarran is a church label, not a people label.
- All of the populations under discussion still speak Sartan.
- The royal house is the House of Fuertelon.
- The southern kingdoms are Kastruneto and Tamarin.
- The warrior king of Fuertelon is Ramiro I.

## Geography / political context
- Northern highland core: Fuertelon.
- Southern limit of early expansion: Sierra Madre / Kingdom of Hirona line.
- Northwest frontier: the Sabean Confederacy, a confederacy of city-states in the area of modern Sabea.
- Southern kingdoms beyond Hirona: Kastruneto and Tamarin.
- Southern diplomatic rival: Kingdom of Jacan.
- Northwestern regional rival: Volisania.

## Early dynastic sequence
- Ramiro I (1197–c. 1228) — unifier and founder of the royal house.
- Sancho I (c. 1228–c. 1261) — southern expansion and consolidation.
- Fernando I (c. 1261–c. 1294) — frontier defense and Sabean wars.
- Alfonso I (c. 1294–1313) — pre-schism ruler; confessional tensions harden under his reign.

## Late monarchs
- Carlos I of Sarta (1705–1716) — born 1669; died 1716; forced to abdicate after the Treaty of Bahia Alta.
- Maria I of Sarta (1716–1747) — born 1684; died 1747; ruled through the royal parliamentary court.
- Alfonso II of Sarta (1811–8 April 1855) — born 1731; installed by Volisania after the Second Sartan Civil War.
- Carlos II of Sarta (1855–unknown) — successor to Alfonso II; early industrial reform era.

## Population working series
- 1690 baseline: about 3.7 million.
- First Sartan Civil War (1738–1747): about 10% mortality; working postwar population about 3.75 million.
- Second Sartan Civil War (1799–1811): about 8.5% mortality; working 1811 population about 4.2 million.
- First official census (1815): about 4.3 million.
- Use these as planning figures, not exact census totals.

## 1811-1846
- Broad domestic support grew for recovering the Volisanian-held coast, but elites stayed cautious after the civil wars.
- Jacan and Echia pushed for regional balance, while fears of stronger Fosian powers made division in Illypnia look dangerous.
- The Constitution of 1812 standardised bureaucracy, tax codes, municipalities, and provinces, and limited the king to a unicameral parliament of lords.
- The Sartan Peasants Revolution of 1837 in Leona involved roughly 10,000 serfs and pushed the lords' parliament to formalise a national military.
- The Act of Standing Military of 1837 replaced estate levies and lordly military contributions with a formal national military.
- The Act on Free Movement of 1838 abolished serfdom, compensated the lords, accelerated urban migration, and strained the treasury.
- Two political blocs formed: a pro-Volisania line and a freer-from-Volisania line. The latter was strongest south of the Sierra Madre and in Leona.
- Coal mining began in 1844 in the Sierra Madre and mainly fed Volisanian demand, driving northern development.
- The Occidental Agreement of 1846 returned the coast as Occidental Province in exchange for a 50% coal discount for 99 years. Anti-Volisanian critics called it the Tontas Trato.
