# History of Zong (1390–1538) — internal notes

## Overview
Period from c. 1390 (end of Jade Sea War) to 1538 (founding of Tamsui). Zong pivoted from continental trade to Shendan Ocean colonialism.

## Key names
- **Emperor Tianzhi** (天治, "Heavenly Governance") — reigned 1302–1349, during Jade Sea War
- **Emperor Tianyong** (天勇, "Heavenly Courage") — reigned 1349–1395, saw end of Jade Sea War, began post-war crisis
- **Emperor Tian'an** (天安, "Heavenly Peace") — reigned 1395–1430, the paralysis emperor
- **Emperor Tianyang** (天洋, "Heavenly Ocean") — reigned c. 1430–1478, reform emperor who pivoted Zong eastward
- **Emperor Tianjing** (天景, "Heavenly Radiance") — reigned 1478–1521, continued Shendan programme
- **Emperor Tiancheng** (天成, "Heavenly Completion") — reigned 1521–1560, Tamsui founding and colonial split
- **Emperor Tianhao** (天浩, "Heavenly Vastness") — reigned 1560–1598, Great Disappointment, escalating tensions
- **Emperor Tianxiao** (天孝, "Heavenly Filial Piety") — reigned 1598–1643, 1606 expulsion decree
- **Xinhai** (新海, "New Sea") — first colony 1456, southern coast, failed due to indigenous resistance, reduced to trading post
- **Anping** (安平, "Peaceful") — second colony 1474, modern-day Louyang, successful
- **Tamsui** — capital of Sinchew colony, founded 1538, penal/plantation settlement

## Political factions
- **Continental Faction** (大陸派) — restore continental trade, negotiate with Daras, western aristocracy
- **Maritime Faction** (海洋派) — pivot eastward, Shendan Ocean exploration, eastern coastal/naval interests
- Factional struggle dominated c. 1390–1430; Tianyang's accession broke the deadlock

## Timeline
- c. 1390: Jade Sea War ends; Darasian trade blockade imposed
- early 1400s: Jade Sea Plague — pandemic from Daras, 10-15% mortality, trans-Fosian
- early 1400s–1420s/30s: Great Famine — currency depression → wheat price crash → agricultural deficits → cyclical collapse → major famine
- c. 1390–1430: Economic crisis, political factionalism, strategic paralysis
- c. 1430: Tianyang accedes; Maritime Faction wins
- 1430s: First Shendan expeditions depart
- 1440s–1450s: Exploratory voyages, seasonal trading contacts, charting of New World coast
- 1456: Xinhai colony founded (southern coast) — fails due to indigenous resistance
- 1474: Anping colony founded (modern-day Louyang) — succeeds
- 1474–1538: Northern Lurandian coastal exploration, incremental expansion
- 1538: Tamsui founded as colonial city within Louyang colony
- 1543: Tamsui Insurgency → colonial split along Qingjiang river → Sinchew and Louyang colonies

## Colonial structure
- **Qingjiang** (清江, "Clear River") — divides Sinchew (northwest) from Louyang (southeast)
- **Hook of Tianhe** — geographic feature where Qingjiang meets the coast
- **Tamsui Insurgency** (1543) — settler revolt by Tamsui colonial leadership, felt neglected by Anping
- Split resolved by Zong court dividing territory into two separate colonies

## Key themes
- State-directed exploration (not merchant-led like Illypnian colonisation)
- Institutional continuity — House of Yuanlong, Societal Assembly survive crisis
- Jade Sea Plague (early 1400s): pandemic from Daras, 10-15% mortality, trans-Fosian
- Great Famine: blockade → currency depression → wheat price crash → agricultural deficits → cyclical famine over 20-30 years
- Plague + famine incentivised emigration to New World in second half of 1400s
- Learning from Xinhai failure → more careful site selection for Anping
- Penal colony model at Tamsui — convicts + voluntary settlers
- High mortality, "make fortunes or die" reputation
- Spread of Baiduism on eastern coast → elite unease → seeds of 1606 expulsion
- Cultural transformation: New World reshaped Zong's imagination
- Spice trade restored access to pepper, cinnamon, nutmeg at lower prices
- Indigenous visitation: trade emissaries, merchants, labourers, religious seekers
- Colonial goods: spices, tropical hardwoods, dyes, cotton, sugar, tobacco
- New World motifs in eastern port architecture, literature, cuisine
- Sengoku Export: Sanese rōnin mercenaries (mid-1500s) in Zong colonies, Iron Baiduism, shaped Illypnian perceptions during Great Disappointment (1564)
