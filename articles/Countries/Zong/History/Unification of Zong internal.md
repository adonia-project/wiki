# Unification of Zong — Internal Notes

## Purpose
Create a history article for the unification era of Zong based on the source material currently available in the wiki repository.

## Source policy
- Do **not** use `articles/unorganized/Zong.mediawiki`; the user explicitly said it is wrong.
- Use `articles/unorganized/Mabatok.mediawiki` as the main source for the later unification phase.
- Use `articles/unorganized/Kingdom of Donghai.mediawiki` for the earlier fragmented Zongic states.

## Working interpretation
- **Zhongyuan** is one kingdom and the core power base.
- The broader historical setting is the **Zongic kingdoms / states**.
- The unification runs across multiple reigns rather than a single king.
- The united polity is commonly the **Kingdom of Zong** and later imperial style is the **Jade Empire**.
- The article should treat the unification as a consolidation process rather than a single battle.

## Revised interpretation after user clarification
- **Nanyang** first enters the Zong sphere through dynastic marriage during the House of Long.
- The Nanyang merger is a slow administrative and personal union, not an instant conquest.
- **Beilong** is a sacred kingdom and the seat of the Tianist religion.
- Zongyuan rulers historically respected Beilong's autonomy, but Beilong later weakens through dynastic decline.
- The final conquest phase should include a **southern march invasion** led by a named commander.
- The later constitutional shift should mention the **Societal Assembly** only briefly.

## Tone and style notes
- Formal, encyclopedic prose.
- Avoid over-specifying names, dates, or battle details that are not present in the source material.
- The article should remain broad enough to cover both the early kingdom-consolidation phase and the later frontier reconquest phase.
- The new version should explicitly show the transition from conquest to court-state governance.

## Historical notes
- In 894, Haizhou's court collapsed financially and Jiaozhi invaded, breaking the southern "Great Peace" buffer between Haizhou, Nanyang, and Jiaozhi.
- The buffer system had been holding against repeated Zhongyuan expansion attempts since the collapse of Donghai.
- This should be treated as a major pre-unification destabilising event, not a unified campaign in itself.
- Beilong's sacred status should be treated as historically important and politically distinctive.
