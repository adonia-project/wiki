# History of Zong (1606-1711) — Internal Notes

## Key Emperors
- **Emperor Tianxiao** (天孝) — r. c. 1605–1643, Tianren's younger brother
  - Issued 1606 Baiduist expulsion decree
  - Suppressed Baidu People's Rebellion (1606-1623)
  - Ruled with Tianist hardliners dominant
  - Growing tension with merchant elite and Societal Assembly
- **Emperor Tianrong** (天榮, "Heavenly Prosperity") — r. 1643–1681
  - 3rd cousin of Tianxiao, aligned with merchant interests
  - Installed by Merchants' Revolution (bloodless transition)
  - Presided over constitutional monarchy transition
  - Sinchew War (1648-1656) fought during his reign
- **Emperor Tianzheng** (天正, "Heavenly Correctness") — r. 1681–1711
  - Succeeded Tianrong
  - End of Secret Trade (1680) under maros pressure
  - First War of the Isthmus (1711) erupted at end of reign

## Timeline
- 1606: Baiduist Expulsion decree
- 1606-1623: Baidu People's Rebellion (100,000+ killed, genocide characterization)
- c. 1612-1615: Lau Kingdom War — Zong invaded Lau Kingdom to destabilize it as revenge for supporting Canglan Rebellion (that assassinated Tianren)
- 1620-1624: Kawaei invasion — Kawaei Yoshiyuki sent 80,000 soldiers from Suzuya island into Shansi, aimed at Zong but bogged down in Shansi
- By 1650: Over 1M Zong Baiduists transported to Sinchew
- 1643: Merchants' Revolution — bloodless transition to Tianrong (3rd cousin, merchant-aligned)
  - General Assembly created, non-royal representatives formalized
  - Constitutional monarchy: emperor retains executive/military/foreign policy, assembly has legislative/fiscal authority
  - Key architects:
    - Lim Chiau-beng (林朝明, 1598-1672): merchant house leader, organized coalition, first elected rep of eastern ports
    - Ng Hong-sik (黃弘錫, 1605-1668): constitutional theorist, wrote Assembly Discourses (議會論), first Chancellor of the Assembly
    - Tan Eng-liong (陳永龍, 1590-1661): colonial rep from Louyang, designed colonial representation system, bridged mainland/colonial interests
    - Ong Tek-khiam (王德謙, 1585-1659): Yuanlong aristocrat, identified Tianrong, persuaded designated heir to renounce, first President of the General Assembly
- 1648-1656: Sinchew War — two interconnected dimensions:
  - Internal civil war in Sinchew: older colonial establishment (plantation owners, pre-expulsion merchants, Sanese mercenaries) vs new Baiduist settlers
  - External war: Zong mainland + Louyang + Sinchew colonial loyalists vs Sinchew Baiduist rebels
  - Louyang provided naval support, supplies, blockade of Sinchew coast
  - Ended with negotiated settlement: Baiduists granted self-governance in exchange for recognizing Zong sovereignty, but dual power structure left unresolved
- 1650s-1670s: Second Lurandian Native Wars — emerged directly from Sinchew War instability
  - Sinchew War drew colonial garrisons away from interior frontier, leaving it undefended
  - Indigenous peoples seized opportunity to reclaim land lost to colonial expansion
  - Sinchew War transitioned into the Second Lurandian Native Wars
  - Louyang also exposed after committing garrisons to Sinchew War
  - Thousands of natives killed, treaties in 1670s didn't resolve underlying conflicts (persisted until 1800s)
- 1653: Restoration Attempt — Tianxiao's designated heir declared himself Emperor Tianwei (天威), supported by western/southern aristocracy, Tianist clergy, western garrisons
  - Failed: defeated by loyalist forces (eastern merchant militia + northern garrisons) organized by Ong Tek-khiam and Lim Chiau-beng
  - Tianwei exiled to northern monastery, died 1671
  - Consolidated constitutional monarchy, curtailed Tianist church political influence
- 1680: End of Secret Trade — Maros pressure, Zong naval deployment
- 1681: Tianrong dies, succeeded by son Emperor Tianzheng (天正, r. 1681-1711)
  - Reign: relative mainland stability, growing colonial tension
  - First War of the Isthmus erupted in final year of reign
- 1711: First War of the Isthmus begins (ends 1716)
  - Causes: Volisania covertly supported Sinchew Baiduist rebels during Sinchew War (supplying firearms/materiel via Balboa smuggling networks) + closure of Volisanian ports (Sant Cristòfor) to Zong commercial traffic
  - Zong launched expedition to seize Sant Cristòfor on the Isthmus of Balboa
  - Volisania + Burawa mobilized against Zong; Breisland and Merica supported Zong
  - Zong captured Sant Cristòfor 1713 but was surrounded by Illypnian counter-offensive
  - Treaty of Remoria (1716): Zong withdrew but got docking/passage rights through Volisanian ports
  - Volisanian humiliation → contributed to Volisanian Civil War (1736)

## Key Themes
- Aftermath of expulsion: Baiduist diaspora transforms Sinchew
- Constitutional transition: Merchants' Revolution, General Assembly, non-royal representation
- Colonial conflicts: Sinchew War, Second Lurandian Native Wars, First War of the Isthmus
- Shansi instability: Lau Kingdom War, Kawaei invasion
- End of Secret Trade under constitutional monarchy

## Geography
- Shansi/Sansei is SOUTH of Zong (not north)
- Order from SW to NE: Daras → Three Luoyan States → Jyunhap → Zong
- Lau Kingdom: breakaway Shansi state (1605), south of Zong
- Kawaei invasion: from Sanese archipelago via Suzuya island into southeastern Shansi
