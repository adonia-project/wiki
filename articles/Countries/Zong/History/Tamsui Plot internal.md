# Tamsui Plot — internal notes

Begun 2026-09-21. Rewritten the same day from a passive refusal of orders into a trade dispute with a petition to the crown.

Article: `Tamsui Plot.mediawiki`. Related: `Chiu Khéng-gio̍k internal.md`, `History of Sinchew (1474-1538).mediawiki`.

---

## 1. What changed, and why

**The original version** had the settlement passively declining to obey Anping — a refusal sustained by the length of the voyage. Accurate as far as it went, but it left the *motive* thin: the grievance was described as administrative, investment and appointments, which is not much to build a rebellion of correspondence on.

**The user's rewrite gives it an economic cause:**
> *"the main issue was the sinchew colony unlike when it was under okheng all goods and people had to stop in anping first, therefore sinchew alwasy recieved the leftover goods and supplies, this was more evident after the war because during the war it was flush with resources as men and troops came in, once the war ended the resoruses dried up and sinchew whenever CKG asked for anything they had to wait for anping approval and usually anping was quite hostile with them because many in anping felt that sinchew was a waste of resources and wanted to see it collapse, so they slowed shipments of goods, and enforced the restriction of direct imports from zong"*

**So the colony is a transit monopoly.** Anping sits between Tamsui and Zong and takes its share first. **Okheng had traded directly; the colony made it a dependency.** The settlement is worse off under the colony than it was under CKG's own private kingdom — which is the whole point.

## 2. The structure of the episode

| phase | content |
|---|---|
| **Background** | under Okheng the territory traded direct; after 1535 all goods passed through Anping. Tolerable during the war (1538–42) when troops and supplies flowed through Tamsui; intolerable after 7 April 1542 when the flow stopped |
| **Anping's hostility** | a body of opinion held the northern territory was a waste of resources and wanted to see it collapse — and those who held that view could act on it |
| **The violations** | from about 1542 Tamsui trades direct again, using **private merchants from Zong**, bypassing Anping. Runs about a year |
| **Discovery** | by **11 August 1543** Anping has evidence |
| **The envoy** | **4 September 1543** — CKG sends an envoy secretly to Huwei, petitioning not for separation but for **governance on its own terms** |
| **The force** | Anping despatches a force to depose and arrest CKG, arriving before the petition is heard |
| **Anping's characterisation** | the administration reports it to Zong as an attempt by CKG **to restore his own polity** |
| **The investigation** | the petition arrives at court at about the same time as Anping's report; the court gets two irreconcilable accounts and sends its own investigators |
| **Findings** | no force raised, no territory seized, no officers deposed or resisted; the substance was the terms of supply. **A breakdown of authority, not a rebellion** |
| **Resolution** | CKG steps down, untried and unpenalised; the office of provisional administrator dissolved; a replacement administrator sent; the territory divided |

**Tân Kéng-seng** is retained as the senior official who *administered* the arrangement at Tamsui — the correspondence, the private carriers, the refusals. CKG is the principal; Tân Kéng-seng is his instrument. **This keeps both established figures and gives each a role.**

## 3. THE DATE PROBLEM — needs the user's decision

**The user wrote: *"in 1544 the colonial government had sent a replacement administrator."***

**Every existing article dates the division of the colony to 1543** — `Colonial Zong`, `Tamsui Plot`, `Treaty of Anping`, `History of Zong`, and the `History of Sinchew` title itself.

**If the replacement arrived in 1544, the split is probably 1544 too.** Options:
- **(a)** The episode runs late 1543 → 1544, and the **division is 1544**. Then many articles need their dates changed.
- **(b)** CKG is deposed late 1543 and the split is **formally** 1543, with the replacement arriving early 1544. Then only the administrator's arrival is 1544.

**And a knock-on:** I had written in `Chiu Bûn-hái` that he *"died in the same year as his son's removal from office and the division of the colony."* **Chiu Bûn-hái died on 9 June 1543 — before the private trade was discovered (11 August) and before the envoy (4 September).** Under the new timeline **he died before the crisis broke**, and that line must be corrected. *(He may still have lived to see the sale of 1535 and eight years of his son's governorship.)*

## 5. What the rewrite gains
**The plot now has a cause worth resisting for**, and the irony is sharper: **CKG ruled Okheng as a sovereign and traded directly with Zong. He surrendered that in 1535 and became a colonial administrator — and within seven years he was worse off than he had been as a private king.** He then broke the colony's trade rules, and was accused of trying to restore the polity he had been forced to sell.

**The accusation may even be true.** Whether he wanted his kingdom back or merely direct trade is not settled by the papers, and the article deliberately presents Anping's characterisation as a characterisation rather than a fact.
