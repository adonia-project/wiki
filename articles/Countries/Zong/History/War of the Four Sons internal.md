# War of the Four Sons — Internal Notes

## Purpose
Create a standalone conflict article for the succession war that followed the death of King Yuanxiong and broke Great Zhongyuan apart.

## User details incorporated
- King Yuanxiong died unexpectedly at **54**.
- His death was **suspected poisoning**.
- The four sons were all **within a few years of each other**.
- They were already governing regions as administrators before the succession crisis.
- Their ages at the start of the crisis were roughly **22–28**.

## Working interpretation
- This is the central dynastic conflict of the Great Zhongyuan era.
- The war begins in **300 CE** and ends in **318 CE**.
- The four sons' administrative regions become the basis for the later successor kingdoms.
- The result is the breakup of Great Zhongyuan into **Haizhou**, **Nanyang**, **Jiaozhi**, and **Zhongyuan**.

## Names and regional assignments used
- **Prince Hailin** — eastern coast — age 28
- **Prince Nanyan** — southern interior — age 26
- **Prince Jiaozhe** — far south — age 24
- **Prince Zhongwei** — central capital and surrounding districts — age 22

## Tone and style notes
- Formal encyclopedic prose.
- Emphasize the administrative origins of the conflict rather than portraying it as a simple battlefield succession.
- Keep the article focused on the collapse of Great Zhongyuan and the emergence of the successor kingdoms.
- The suspected poisoning should remain framed as a contemporary suspicion, not a confirmed fact.
