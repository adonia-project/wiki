# Golden Age of Zong — Internal Notes

## Purpose
Create an era article for the period between the late unification years and the Jade Sea War, framed as the **Golden Age of Zong**.

## User guidance incorporated
- Era name: **Golden Age of Zong**.
- Date range: **c. 1230–1300**.
- Political form: Zong transitions from an absolute monarchy to a court state.
- The **Societal Assembly** originated in **Nanyang** as an **aristocratic senate**.
- **Tianwu of Zong** mandated its central adoption.
- The assembly confirms the emperor, while the emperor still holds ultimate authority and has a seat in it.
- The era should emphasize imperial growth, peaceful prosperity, naval buildup, and court reform.
- Sanese trade is important.
- Chiang/Jyunhap developments remain background context only.
- The post-Tianwu ruler is a working name: **Tianhe of Zong**.
- The user prefers the assembly reform to be framed as a **conservative stabilising reform**.

## Working interpretation
The era begins while the unification project is still completing and continues into the early imperial order after Tianwu's reforms. The court becomes more institutional and less dependent on personal monarchy alone. The Societal Assembly is the main mechanism by which elites are incorporated into the new order, helping prevent the kind of fragmentation seen in earlier Zongic history.

- The article should present this era as prosperous but not static.
- The assembly should feel like a serious elite institution, not a modern parliament.
- Zong's naval growth should feel natural and tied to trade, not just military ambition.
- The southern frontier should stay in the background, with only a brief mention of Chiang State and the Jyunhap.
- The list article can later expand the imperial sequence beyond the early period.

## Jyunhap — confirmed lore (2026-04-22)
- The Jyunhap formed from **approximately 30 princely states**
- These states existed in the zone between Zong and the Luoyan States
- Formed after the **collapse of the Kingdom of Chiang**
- Chiang collapsed due to a **Buddhist rebellion** against its Tianist king, who had been oppressing Buddhists
- The Jyunhap federation therefore emerged from a religiously motivated political fragmentation, not purely a military collapse
- Tianwu married 3 daughters to Jyunhap princely states: **Fanling**, **Saikung**, **Tsimsha**
- Tianhe (Tianwu's son/successor) married the princess of Bingzhou → personal union with Bingzhou

## Religion in Zong during the Golden Age
- Zong was **multi-religious** during this period
- **Tianism** was the dominant court religion in the north/interior
- **Buddhism** was accepted and practiced in the south
- This religious pluralism contrasted with the Tianist monoculture of Chiang, whose oppression of Buddhists caused its collapse
- Relevant for future articles on: Zong religion, Jyunhap formation, Kingdom of Chiang
