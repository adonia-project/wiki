# United Midlands — Internal Notes

## Overview
The United Midlands is a large English-speaking country in [[Lurandia]] and a former colonial possession of [[Mercia]]. Its capital and largest city is [[Brukelyn]]. The country has a population of about 182 million and sits in the middle-income range economically. It is best described as a semindustrial state: it has a substantial manufacturing base and a modern service sector, but development is highly uneven across the country.

The defining feature of the United Midlands is the sharp divide between its highly developed capital region and the rest of the country. The capital is a true primate city, concentrating government, finance, higher education, advanced services, and the country's best infrastructure. Outside the capital, the economy is much more dependent on secondary manufacturing linked to larger and more developed foreign markets.

## Historical background

### Colonial era
The United Midlands developed under Mercian rule as a colonial territory within Lurandia. The colonial administration established English as the dominant public language and built the core institutional framework that still shapes the modern state, especially the civil service, legal system, education, and transport corridors.

Colonial development was uneven even then: the colonial capital and its immediate hinterland were favoured for administrative and commercial investment, while the broader interior was treated more as a supply base and manufacturing support zone.

### Postcolonial state formation
After decolonisation, the United Midlands retained the colonial administrative language and much of the existing state structure. This helped it preserve continuity in governance and commercial relations, but it also reinforced the existing spatial imbalance between the capital and the rest of the country.

The modern state is best understood as a highly centralised postcolonial economy that has grown steadily without fully closing its regional development gaps.

## Economy and development

### Capital-led development
The capital is the country's main growth engine and by far the most developed urban area. It concentrates:
- central government and bureaucracy
- banking and insurance
- universities and research institutions
- telecommunications and professional services
- high-end retail and modern construction
- foreign investment and diplomatic activity

This concentration has made the capital significantly wealthier and better connected than the rest of the country. It is the main site of infrastructure upgrading, private investment, and consumer spending.

### Secondary manufacturing economy
Outside the capital, the United Midlands is more of a secondary manufacturing economy than a high-end industrial power. Many regions specialise in:
- garments and textiles
- food processing
- consumer assembly
- building materials
- light machinery and components
- subcontracted production for foreign firms

A large share of this manufacturing is tied to demand from more developed countries, meaning the United Midlands often sits lower in regional value chains. Its factories are important, but they usually operate as suppliers, assemblers, or processing centres rather than as major originators of advanced technology.

### Middle-income profile
The country's middle-income status reflects a mixed economy:
- strong enough to support large-scale urban industry and services
- not wealthy enough to escape dependence on external markets and foreign capital
- not poor enough to be treated as a low-income state

This gives the United Midlands a transitional character: it is more industrialised than many of its neighbours, but its development remains uneven and geographically concentrated.

### Development imbalance
The core policy challenge is the contrast between the capital and the rest of the country. The capital has far higher incomes, stronger infrastructure, and better public services, while secondary cities and provincial zones often depend on export manufacturing, logistics, or lower-margin industrial work.

This has likely produced:
- internal migration toward the capital
- land and housing pressure in the metropolitan core
- underinvestment in provincial infrastructure
- regional inequality between the centre and the periphery
- political tension over resource allocation and public spending

## Society and language
English is the dominant language of state, education, business, and media. The colonial legacy means the United Midlands likely has a strong anglophone civic identity, even if there are local varieties or regional accents. English also gives the country a practical advantage in trade, diplomacy, and access to foreign investment.

## Article framing notes
For a public country article, the United Midlands should probably be framed as:
- a major English-speaking Lurandian state
- a former Mercian colony with strong institutional continuity
- a middle-income semindustrial economy
- a country of extreme capital concentration and uneven regional development
