# Adonian Community Mission for Lacashe — Internal Notes

## Article Overview
- **File**: `Adonian Community Mission for Lacashe.mediawiki`
- **Subject**: International intervention in Lacashe
- **Period**: 1981–1983
- **Scope**: Domestic unrest, AC intervention, AC internal politics, three-sided conflict, and the constitutional settlement that followed

## Core Facts
- Triggered by the **Guladga University Massacre** in 1981 and the wider unrest of 1980–1982.
- Liberal parties in Lacashe endorsed intervention of the Adonian Community, giving the intervention domestic political backing.
- Mandated by the **Adonian Community Council**.
- Focused on security, civilian protection, and stabilisation rather than direct regime change rhetoric.
- Ended with the **1983 constitution**, universal suffrage, and the first post-intervention elections.

## Scale of the conflict
- Estimated forces in the article:
  - **International coalition**: ~75,000 troops
  - **Lacashe Armed Forces**: ~45,000 active personnel
  - **Lacashe Kadarist Association**: ~700 armed militants
- Estimated losses in the article:
  - **AC**: 18 killed, 53 wounded
  - **Lacashe Armed Forces**: 71 killed, 147 wounded
  - **Kadarists**: 112 killed, 86 captured

## Timeline beats added in the article
- 1981 opens with the **Guladga University Massacre**, which becomes the immediate trigger for the crisis.
- Mass arrests, demonstrations, and curfews define the first phase of unrest in Guledga.
- Student organisers, lecturers, dockworkers, and younger civil servants broaden the protest base during 1981.
- Armed Kadarist cells expand the conflict into workers' districts and the industrial outskirts during late 1981 and 1982.
- The AC holds an extraordinary session in Areza, described as a special convention on the Lacashe question.
- Liberal parties in Lacashe request AC support and help provide the intervention with domestic legitimacy.
- Lacashe's membership status is placed in limbo pending settlement.
- Coalition troops secure the airport, port district, and university hospital, then create protected corridors for civilians and aid.
- Kadarist commander **Musa Gurey** leads militant operations against police positions and transport corridors.
- Prime Minister **Raúl Sarmiento** appears in the late-stage negotiations alongside government ministers.
- General **Leandro Voss** serves as the senior AC military commander.
- General **Emilio Serrat** orders parts of the Lacashe Armed Forces to stand down, triggering the collapse of government authority in Guledga.
- Roundtable talks in Guledga move the crisis from security crisis to constitutional negotiation.
- The constitutional draft is promulgated on **22 November 1983**.

## Narrative beats added in the article
- Student organiser **Leila Rooble** becomes a visible face of the protests after the massacre.
- **Colonel Eduardo Constans** represents the hardline security response in Guledga.
- **Special Representative Sofia Aretza** leads the AC political and stabilisation effort.
- The mission is framed as a coalition intervention rather than a lone AC deployment.
- The opening invasion includes AC, Baliscan, and Asikyiran troops.
- The Lacashe Armed Forces are the main conventional opponent, while Kadarist militants complicate the security environment.
