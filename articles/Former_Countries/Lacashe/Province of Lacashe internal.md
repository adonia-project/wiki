## User-Provided Details (Session 2026-04-12)

### Scope & Structure

### Governance
- Pre-1709: Administrator General appointed by imperial court
- 1709-1789: Autonomous parliamentary republic (unitary, land-owner franchise)
- Governor: handled Burawa affairs; foreign affairs controlled by Burawa

### Economy
- Cotton: dominant crop, largest exporter by mid-18th century
- Large estates concentrated in few families by 1700s
- Planter families: House of Vitalis, House of Rooble, House of Gurey, House of Constans
- Triangle trade + direct global trade

### Demographics (1780s estimates)
- Enslaved: 65%
- Free Burawi settlers: 18%
- Mixed-race (free): 8%
- Dakari/Sabian families: 5%
- Indigenous Tapuya: 2%

### Social Hierarchy
- Colorism and regionalism over racism (Burawi are darker than Illypnians)
- Lighter-skin Burawi at top
- "Clansmanship" — noble families at top
- Hierarchy: noble families → land-owning non-elite → landless merchants/workers → slaves
- North Kaftian slaves had lighter skin → phrenology movement (early 1700s) to justify

### Slavery Sources
1. Ayorin and eastern interior
2. Burawi North Kaftia (lighter-skinned peoples)

### Independence (1789)
- Abolition of Slavery Act → Lacashe parliament immediately filed independence motion
- Governor Jaamac Warsame signed declaration
- Burawa didn't want to fight (politically unhealthy)
- Recognition in 1792 for anti-piracy cooperation

### Religion
- Lacashe Church formed after independence (Bishop Filipos led breakaway)
- BCC excommunicated them
- Political split, not theological

### Indigenous Peoples
- Tapuya Confederacy occupied region pre-1516
- Disease + conflict → population collapse
- Survivors: fled, assimilated, or enslaved

### War of Liberation (1853–1856)
- Coalition: Burawa, Balisca, Volisania, Asikyira, Illypnian support
- Internal forces: freedman and enslaved revolutionaries
- Context: Lacashe was one of last vestiges of slavery
- Embargoes by 1853, black market trade undercut tariffs
- War = noble cause + economic interests (slave labor undercut competitors)
