# The Crossing (Aldana novel) — Internal Notes

## Overview
Novel by Marcos Aldana, 1931. Black Balboan migration from Balboan interior to Sant Cristòfor. Foundational work of Black Balboan literature. Banned in Balboa.

## Invented Details

### Characters
- **Esteban Aldava** — father, plantation labourer, injured, declines in Sant Cristòfor, dies by 1929
- **Caridad Aldava** — mother, becomes community organiser in exurb, moral centre of novel
- **Dolores Aldava** — eldest daughter, self-taught literacy, becomes clerk for Zong merchant, social mobility distances her from family, marries Zong merchant, moves to western quarter
- **Tomás Aldava** — son, radicalised in exurb, labour organiser, arrested during canal strike
- **Marcos Aldava** — young boy (named after author? coincidence or deliberate authorial insertion)
- **Remedios** — border guide, veteran of the crossing, pragmatic, only appears in crossing sequence
- **Roldán** — Balboan overseer, embodies plantation violence, only in opening chapters

### Plot structure
- Opens 1912 on Hacienda del Norte (cotton plantation, Balboan interior)
- Family departs after Esteban's injury + Dolores's beating for literacy
- Crossing sequence (3 chapters) — on foot, at night, guided by Remedios, through scrubland border zone
- Arrival in Sant Cristòfor → migrant reception centre → exurb housing
- Second half: family diverges over a decade (1912–1929)
- Climactic: canal strike, Tomás arrested, dispersed without violence
- Final scene 1929: surviving family members share meal (olloc), awkward reunion, no resolution

### Publisher
- **Portnou Press** — small Zong-language and Balboan Creole publishing house in Sant Cristòfor commercial district

### Translations
- Zong: ''Kè-koè'' (過界, "Crossing the Boundary") by Tân Hui-chong (陳惠忠), 1934
- Volisan: ''El Trànsit'', 1936

### Censorship
- Ong Hui-seng's administration requested 2 passages cut for second printing (1932): overseer's corporal punishment + Zong border guard bribe demand
- Aldana reluctantly agreed; passages restored in 1952 posthumous edition (Jade Peninsula Literary Society, Huwei)
- Banned in Balboa 1932, ban remained until mandate period

### Critic
- **Lîm Tek-kian** (林德謙) — Zong critic, 1935 essay: novel is "Zong in its restraint and Balboan in its subject, and belongs to neither tradition entirely"

### Adaptations
- Stage: 1964 (Sant Cristòfor Repertory Company, focused on crossing); 1988 (Balboa National Theatre, focused on family dispersal)
- Film: 1979, directed by Enrique Vidal, mixed reception

### Cultural legacy
- "The crossing" (''el cruce'') = common term for the broader Black Balboan migration
- "After the crossing" = shorthand for legal equality without economic substance

## Key Dynamics
- Novel's central tension: legal equality vs. material inequality in Zong Balboa
- Family as microcosm of Black Balboan community — each member represents a different response to same conditions
- The crossing as irreversible threshold (not a bridge)
- Two cities: earliest literary depiction of the urban divide
- Language choice (Balboan Creole) as political statement — vs. Zong literary tradition
- Style: direct, unadorned, short sentences, concrete descriptions — contrasted with Kò͘ Chheng-hông's lyrical experimentation
