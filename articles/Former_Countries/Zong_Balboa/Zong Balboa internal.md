# Zong Balboa — Internal Notes

## Article Overview
- **File**: `Zong Balboa.mediawiki`
- **Subject**: Zongese dependent territory on the Balboa isthmus (1899–1950)
- **Period**: 1899–1950
- **Scope**: Full history from establishment through Shining Star era, Great Adonian War siege, and dissolution; expanded culture section with invented lore

## Core Facts
- **Formed by**: Treaty of Sant Lázaro (1899), following Second War of the Isthmus
- **Territory**: Sant Cristòfor + Balboa Canal zone (including Portnou)
- **Capital**: Sant Cristòfor
- **Governance**: Zongese-appointed governor + local Deliberative Assembly (est. 1912)
- **Canal revenue**: All remitted to Zong; territory benefited from commercial activity
- **Dissolved**: 1950 → Adonian Community mandate → returned to Balboa 1960

## Hokkien Name
- 巴波亞屬地 (*Pa-po-a Sio̍k-tē*) = "Balboa Dependency"
- Formal English: "Dependent State of Balboa and Sant Cristòfor"

## Demographic Transformation
- **Pre-1899**: White/mixed Balboan merchant majority + Zongese minority
- **Post-1899**: Balboan planter class departed; influx of:
  - Black Balboans (escaping persecution in Balboa interior)
  - Sinchewese lower-class workers (port/canal labour)
  - Zongese middle class and elites (administration, commerce)
- **By 1920s**: Three principal communities — Zongese elite, Sinchewese working class, Black Balboan majority
- Egalitarian laws were deliberately designed to attract Black Balboan workers away from Balboa (both humanitarian and self-interested)

### Ethnic Composition Shift (Wartime)
- **Key dynamic**: Zongese elites could evacuate by sea (naval convoys, connections, resources); Sinchewese/Black Balboan working class could not leave — remained and bore brunt of siege
- Post-war population became much more Black/brown because Zongese elites departed and mostly did not return

| Ethnic group | 1934 | 1947 | 1950 |
|-------------|------|------|------|
| Black Balboan | 42% | 58% | 55% |
| Mixed Balboan (brown) | 22% | 24% | 25% |
| Sinchewese | 18% | 14% | 15% |
| Zongese | 15% | 2% | 3% |
| White Balboan | 3% | 2% | 2% |

- Black + mixed Balboan: 64% pre-war → 82% post-war
- Zongese collapse: 15% → 2%; only fraction returned (post-war devastation, loss of canal, dissolution offered no incentive)
- 1950 Zongese 3% = mostly administrators/technical personnel retained by mandate authority, not former commercial elite
- Post-war population recovery (1.75M → 1.9M) driven by Black/mixed Balboan returnees + some Sinchewese workers

### Literature (all invented)
- **Kò͘ Chheng-hông** (辜清鴻): Zongese-born poet; collection ''Hái-kiap Chhiú-kì'' (海峽手記, "Isthmus Notes", 1927) — blended Hokkien lyric forms with Volisanian and Balboan Creole influences; foundational work of Lurandian Zongese literature. Died of malnutrition during siege, 1943.
- **Marcos Aldana**: Black Balboan novelist; ''The Crossing'' (1931) — family's journey from rural Balboan plantation to Sant Cristòfor; banned in Balboa, translated into Hokkien and Volisanian. Departed for Zhongyuan before the war; did not return.

### Visual Arts (all invented)
- **Sant Cristòfor School** of painting (c. 1915–1935): Combined Zongese ink-wash, Volisanian colonial colour palettes, Sinchewese decorative motifs. Principal figures:
  - **Tân Êng-kok** (陳英國)
  - **Luisa Vargas**
  - **Phạm Đức Minh**
- Paintings largely destroyed or dispersed during the war; few survive outside the territory

### Music and Performance (all invented)
- **Canal District**: Mixed neighbourhood along canal approach; known for nightlife, informal musical performances, fusion of Black Balboan plantation music with Zongese and Sinchewese elements
- **Sant Cristòfor Municipal Theatre** (built 1921): Principal formal venue; hosted Zongese opera, Volisanian classical companies, visiting Sanese performers; administered by municipal arts council (Deliberative Assembly-appointed); subsidised by canal-derived municipal revenue. Destroyed by aerial bombing, 1938.

### Cuisine
- Creole cuisine mixing Zongese, Sinchewese, Volisanian, and Black Balboan traditions
- **Olloc**: EXISTING dish (from talod.miraheze.org/wiki/Olloc) — Zongese-Balboan soup: broth, rice noodles, herbs, meat (beef=olloc vadella, chicken=olloc pollastre). Originated at Xef Cafe in Sant Cristòfor. Name from Zongese shortening of Volisanian "olla de foc" (pot of fire). Now enjoyed worldwide.
- **Xef Cafe**: EXISTING restaurant — previously known for pork stews, began serving beef/offal rice noodle dishes adapted from Jade Peninsula noodle soup traditions

- Black Balboan students secured scholarships to metropolitan Zong universities

## Key Dynamics
- **Egalitarianism as competitive weapon**: Universal suffrage + anti-discrimination distinguished Zong Balboa from repressive Balboa; drained Balboa's workforce
- **Canal revenue extraction**: All canal fees went to Zong; territory's wealth came from being the trade hub (port, warehousing, services)
- **Rapid industrialisation**: Zong-funded capital investment in 1910s–1920s; factories (processed goods, textiles, construction materials) in Sant Cristòfor; expanded plantations/farms along canal zone with better salaries than Balboan interior
- **Labour migration patterns**: Black Balboans → canal zone plantations/farms (better wages); mixed + some Black Balboans → city factories
- **"Shining star of Adonia"**: Phrase attributed to contemporary observers; peak era ~1920s–early 1930s
- **Social stratification vs. legal equality**: The "two cities" — built-up skyscraper core vs. hastily built exurbs; no race riots but persistent tensions; Zongese population avoided poorer neighbourhoods
- **Housing crisis**: Territory tripled in population 1910–1934; informal slums/exurbs housed majority of Sinchewese + Black Balboan populations; 1931 social housing programme + slum clearance announced, construction began 1932, interrupted by war 1935
- **Canal zone**: More rural; plantations + informal conurbations around lock stations, maintenance depots, processing facilities
- **Cultural destruction**: Most cultural infrastructure destroyed during siege — Theatre (1938), newspaper archives, Academy converted to hospital, Kò͘ Chheng-hông died, Aldana departed, Sant Cristòfor School paintings lost

## Great Adonian War (1935–1947)
- **GAW opened with the Balboa Campaign**: Secret plan by Volisania + Balboa to invade
- **Canal zone lost** in opening weeks (insufficient defences outside city walls)
- **Sant Cristòfor held**: Walled city + Zongese naval supply lines via Kendriyan approaches
- **Siege conditions**: Heavy bombing, famine, rationing; Deliberative Assembly continued under constraints
- **War ended 1947**: Volisanian fascist government collapsed under liberal forces
- **Territory devastated**: Infrastructure destroyed, population diminished

## Post-War (1947–1950)
- Zong did not press for restoration of pre-war territorial arrangement
- 1950: Formally dissolved → Adonian Community mandate
- Mandate tasked with: canal reconstruction, resettlement, determining permanent status
- 1960: Returned to Balboa
