# Marcos Aldana — Internal Notes

## Overview
Black Balboan novelist (c. 1898–1959). Author of The Crossing (1931), The Exurb (1938), Remedios and Others (1946). Foundational figure of Black Balboan literature. Lived in Sant Cristòfor 1914–1935, then in Huwei 1935–1959.

## Invented Details

### Biography
- Born c. 1898, rural Balboan interior ("a cotton place, two days' walk from the provincial capital")
- Parents: plantation labourers (names not given)
- Self-taught literacy from discarded newspapers and shipping crate labels
- Migrated to Sant Cristòfor 1914 (age ~16)
- First work: canal zone labourer (1914–1917)
- Typesetter at El Faro del Istmo (1917+)
- First published story: "The Box" ("La Caja"), New Horizon, 1922
- ~20 short stories published 1922–1930 in New Horizon, Seng-kì-pò, Chương Châu Nhật Báo
- Departed Sant Cristòfor 1935 on Zong naval evacuation vessel
- Lecturer in Lurandian literature, University of Huwei (1936–1946)
- Died 14 February 1959, Huwei, respiratory failure
- Buried in Huwei Municipal Cemetery (foreign residents section)
- Memorial plaque at Sant Cristòfor Academy site, 1965

### Family
- Married **Chen Su-lêng** (陳素瓊), a Zong schoolteacher in Sant Cristòfor public school system, 1926
- Interracial marriage — legal under anti-discrimination statutes but uncommon; attracted comment in both communities
- Two children; only daughter named: **Chen Hui-lân Aldana** (b. 1928) — became the first mixed-race member of the Parliament of Zong
- Son's name and details not established
- Family lived in harbour-front district (not exurbs, not western quarter)
- Entire family departed together on Zong naval vessel in 1935

### Departure controversy
- Left on Zong naval vessel before siege lines closed
- Critics: abandonment of the community he documented
- Aldana's response: "The Writer and the Siege" (1948 essay) — "I wrote about the crossing. I did not expect to make it again. The people I wrote about could not leave. I could. I left. I have no defence and I offer none. The work is what remains."
- Never returned to Sant Cristòfor or Balboan interior

### Works

**Novels:**
1. The Crossing (El Cruce) — 1931 — covered in dedicated article
2. The Exurb (El Arrabal) — 1938 — single-day novel set in Sant Cristòfor exurbs; influenced by Zong "day novel" (日記小說, ji̍t-kì sió-soat) tradition; small edition, many copies destroyed in siege; not reprinted until 1955; some critics regard it as more accomplished than The Crossing
3. Remedios and Others (Remedios y Otros) — 1946 — border crossing from Remedios's perspective; each chapter a different crossing; written in exile in Huwei; Lîm Tek-kian: "the work of a man writing from the far side of his own life"

**Short story collections:**
1. The Canal and Other Stories (El Canal y Otros Cuentos) — 1941 — canal zone memories
2. Letters from the Western Quarter (Cartas del Barrio Oeste) — 1951 — epistolary stories, Black Balboan clerk in Zong merchant's office (related to Dolores Aldava character)

**Selected criticism:**
- "The Language of the Crossing" (1943) — Creole as distinct literary language, not Volisan dialect
- "The Writer and the Siege" (1948) — departure justification
- "Zong and Creole: Two Literatures of the Isthmus" (1952) — comparative essay
- "What Remains: The Shining Star Ten Years After" (1957) — legacy essay

### Key relationships
- **Lîm Tek-kian** (林德謙) — Zong critic who wrote influential assessments of both The Crossing and Remedios and Others
- **Kò͘ Chheng-hông** — Zong contemporary; contrasting literary style (lyrical vs. realist)
- **Portnou Press** — publisher of The Crossing
- **New Horizon** — primary venue for early stories; published special issue on The Crossing

## Key Dynamics
- **Legal equality vs. material inequality**: Central theme across all works
- **Writer's distance from community**: The departure from Sant Cristòfor became a defining moral question; the later work is self-conscious about this distance
- **Creole as literary language**: Political act of writing in Balboan Creole; "The Language of the Crossing" essay argued for its recognition as distinct literary language
- **Style shift**: Sant Cristòfor period = direct realism; Huwei period = reflective, essayistic, meditative
- **Day novel influence**: The Exurb structured after Zong literary tradition (ji̍t-kì sió-soat) — shows Aldana engaging with Zong literary forms
