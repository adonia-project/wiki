# Adonian Community Mandate of the Balboa Canal Zone — Internal Notes

## Overview
AC mandate (1950–1973). 30km corridor around the Balboa Canal + all territory south of the canal, including Portnou and Sant Llàtzer. Area: 5,914.223 km². Directly administered by AC through appointed High Commissioner. Primary purpose: reconstruct and operate the Balboa Canal. Returned to Balboa 1973 (Treaty of Areza), except Areza which remains international.

## Geography
- **30km corridor** around the canal (both sides)
- **All territory south of the canal** (including agricultural land)
- **Portnou**: western terminus of canal, only significant urban centre
- **Lake Alvarado**: artificial lake on the canal; Areza on north shore
- **Areza enclave**: carved out 1960, self-governing, not under High Commissioner's authority

## High Commissioners
1. **Jürgen Heidemann** (1950–1958) — Breislandic civil engineer; directed canal reconstruction
2. **Fadumo Mahadala** (1958–1967) — Burawi diplomat; managed civilian grievances and canal expansion
3. **Pere Rovira** (1967–1973) — Volisan jurist; negotiated Treaty of Areza and transition

## Governance
- Direct AC administration via High Commissioner (executive authority over all matters)
- **No civilian representation** — unlike Sant Cristòfor mandate, no Consultative Assembly
- Three directorates:
  - **Directorate of Canal Operations** (largest, best-funded) — lock systems, dredging, traffic control
  - **Directorate of Reconstruction** — physical repair of canal infrastructure
  - **Directorate of Civil Affairs** — civilian population, courts, municipal services
- Canal-first priorities: civilian services (housing, education, healthcare) underfunded relative to Sant Cristòfor mandate
- Source of persistent civilian grievance, especially in Portnou

## Canal Reconstruction
- Funded by **General War Reparations Fund** (Treaty of Klow) + AC member state contributions
- **Phase 1 (1950–1953)**: Debris clearance, lock repair, channel dredging; workforce from civilian population + contracted labour from Sant Cristòfor mandate
- **Phase 2 (1953–1956)**: Warehouses, loading facilities, signalling systems, admin buildings
- **Phase 3 (1956–1958)**: Channel deepening, modern traffic control, permanent operations staff training
- **Partial operation**: 1953 (limited commercial traffic through eastern locks)
- **Full operation**: 1956
- **Traffic volume**: ~80% of pre-war by 1958
- Canal toll revenue retained by mandate → fiscal independence (unlike Sant Cristòfor mandate)

## Population & Economy
- 1950: ~280,000 → 1960: ~420,000
- **Portnou**: western terminus of canal, largest urban centre; 90,000 (1950) → 150,000 (1960)
- **Sant Llàtzer**: eastern terminus of canal, eastern entrance; second town; primary civilian admin centre during early reconstruction; less heavily damaged than Portnou
- Termination ceremony held in Sant Llàtzer (not Portnou)
- Ethnic composition: **more white/black, less mixed** than Sant Cristòfor; sharp racial divide; white Balboan (Volisan colonial descendants) significant in Portnou commercial/professional quarters; Black Balboan majority in agriculture south of canal + working-class Portnou; mixed Balboan proportionally smaller (canal zone less cosmopolitan than city); Sinchewese small (harbour workforce only); Zong negligible (almost all departed)
- South of canal: predominantly Black Balboan agricultural communities
- Agricultural transition: same as Sant Cristòfor — Zong owners departed, Black Balboan workers/supervisors took over; mandate didn't intervene
- Economy dominated by canal operations + Portnou harbour/services

## Areza
- Established by Treaty of Balboa Harbour (1960)
- On north shore of Lake Alvarado
- Permanently neutral; independent of Balboa; own governance, military base (AC Garrison Areza), tribunals, AC mandate law
- AC Assembly permanently seated there (building completed 1973)
- Status in perpetuity — no expiry
- Created territorial anomaly within mandate: Areza enclave not under High Commissioner's authority

## Termination
- **Treaty of Areza** (1973) — returned canal zone to Balboa (except Areza)
- Canal transferred to Balboan ownership/operation; tolls/tariffs subject to AC Canal Commission review
- Portnou + civilian settlements transferred to Balboan civil administration (Twelve Guarantees apply)
- Areza excluded from transfer — remains international
- ICPF retains mandate in canal zone + Areza
- Formal termination: 1 July 1973
- Civilian reception: cautious acceptance (unlike 1960 Sant Cristòfor silent protest) — population chafed under canal-first priorities

## Key Dynamics
- **Canal-first governance**: Mandate existed to serve the canal, not the civilian population; contrast with Sant Cristòfor mandate's civilian focus
- **No representation**: No Consultative Assembly; no civilian voice in governance; major grievance
- **Fiscal independence from reparations fund**: Canal tolls funded mandate operations; less dependent on AC allocations than Sant Cristòfor
- **Areza anomaly**: International city within mandate territory but outside High Commissioner's authority
- **Longer duration than Sant Cristòfor mandate**: 1950–1973 (23 years) vs. 1950–1960 (10 years); reflects canal's strategic importance
- **Agricultural transition**: Same "second men" pattern as Sant Cristòfor but mandate didn't intervene or formalise it
