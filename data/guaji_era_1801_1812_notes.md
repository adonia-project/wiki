# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1801–1812 (PD Governance and Northern Confederation Invasion)

## Overview

Period from end of Volisanian Civil War (1801) through Northern Confederation Invasion of Guajicutea (1812). PD enacts sweeping constitutional reforms: universal citizenship (all races), representative assembly based on population, directly elected president. PD government under President Mateo Kurí severs ties with Lacashe (anti-slavery), League dissolves (1807). NR splits: ANG (reformist business), old NR (rump), NR exiles (radicals who go to Northern Confederation). NC declares independence from Volisania (1808). NR exiles led by Ramón Cifuentes plot coup with NC/Hesperia support. 1812: NC naval squadron invades, 2 months, president arrested, Guaji becomes NC colony. Violent aftermath, years of internal rebellions.

## Constitutional Amendment of 1803

Three fundamental changes:
1. **Universal citizenship**: All residents — Sartan, Guaji, Kaftian, Bayani — granted full citizenship and political rights. Property qualification removed for all adult citizens. Women excluded.
2. **Representative assembly**: Senate (2 per distrito regardless of population) → Asamblea Nacional (seats allocated by population). Shifted power to denser rural/urban areas (PD base).
3. **Separate executive**: Cónsul (elected by senate) → Presidente (directly elected by citizens). 4-year term. Separate executive branch.

- NR opposed all three changes.
- ANG (not yet formally split from NR) supported enfranchisement but opposed assembly restructuring and direct executive as too radical.

## President: Mateo Kurí

- First directly elected president (1804).
- Guaji orator and organiser, rose through PD parish network infrastructure.
- First Guaji to serve as head of state.
- Implemented PD domestic programme: universal citizenship registration, proportional assembly, secular education, infrastructure.
- Enacted PD anti-slavery foreign policy: severed alliance with Lacashe.
- Arrested during 1812 invasion.
- Name: Sartan first name + Guaji surname. Kurí = kuri (stone) + -rí (person of) = "person of the stone."

## NR Coup Leader: Ramón Cifuentes

- Former NR senator and landowner.
- Led NR exiles to Northern Confederation to seek coup support.
- Argued PD government illegitimate, reforms imposed without consensus, rupture with Lacashe isolated Guaji diplomatically.
- Cifuentes = established Sartan elite family name (connects to Cónsuls Hernán Cifuentes #5 and Lucas Cifuentes #15).

## Foreign Policy and League Dissolution

- PD anti-slavery position (core plank since Makurí founded movement).
- Severed alliance with Lacashe (slave-holding republic). Cornerstone of Guaji foreign policy since 1723 removed.
- League (founded 1764) undermined by Guaji-Lacashe rupture. Two founding members estranged. Anti-piracy cooperation deteriorated.
- League formally dissolved 1807.
- Piracy resurged without league patrols.

## Political Realignment

NR splits into three factions:

1. **Asociación Nuevo Guaji (ANG)**: Reformist, business/merchant interests. Accepts reforms, competes in elections. Similar to PD on economics but advocates: non-military constabulary (civilian police), non-religious secular education. Gains seats. Pragmatic alternative to PD.
2. **Old NR**: Rump, continues opposing reforms, shrinks electorally. Small presence in Asamblea Nacional.
3. **NR exiles**: Radicals who refuse to accept reforms. Leave Guajicutea, go to Northern Confederation. Seek foreign support for coup. Led by Ramón Cifuentes.

- Three-party system replaces old NR-PD binary.
- ANG occupies middle ground.

## Northern Confederation

- Declared independence from Volisania 1808.
- Treaty of Daramazán (1809) formally recognised.
- Union of Baliscan republics: Hesperia, Cerdanya, and others.
- Hesperia = major commercial/maritime republic within NC. Business interests in Capuyaquiran Sea.
- NC is confederal, decentralised structure.
- Language: Baliscano (creole).
- Bayara's business interests drove the invasion: PD anti-slavery stance and league dissolution seen as obstacles to trade.

## Invasion of Guajicutea (1812)

- NC naval squadron arrives in Guajicutean waters, carrying Cifuentes and supporters.
- Primarily driven by Bayara's business interests.
- Contentious within NC — some member states opposed intervention.
- Lasted 2 months.
- NC forces + NR sympathisers vs. Guajicutean military units loyal to government.
- Guajicutean forces weakened: reduced military spending under PD, league naval cooperation dissolved.
- Government overthrown. President Kurí arrested. Asamblea Nacional dissolved.
- Guajicutea placed under NC colonial rule.
- Seen internationally as show of force by NC.

## Aftermath

- Asamblea Nacional dissolved. Re-elections under NC occupation forces.
- Partido Unidad Guajicuteana formed — supports occupation government.
- Platform: endorses NC authority, colonial arrangement.
- Invasion was violent. Years of internal rebellions followed.
- PD supporters and opponents of foreign rule mounted insurgencies in rural districtos.

## Key Figures

- **Mateo Kurí** — President (1804–1812). PD. First Guaji head of state. Charismatic orator. Arrested in 1812 invasion.
- **Ramón Cifuentes** — NR exile leader. Former NR senator/landowner. Led coup plot with NC support.
- **Esteban Makurí** — PD founder (referenced). Founded movement in late 1760s. Kurí succeeded his political project.

## New Entities/Concepts Introduced

- **Constitutional Amendment of 1803** — universal citizenship, Asamblea Nacional, separate executive presidency.
- **Asamblea Nacional** — representative assembly replacing senate. Seats by population.
- **Presidente** — directly elected president, head of separate executive branch. 4-year term.
- **Asociación Nuevo Guaji (ANG)** — reformist NR split. Business interests. Non-military constabulary, secular education.
- **Partido Unidad Guajicuteana** — occupation party. Supports NC colonial government.
- **Invasion of Guajicutea (1812)** — NC naval invasion. 2 months. Overthrew elected government.
- **Northern Confederation** — Baliscan confederal state, independent from Volisania (1808). Founding members: Hesperia, Cerdanya, others.

## Internal Notes (not for wiki)

- **PD governance period**: 1801–1812. First time PD held executive power. Previous eras had PD as opposition movement.
- **Lacashe rupture**: Exact date uncertain. Likely 1805–1806, after Kurí takes office and implements anti-slavery foreign policy.
- **League dissolution**: Consequence of Guaji-Lacashe rupture. League couldn't function without its two founding members cooperating.
- **NC independence**: 1808 declaration, 1809 Treaty of Daramazán. NC is Baliscan (Caribbean-equivalent) entity.
- **Hesperia's role**: Commercial/maritime interests drove invasion. Contentious within NC — not all member states supported.
- **Balboa**: Volisanian viceroyalty, stayed loyal to Volisania. Did not join NC. Opposed invasion.
- **Tapuya**: Independent 1811, separate from NC. Supported NC invasion of Guaji.
- **Asikyira**: Opposed invasion. Removed consul. Former League co-founder.
- **Women**: Still excluded from suffrage after 1803 amendment. "All adult citizens" construed to mean men only.
- **Next era**: Post-1812 occupation period. NC colonial rule. Internal rebellions. Partido Unidad Guajicuteana. Duration uncertain.
