# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1630–1683 (Sarta Colony, Consolidation Period)

## Overview

Five decades of relative stability and growth under Sartan colonial rule. Colony transitions from fragile outpost to established colonial society. Normalization with Burawa (1634), maturation of Volisanian trade, expansion to Breisland/Mercia trade. Naval-industrial infrastructure: Astillero Real de Waribá (royal shipyard, 1638), Castillo de San Esteban (citadel fort, 1642–1656). Population grows ~60k → ~80k. Cultural fusion: Sartan-Guaji language, Guaji Catholicism (syncretic religion), first colonial literature, Kari Yabí transcription. Five governors. Era ends 1683 with onset of Sarta-Volisania War.

## Normalization with Burawa

- War of the Mesogean ended 1610. Burawa never formally normalized with Sarta over Guajicutea.
- 1617 Petran Church intervention was Volisania only — Burawa didn't participate.
- By early 1630s, pragmatism prevails. Burawa's focus shifted to New Burawa, Aruanãyára consolidation.
- Kingdom of Murani (Burawi protectorate) served as de facto intermediary for informal trade.
- 1634: Burawa-Sarta normalization agreement. De facto recognition. Trade legalized. Burawi merchants return to Waribá (merchant quarter). Burawi Catholic Church NOT permitted to return.
- Not warm — Burawa still regards loss of Guajicutea as strategic setback. Commercial, not political, relationship.

## Murani Relations

- Murani and Guaji = both Capuyaquiran peoples. Connected through Caracurí maritime tradition, trade, intermarriage.
- Different colonial spheres: Murani under Burawa, Guajicutea under Sarta.
- Cross-strait trade, family visits, cultural exchange continue despite political boundary.
- Murani = cultural refuge. Burawi protectorate lighter than Sartan colonial rule. Guaji traditional practices (lunar calendar, rituals, oral traditions) survive more openly in Murani.
- Guaji who want to maintain traditional practices connect with Murani relatives.

## Volisania Trade

- 1617 Petran Church deal trade relationship matures through 1630s.
- Volisania's western Capuyaquiran colonies (Boraku, Kartak, Balboa) = natural trade network.
- Shared Petran Catholic tradition = diplomatic foundation.
- Not warm — Volisania hasn't forgotten being outmaneuvered. Sartan sovereignty not formally recognized.
- Commercially practical. Exports: manufactured goods, textiles, metals. Imports: agricultural products, dried fish, artisanal goods. Flows through Waribá.

## Breisland and Mercia Trade

- Breisland's Lurandia colony (~1500km) = former embargo-era resupply point, now regular trade lane.
- Breislandic goods: iron tools, textiles, manufactured items. Also cultural influences: architecture, foodways, craft traditions.
- Mercia = smaller volume but adds another layer. Different goods and cultural influences.
- Diversification reduces vulnerability to any single trade partner. Ends isolation of embargo years.

## Naval and Military Infrastructure

### Astillero Real de Waribá (Royal Shipyard of Waribá)
- Established 1638 under Governor Gabriel Soto. First dry dock + repair wharf.
- Purpose: reduce dependence on Lurandia (~1500km) for ship repair. Embargo showed vulnerability.
- 1651: Expanded under Governor Vargas. Second dry dock + full shipbuilding capacity. Colony starts producing own vessels.
- 1660s: Regular production of colonial vessels — trade ships, patrol vessels.
- Merged two shipbuilding traditions:
  - Sartan: caravel/galleon construction, iron fastening, Illypnian hull design
  - Guaji: Yabikú joinery (interlocking carved joints, tighten under load), local timber/resin/caulking knowledge
- Hybrid vessel type: Sartan hull forms + Guaji joinery reinforcements, local tropical hardwoods (rot/teredo resistant)
- Largest industrial installation in Capuyaquiran Sea. Major employer in Waribá. Site of cultural exchange.

### Castillo de San Esteban (Castle of Saint Stephen)
- Citadel fort complex on promontory overlooking Waribá harbor.
- Construction: 1642 (began under Governor Soto) – 1656 (completed under Governor Cifuentes).
- Purpose: defend Waribá (primary port, naval base, entry point) against naval attack from Burawa or Volisania.
- Design: stone walls, four bastions, gun batteries covering harbor approaches.
- Capacity: garrison ~500 soldiers, governor's wing (emergency use), powder magazines, cistern system (freshwater for siege).
- Largest fortification in Capuyaquiran Sea. Surpassed Volisanian fort at Barikú and any Burawi installation.
- Demonstrated Sartan crown's commitment to holding colony permanently.
- Construction: Guaji labor + Guaji stoneworkers (descendants of catacomb corridor craftsmen). Volcanic highland stone. Fusion of Sartan military architecture + Guaji masonry.

## Cultural Fusion

### Language
- Sartan dominant in cities, trade, religion, government (language policy since 1609).
- Two generations of Sartan-dominant speakers in cities by 1683.
- Guaji survives in rural areas, fishing communities, among elders.
- Urban Guaji bilingual or Sartan-dominant. Rural Guaji maintain language with varying Sartan influence.
- Murani = reservoir for Guaji language (less colonial pressure under Burawi protectorate).
- Internal note: trajectory toward modern Spanish by ~1700 well advanced by 1683.

### Religion — Guaji Catholicism
- Sartan Petran Catholic monopoly on formal institutions.
- Guaji spiritual traditions absorbed into syncretic practice = Guaji Catholicism.
- Sea/mountain/sky → Trinity mapping (started during Burawi period, deepens under Sartan rule).
- Cremation vs. burial tension persists. Hybrid practice emerges: Catholic funeral rite + cremation. Ashes kept/scattered rather than buried.
- Lunar calendar survives in rural agricultural/fishing communities (practical: tides, planting, storms). Solar calendar governs official life.
- By 1683: Guaji Catholicism = popular-level syncretism, institutional-level resistance from church hierarchy.

### Literature
- First written works by Guaji-born authors in Sartan. Histories, poetry, religious texts.
- Kari Yabí transcription into Sartan (c. 1650s) = major cultural moment. Oral/Bikumaku record → written Sartan. Bridges oral and written traditions.
- Other works: colonial-era histories, religious poetry (Petran + Guaji imagery), practical texts (agriculture, navigation, medicine combining Sartan and Guaji knowledge).

### Material Culture
- Architecture: Sartan stone/brick/tile + Guaji wood/bahareque. Urban landscape of three cities increasingly Sartan-style admin buildings, churches, residences alongside Guaji structures.
- Clothing: Sartan textiles/tailoring + Guaji lightweight tropical preferences. Fusion garments.
- Food: Sartan imports (wheat, olive oil, wine) + Guaji staples (cassava, yams, makú, fish). Distinct colonial cuisine.
- Naming: -an suffix feudal names fade in urban contexts → Sartan given + family names. Rural areas maintain older conventions longer.

## Key established details

- **Burawa-Sarta normalization**: 1634. De facto recognition. Trade legalized. Burawi merchants return to Waribá. Burawi Catholic Church stays out.
- **Astillero Real de Waribá**: Royal Shipyard. Est. 1638 (first dry dock/repair). Expanded 1651 (shipbuilding capacity). 1660s producing colonial vessels. Sartan + Guaji (Yabikú) hybrid shipbuilding. Largest industrial installation in Capuyaquiran Sea.
- **Castillo de San Esteban**: Citadel fort. Construction 1642–1656. On Waribá promontory. Stone walls, 4 bastions, garrison ~500, governor's wing, powder magazines, cisterns. Largest fortification in Capuyaquiran Sea. Guaji stoneworkers + volcanic highland stone.
- **Murani**: Burawi protectorate, cultural refuge for Guaji traditions, cross-strait trade conduit.
- **Volisania trade**: Post-1617 maturation. Commercial, not warm. Through Waribá.
- **Breisland/Mercia**: Trade diversification. Lurandia route. Cultural influences.
- **Population**: ~60k (1630) → ~80k (1683). Arrival cap 500/year maintained. ~24/km² density.
- **Kari Yabí transcription**: c. 1650s. First time old Guaji stories written in colonial language.
- **Guaji Catholicism**: Syncretic religion. Sea/mountain/sky → Trinity. Cremation hybrid. Lunar calendar rural survival.
- **Governors**: Gabriel Soto (1630–1640), Hernán Vargas (1640–1651), Rodrigo Cifuentes (1651–1663), Mateo Salazar (1663–1674), Felipe Ortega (1674–1683).
- **Era end**: 1683. Sarta-Volisania War begins (lasts 18 years, Sarta loses coastal access). Next era.
