# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1812–1824 (NC Occupation, Bloody Decade, and Virtuous Revolution)

## Overview

Period from Northern Confederation invasion (1812) through Virtuous Revolution (1824). NC establishes military occupation with PUG as civilian face. Race codes, miscegenation laws, and antiliberal laws reverse 1803 reforms. Resistance coalesces as Guarda Revolutionary — guerrilla movement operating from jungle interior. Bloody Decade (1814–1822): sustained conflict, state violence (public beheadings, house burnings), national trauma. June 1824: Pastry War erupts within NC (Bayara vs Hesperia). Hesperian troops withdrawn from Guajicutea. Power vacuum enables Virtuous Revolution (late 1824): Guarda Revolutionary emerges from jungles, popular uprisings in cities, PUG collapses, self-governance restored.

## Occupation Government

- **Military governor**: Capitán-General appointed by NC. Supreme authority.
  - Vittorio Calvi (Bayaran, 1812–1820): Implemented race codes, oversaw early Bloody Decade
  - Marco Vendrell (Cerdanyan, 1820–1824): Oversaw late Bloody Decade, troop withdrawal
- **Civilian head**: Ramón Cifuentes (NR exile leader). PUG civilian government. No electoral mandate.
- **Advisory council**: Appointed. PUG members + Sartan landowners. Replaced dissolved Asamblea Nacional.
- **Garrison**: NC troops at Waribá and Tabakurí. ~2,000–3,000 troops (fluctuating).
- **Authority**: Effective in coastal cities and central valleys. Jungle interior beyond control.

## Race Codes (Códigos de Raza, 1813)

- Racial classification: Baliscan, Sartan, Guaji, Bayani, Kaftian
- Voting restricted to Baliscan and Sartan citizens only
- Reversed 1803 universal citizenship
- Property ownership restrictions for non-Baliscan/non-Sartan
- Movement permits required for non-Baliscan/non-Sartan traveling between districtos
- Baliscan added to dominant class alongside Sartan (NC settlers/administrators)

## Miscegenation Laws (Leyes de Mestizaje, 1813)

- Prohibited marriages between Baliscan/Sartan and Guaji/Bayani/Kaftian
- Existing interracial marriages declared invalid
- Criminal penalties: imprisonment, forfeiture of property
- Targeted the mixed Sartan-Guaji class that had developed over centuries

## Antiliberal Laws (Leyes Antiliberales, 1813)

- PD banned as political organization
- ANG activities restricted
- Press censorship: prior authorisation required
- Public assembly >10 persons requires permit
- Secular education dismantled, Church control of schools restored
- 1803 constitutional reforms fully revoked

## Guarda Revolutionary

- **Formation**: 1813–1814, in response to race codes and antiliberal laws
- **Composition**: Former Guajicutean military units (refused surrender), dissolved PD members, Guaji community leaders, smaller numbers of Bayani and Kaftian fighters
- **Base**: Jungle interior — volcanic highlands, central valleys, dense tropical vegetation
- **Tactics**: Hit-and-run attacks on NC supply lines, PUG offices, occupation infrastructure. Knowledge of difficult terrain. Seasonal operations (active dry months, reduced storm season).
- **Support**: Rural Guaji communities provided food, shelter, intelligence
- **Significance**: Early example of sustained guerrilla warfare in Capuyaquiran Sea region
- **Role in revolution**: Emerged from jungles in late 1824, moved toward coastal cities, key military force in Virtuous Revolution
- **Post-revolution**: Transitioned from guerrilla movement to political organisation

## Bloody Decade (Década Sangrienta, 1814–1822)

- Sustained conflict between Guarda Revolutionary and NC occupation forces + PUG militias
- **State violence tactics**:
  - Public beheadings: Captured Guarda fighters executed in town plazas. Attended by occupation soldiers and PUG officials. Intended as deterrent.
  - House burnings: Collective punishment. Villages suspected of supporting Guarda had dwellings and food stores destroyed.
- **Geography**: Concentrated in rural districtos with significant Guaji populations (central valleys, highland foothills). Coastal cities under occupation control — lower direct violence but political repression, surveillance, movement restrictions.
- **Pattern**: Cycle of guerrilla attacks → state reprisals → civilian displacement → more support for resistance
- **Peak**: 1816–1820
- **Outcome**: Guarda weakened but not defeated. Maintained jungle interior control throughout.
- **Legacy**: National trauma. Shaped subsequent political culture and attitudes toward foreign intervention.

## Economic Conditions

- Trade routes redirected to serve NC commercial interests (especially Bayara)
- Astillero Real de Waribá under NC military control, repurposed for naval maintenance/repair
- Taxation increased to support occupation garrison
- Agricultural production disrupted (fields abandoned, farmers displaced/conscripted)
- Food shortages in coastal cities (dependent on rural production)
- Manufacturing sector declined (NC favoured raw material export over local manufacturing)
- Pre-occupation light manufacturing (developed during republican period) deteriorated

## Pastry War and Troop Withdrawal

- **June 1824**: Pastry War (Guerra de Pastisceri) erupts within NC
- Bayara vs Hesperia — intercommunal tensions in Estuarian wetlands, disputes over confederal power
- Triggered by murder of Volisanian pastry chef in Xàbia, escalating reprisals
- Year-long conflict, ended by Navassan Charter
- **Impact on Guajicutea**: NC forced to redeploy troops from external commitments
  - Hesperian troops withdrawn from Guajicutea garrison
  - Bayaran garrison reduced below control threshold
  - Capitán-General Vendrell oversaw withdrawal
- Power vacuum: PUG unable to maintain authority without garrison support

## Virtuous Revolution (Revolución Virtuosa, late 1824)

- Triggered by troop withdrawal (Pastry War)
- Guarda Revolutionary emerged from jungle interior, moved toward coastal cities
- Popular uprisings in Tabakurí and Waribá — organised by underground PD networks that survived antiliberal laws
- PUG government collapsed without military support
- Ramón Cifuentes fled Guajicutea
- NC unable to respond (consumed by Pastry War)
- Minimal bloodshed — remaining occupation forces withdrew rather than fight
- Name: "Virtuous" = moral legitimacy of resistance vs illegitimate occupation. Restoration of sovereignty, not a coup.
- 1803 constitutional framework restored (universal citizenship, Asamblea Nacional, elected presidency)

## Key Figures

- **Capitán-General Vittorio Calvi** — Bayaran naval officer. NC military governor 1812–1820. Implemented race codes, oversaw early Bloody Decade.
- **Capitán-General Marco Vendrell** — Cerdanyan military officer. NC military governor 1820–1824. Oversaw late Bloody Decade and troop withdrawal.
- **Ramón Cifuentes** — NR exile leader, coup orchestrator. Installed as PUG civilian head. Fled during Virtuous Revolution.
- **Mateo Kurí** — Arrested president (1804–1812). Exiled to Sarta in 1813. Remained in Sarta throughout occupation. Symbolic humiliation — returned to former colonial power.
- **Tomás Yukurí** — Guaji, former Guajicutean army officer. Guarda Revolutionary military commander. Knowledge of highland terrain. Led military operations from jungle interior. Commanded Guarda forces during Virtuous Revolution.
- **Diego Nasarí** — Bayani, PD underground organiser. Guarda Revolutionary political commander. Directed political and underground network. Coordinated popular uprisings in Tabakurí and Waribá during Virtuous Revolution. Surname from Arabic *nasr* (victory) + -í suffix, adapted to Sartan phonology — reflects Bayani Arabic-language heritage.

## Internal Notes (not for wiki)

- **Governor names**: Vittorio Calvi (Italian-influenced, Bayaran), Marco Vendrell (Catalan-influenced, Cerdanyan). Baliscan names are a mix of Italian/Spanish/Catalan influences per TALOD wiki.
- **Capitán-General title**: Spanish colonial military governor title. Fits Sartan-influenced Guajicutea. NC may use Baliscano internally but Sartan for local administration.
- **Kurí's fate**: Exiled to Sarta in 1813. Remained in Sarta throughout occupation. Symbolic humiliation — first Guaji head of state returned to former colonial power. Alive at time of Virtuous Revolution — could return post-revolution.
- **Guarda Revolutionary leadership**: Two leaders — Tomás Yukurí (Guaji, military commander) and Diego Nasarí (Bayani, political commander). Dual structure: military + political.
- **Pastry War timeline**: June 1824 start, year-long, ended by Navassan Charter. Cerdanyan Legion administered Estuarian territories, withdrawn 1826. Compromise of 1827 failed. NC collapsed 1828 (Bayara seceded 6 July 1828).
- **NC non-response to revolution**: NC consumed by Pastry War (1824–1825), then Navassan Charter aftermath, then Compromise of 1827, then collapse 1828. No capacity to retake Guajicutea.
- **Article scope**: 1812–1824. Covers full occupation arc: invasion → occupation → resistance → liberation. "Bloody Decade" (1814–1822) is a subset of this period.
