# Governors of Guajicutea (Sarta Colony)

Colonial governors appointed by the Sartan crown to administer the Sarta Colony of Guajicutea (1609–). The colony was established in 1609 after the Sarta-Guajicutea War (1608–1609) deposed Boqor Carlos I and ended the Boqor line (1536–1609).

| # | Name | Tenure | Notes |
|---|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Diego Vela | 1609–1618 | First governor. Oversaw establishment of colonial administration, purge of feudal bikabás, church expulsion, language policy, early Bayani migration. Weathered trade embargo (1610–1617) and famine of 1612. Recalled 1618. |
| 2 | Alonso Mera | 1618–1630 | Second governor. Managed post-embargo stabilization, Petran Church trade resumption (1617), arrival cap (1618), population growth to ~60k, urbanization of three cities. |
| 3 | Gabriel Soto | 1630–1640 | Oversaw early normalization period. Burawa-Sarta normalization agreement (1634). Trade diversification begins. |
| 4 | Hernán Vargas | 1640–1651 | Consolidation of trade networks. Murani cross-strait trade formalized. Population growth steady. |
| 5 | Rodrigo Cifuentes | 1651–1663 | Cultural flowering. Kari Yabí transcription (c. 1650s). First colonial-era literature emerges. |
| 6 | Mateo Salazar | 1663–1674 | Agricultural expansion. Population pressure on land. Guaji Catholicism increasingly visible. |
| 7 | Felipe Ortega | 1674–1691 | Last crown-appointed governor of pre-war period. Longest-serving (17 years). Killed by peasant mob in Waribá, 1691. Food shortages, increased taxation. |
| — | Waraku | 1691 (3 months) | Peasant ruler, NOT a governor. Guaji peasant who seized power during Interregnum of 1691. Confined to Waribá area. |
| — | Andrés Caldera | 1692–1710 | Temporary administrator, NOT a crown-appointed governor. Sartan ship captain (Waribá squadron). Installed by Breisland-Mercia alliance. Military officer acting as civil administrator. Continued post-war until civilian rule restored 1710. |
| 8 | Tomás Herrera | 1710–1723 | Civilian governor appointed by Sartan crown after military rule. Colony under civilian administration until peaceful revolution of 1723. |

## Notes

- Governors were Sartan crown appointees, not local leaders. They replaced the Boqor (king) system and the feudal bikabári (territorial lords) as the supreme authority on the island.
- The colonial administration included a large bureaucracy and military presence, as Guajicutea was Sarta's first and only colony.
- The governor's seat was at Tabakurí, the inland capital inherited from the Boqor period.
- Provincial administrators (replacing the 16 bikabás) reported to the governor.
- During the Sarta-Volisania War (1683–1705), the crown-appointed governor system broke down. Felipe Ortega was killed in 1691, and the colony was governed by a peasant ruler (Waraku, 3 months) and then a temporary military administrator (Captain Andrés Caldera, 1692–1710).
- Civilian rule was restored in 1710. The colony remained under Sartan sovereignty until the Peaceful Revolution of 1723, when Sarta ceded control to the Padres de Guajicutea and the Republic of Guajicutea was established. The governor system was replaced by a senate and Cónsul.
