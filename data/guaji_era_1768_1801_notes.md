# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1768–1801 (Republic — Political Polarisation)

## Overview

Period from 1768 constitutional reform through end of Volisanian Civil War (1801). Defined by emergence of two competing ideologies: National Republicanismo (NR, conservative, Sartan elite) vs. Patrimonio Diversidad (PD, liberal, inclusive). Guaji-led Petran Catholic Church secretly supports PD → church-state conflict → constitutional amendment limiting church power (1784) → expropriation of missions. Citizenship debate (Kaftians, Bayani excluded). Piracy management via League. Culture, art, music flourishing. Population growth. End of Volisanian Civil War (1801) reshapes region.

## Political Ideologies

### National Republicanismo (NR)
- Foundational ideology of the republic (since 1723).
- Core principles: nationhood, social hierarchy, guarded liberalism.
- Nationhood: republic = compact among Sartan and Guaji peoples (the "founding peoples").
- Social hierarchy: accepts class structure as natural.
- Guarded liberalism: economically liberal, politically restricted.
- Party of: Sartan elite, landowners, general Sartan class.
- Defends landowning franchise, values Lacashe alliance (pragmatic), resists further democratisation.
- Treats PD's anti-slavery position as naive idealism.

### Patrimonio Diversidad (PD)
- Founded by Esteban Makurí, a Guaji native educated through parish schools.
- Makurí = makú (native Adonian tuber crop) + -rí (person of place/lineage) = "person of the makú" — encodes movement's base among farmers/plantation workers.
- Makurí trained as parish clerk, absorbed Enlightenment political thought through Church educational networks.
- Began organising through parish connections in late 1760s, publishing pamphlets in Sartan.
- Parish networks (sympathetic Guaji clergy) = infrastructure for PD's early growth.
- Spread from rural districtos to port cities (artisan/labourer communities embraced PD).
- By mid-1770s: PD competing for senate seats in several districtos. First organised challenge to NR dominance.
- Core principles: greater liberalisation, democratisation, universal citizenship.
- "Heritage of diversity" — Guaji's diversity is a heritage to celebrate, not a hierarchy to maintain.
- All peoples (Sartan, Guaji, Kaftian, Bayani) should be citizens and build society.
- Base: former farmers, plantation workers, rural labourers, urban workers.
- Opposes Lacashe alliance due to slavery.
- Secretly supported by Church (Guaji-led clergy).
- Introduces citizenship bills: 1786 (defeated), 1791 (defeated, margin narrows), 1796 (defeated, margin narrows further).

### Pre-1768: Single ideology
- Before 1768, NR was the only ideology. Senate was consensus body, not contested.
- Elections were contests among landowners sharing same assumptions.
- 1768 reform cracked consensus open → PD emerges → senate becomes contested arena.

## Church and State

### Church demographic shift
- Sartan Petran Catholic Church became increasingly Guaji in clergy over decades since 1723.
- Sartan-born priests gradually replaced by Guaji-born clergy (converts and descendants).
- By 1780s: Church hierarchy predominantly Guaji.
- Guaji clergy more sympathetic to PD's inclusion/diversity arguments.

### Church's free reign
- Constitution of 1723 made no provision for secular governance.
- Church operated schools, managed charitable institutions, held substantial urban/rural property.
- Church's educational mission spread literacy → peasant political awareness → seen by NR state as obstruction.

### Constitutional Amendment of 1784
- Pushed by Cónsul Carlos Mendoza (1783–1787, NR-aligned).
- Restricted Church role in education to religious instruction only.
- Removed Church authority over secular schooling.
- Placed Church properties/missions under distrito government jurisdiction.
- Authorised expropriation of Church mission lands for urban development.
- Did NOT disestablish Church (Sartan Petran Catholicism remained official religion).
- Stripped Church of temporal power exercised since 1723.

### Expropriation of missions
- Church missions in rural districtos held substantial agricultural land (colonial-era grants).
- Transferred to distrito governments → sold/leased for urban development and agricultural reform.
- Church saw this as political attack disguised as land policy.
- Church argued: assault on Guaji people's right to organise own religious/educational life.
- State accused Church of conflating institutional self-interest with public good.

### Church response
- Publicly: parish priests and bishops denounced amendment from pulpit.
- Privately: channelled support to PD through parish networks.
- Church's defence of institutional interests + support for political liberalisation became intertwined.
- Alliance between Guaji clergy and PD survived and grew stronger.

## Citizenship Debate

- **Pre-1768**: Citizenship = Sartans and Guaji only. Kaftians and Bayani = residents, not citizens. Pay taxes, labour, serve in military, but no political rights.
- **NR position**: Citizenship = constitutional category, not humanitarian one. Extending to Kaftians/Bayani dilutes political identity. Republic = compact between "founding peoples."
- **PD position**: All peoples (Sartan, Guaji, Kaftian, Bayani) should be citizens. Kaftians on island 200+ years, Bayani 100+ years — not newcomers but generations-old communities. Republic that excludes large share = oligarchy, not republic.
- **Citizenship bills**: 1786 (defeated), 1791 (defeated, margin narrows), 1796 (defeated, margin narrows further).
- **Status by 1801**: Unresolved. Central political issue for next era.

## Piracy and Regional Security

- Capuyaquiran and Abyalan League continues anti-piracy mission.
- Coordinated naval patrols reduce piracy on major trade lanes.
- Joint courts process increasing cases in 1770s.
- Piracy NOT eliminated — Volisanian Civil War continues to destabilise Capuyaquiran Sea.
- Pirate bands operate from uninhabited islands beyond league patrol range.
- Republic's naval capacity grows slowly after 1761 Astillero Real seizure.
- Shipyard repairs/refits league patrol fleet, does not yet produce new warships.
- League reliance on Guajicutean shipyard = central role in regional security + burden on industrial capacity.

## Regional Politics

### Lacashe (closest ally, controversial)
- Alliance = cornerstone of Guajicutean foreign policy.
- PD opposes alliance due to Lacashe's chattel slavery.
- PD's anti-slavery position resonates with Guaji and Kaftian populations.
- NR defends alliance as pragmatic necessity.
- 1789: Lacashe declares independence (rather than accept Abolition of Slavery Act). PD calls for severing ties. NR refuses. Alliance survives but damaged.
- PD's anti-slavery stance = defining feature, permanent friction with NR.

### Asikyira
- Stable but distant. League cooperation on anti-piracy.
- Geographic distance limits commercial/diplomatic exchange.
- Natural ideological partner for PD (mixed Burawi-indigenous, indenture-based economy) but practical distance limits relationship.

### Volisanian Viceroyalties
- Balisca, Balboa, Tapuya: semi-autonomous, trade directly with Guajicutea.
- Important market for manufactured goods.
- Civil war makes routes unreliable, political status uncertain.
- End of civil war (1801) raises questions about their future status.

### Zongosphere
- Zong merchants expand presence in Waribá after 1765 emissary mission.
- Zong textiles, porcelain, manufactured goods → Waribá. Guajicutean sugar, tobacco, dyestuffs → Zong.
- Deepening Zong relationship politically controversial.
- NR views Zong as threat to commercial balance (values Breislandic trade despite deterioration).
- PD welcomes Zong as diversification of economic relationships.

## Culture, Art, and Music

### Literature
- Sartan-language literature by Guaji-born authors continues.
- Historical writing emerges as distinct genre (1780s): first comprehensive histories of Guajicutea in Sartan, drawing on Guaji oral traditions + Kari Yabí.
- Political pamphleteering (NR-PD competition) = new form of political literature.

### Music
- Most distinctive cultural form of period.
- Fusion of Sartan harmonic traditions + Guaji rhythmic structures = Guajicutean musical style (neither Sartan nor Guaji, something new).
- Waribá: cosmopolitan, absorbs Zong + Volisanian + Sartan + Guaji elements.
- Barikú: closer to military/naval traditions of colonial period.
- Parish church = primary venue for musical performance. Guaji clergy contribute choral/instrumental traditions blending religious and secular.

### Visual Art
- Less developed than literature/music but emerging from Church decorative traditions.
- Church painting/sculpture by Guaji artisans: Sartan religious iconography + Guaji natural motifs (volcanic landscapes, sea creatures, tropical flora).
- Style begins to influence secular art.

### Economy
- Plantation → light manufacturing shift accelerates.
- Astillero Real = centre of industrial cluster (ship repair, timber milling, rope-making, sailcloth, iron fitting).
- Sugar refining, tobacco curing expand (value-added processing).
- Textile production grows (imported raw cotton from Zong).
- Economic divide: plantation landowners (NR) vs. urban manufacturers (PD) reinforces political divide.
- Zong trade deepens, Breisland trade continues to decline.
- End of Volisanian Civil War (1801): uncertain future for viceroyalty trade.
