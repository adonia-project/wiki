## War of the Mesogean — New World theater

- **Fought primarily in Old World** — New World was secondary
- **New World warfare**: naval impressment, capture of merchant ships, no land combat
- **Volisanian ships**: sheltered in Guaji ports (Barikú, Waribá) before sailing to Old World
- **No fighting on Guajicutea** — both powers hesitant to escalate (would give Sarta/Mercia/Breisland pretext for protection)
- **Soldier stopovers**: common, tension but not conflict
- **War ends**: 1610 (after Sarta takeover)

## Sarta-Guajicutea War (1608–1609)

- **Belligerents**: Sarta vs Guajicutea (Carlos I)
- **Duration**: Late 1608 – early 1609 (brief)
- **Sartan forces**: Fleet sent from Sarta
- **Guaji opposition**: Limited — feudal lords' retinues, no standing army
- **Key event**: Capture of Tabakurí (early 1609)
- **Result**: Carlos I deposed. Colony established.
- **Carlos I fate**: Disputed — exile, imprisonment, or execution unclear

## Colonial administration (1609)

### Purge of feudal lords
- All 16 bikabás purged — removed from positions, stripped of territorial authority
- Bikabá territories → provinces managed by Sartan regional administrators
- Large bureaucracy and military brought in (first Sartan colony, heavy investment)
- No power-sharing with local elites

### Religious reorganization
- Both Burawi Catholic and Petran Catholic churches expelled
- Sarta follows Petran Catholicism (same tradition as Volisania, but Sartan-controlled)
- Sartan Petran Catholic missionaries and bishops brought in to replace existing clergy
- Removed last institutional ties to Burawa and Volisania

### Economic policy
- Farm expansion for agricultural export
- Slavery considered but rejected (slave trade souring internationally)
- Laissez-faire approach: courted foreign diaspora for cheap labor
- Bayani Baliscans = largest migrant group
  - Persecuted in Balisca (province of colonial Volisania)
  - Arabic-base language
  - Migrated in significant numbers to work in fields

### Language policy
- Writing adapted to Latin alphabet in late 1500s by Burawi missionaries
- Most Guaji spoke native language (Guaji)
- Sartans required all religion and trade conducted in Sartan
- Hard to enforce (rural population continued native language in daily life)
- **LANGUAGE ALIGNMENT NOTE (internal only, not for wiki)**: Over the next ~100 years, the Guaji language will undergo dramatic changes and modifications due to Sartan language pressure, eventually becoming essentially modern Spanish. This is the long-term linguistic trajectory — do not mention in wiki articles, but use as guiding context for future language development.

### Sartan Catholicism
- Sarta = Petran Catholic tradition (same as Volisania's, but under Sartan authority)
- NOT Burawi Catholic
- Sarta brought own missionaries and bishops (Sartan Petran Catholic hierarchy)
- Replaced both existing church establishments (Burawi and Volisanian Petran)
