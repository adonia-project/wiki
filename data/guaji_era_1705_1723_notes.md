# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1705–1723 (Post-War to Republic)

## Overview

Post-war period. Sarta bankrupted, landlocked. Sartan elite influx to Guaji. Companyia Reial grows wealthy from First War of the Isthmus (Volisania vs Zong, 1711–1716). Republican literature spreads. Don Vicente de la Vega leads peaceful revolution. 1723: Sarta cedes control to Padres de Guajicutea. Republic established — senate elected by landowners, Cónsul as head of state. Highly unequal: Sartan-Guaji elite in power, natives/Kaftians/Bayani excluded.

## Key Figures

### Don Vicente de la Vega
- Former Sartan nobleman (Don = noble title)
- Emigrated to Guajicutea during Sarta-Volisania War (1683–1705)
- Became large landowner through royal land sales
- Exposed to Enlightenment literature through trade connections with Mercia and Breisland
- Convinced that Guajicutea should be self-governing
- Convinced the king of Sarta to give up the colony — pragmatic argument: colony self-governing in practice, Sarta has no navy, republican movement growing, peaceful transfer preserves relations
- Leader of the Padres de Guajicutea

### Andrés Caldera (1705–1710)
- Continued as military administrator after Treaty of 1705
- Colony under military rule until 1710
- Civilian rule restored in 1710

## First War of the Isthmus (1711–1716)

- War between Volisania and Zong, fought in Balboa region
- "Isthmus" = geographic feature in Balboa
- Companyia Reial del Capuyaquiran expanded Astillero Real and port facilities to support Volisanian war effort
- Astillero Real fully operational under Volisanian management — producing vessels for Volisanian navy and merchant marine
- Companyia Reial invested in expanding dry docks, warehouses, port infrastructure at Waribá
- Companyia Reial grew wealthy from war demand (1711–1716)
- Brought Volisanian naval personnel and merchants to Guajicutea in large numbers
- Colonial economy benefited from increased commerce
- **1715: Volisania LOST the war**
- Loss opened trade routes to Zong territories — Zong, Louyang, Sinchew
- Cheap Zong goods flooded into Capuyaquiran market — cotton products, manufactured goods
- Prices dropped across the board
- Cotton products (raw cotton + textiles) cheaper to import from Zong than produce locally → Guaji cotton industry killed

## Economic Shift (post-1715)

- Cotton industry collapse forced shift to other exports
- **Sugar**: primary replacement cash crop. Tropical climate, existing infrastructure, high prices in Volisanian/Breislandic/Mercian markets
- **Tobacco**: already a pre-colonial export, expanded as secondary cash crop
- **Dyes** (indigo, annatto): tropical, high-value, low-weight, suited to smallholder production
- **Dried fish**: already produced, scaled for colonial population + foreign ships at Waribá
- **Cassava flour**: already a staple export, scaled for demand
- Trade pattern: raw materials/agricultural goods OUT, cheap Zong manufactured goods IN
- Sartan-Guaji landowning class invested in sugar processing + tobacco curing
- Smallholder farmers (Guaji, Bayani) shifted to subsistence food + cash crop farming
- Uneven: landowners profited, laborers/smallholders bore costs of transition

## Volisanian Trade Resumption

- Trade severance during Sarta-Volisania War (1683–1705) reversed by Treaty of 1705
- Companyia Reial presence = institutional framework for renewed commercial exchange
- Volisanian goods: manufactured items, textiles, metals → Guajicutea
- Guajicutean goods: agricultural products, dried fish, artisanal goods → Volisania
- Flowed through Companyia Reial channels at Waribá

## Sartan Elite Influx

- Began late 1690s, accelerated after 1705
- Sartan elites, nobles, wealthy families fled devastated mainland
- Guajicutea offered: relative stability, arable land, maritime trade access
- Increased population and tax base
- Brought wealth from Sarta → filled colonial coffers
- Increased reliance on foreign imports (new arrivals' consumption habits)
- Shifted colony's political center of gravity — Guajicutea more important than mainland Sarta
- Purchased land, established trading houses, integrated into colonial administration

## Republican Literature

### "Discurso sobre la República Libre" (Discourse on the Free Republic)
- Key republican text, circulated c. 1718
- Argued for self-governance modeled after ancient republics
- Proposed: senate elected by property holders, elected head of state for foreign affairs
- Drew on Commonwealth of Louyang (Zong colony) — representative system giving landowners equal representation
- Influenced by Enlightenment ideas from southern Illypnia (Mercia, Breisland)
- Circulated among Sartan-Guaji elite

### Broader intellectual context
- Enlightenment movement sweeping southern Illypnia (Mercia, Breisland)
- Commonwealth of Louyang in Zong colony = model for representative government
- Same intellectual fervor reaching Guajicutea through trade networks and Sartan émigrés
- Republican/liberal ideologies spreading among educated elite

## Royal Land Sales

- Sartan crown sold royal lands in Guajicutea to recoup funds
- Sold to: trading families, wealthy Sartan émigrés (war escapees)
- Created class of large landowners with economic power but no political representation
- This landowning class = political base of republican movement
- Same families that would form the Padres de Guajicutea

## Peaceful Revolution of 1723

- Don Vicente de la Vega convinced king of Sarta to give up colony
- Pragmatic argument: colony self-governing in practice, Sarta has no navy, movement growing, peaceful transfer preserves relations, refusal risks violent revolt
- King agreed — influenced by Enlightenment ideas and practical realities
- 1723: Sarta formally ceded control to Padres de Guajicutea
- Peaceful — negotiated through diplomatic channels, not military action

## Republic of Guajicutea

### Structure
- **Senate**: elected by landowners (property-based suffrage)
- **Cónsul**: head of state, represents senate in international affairs
- Modeled after ancient republics and Louyang Commonwealth
- Suffrage restricted to property holders

### Inequality
- Political power: Sartan-Guaji landowning elite
- Excluded: Guaji natives, Kaftians (descended from enslaved people brought during Burawi colonial period), Bayani (migrants who labored in agricultural fields for generations)
- Republic of the wealthy, by the wealthy, for the wealthy
- Highly unequal society at founding
- Sets up future conflicts

- **First War of the Isthmus**: 1711–1716. Volisania vs Zong in Balboa. Companyia Reial expands and grows wealthy.
- **Volisanian trade**: Resumes formally post-1705. Through Companyia Reial channels at Waribá.
- **Civilian rule**: Restored 1710. Military rule under Caldera 1705–1710.
- **"Discurso sobre la República Libre"**: c. 1718. Key republican text. Self-governance, ancient republic model, Louyang Commonwealth influence.
- **Royal land sales**: 1710s–1720s. Crown lands sold to trading families and wealthy émigrés. Creates landowning political base.
- **Don Vicente de la Vega**: Former Sartan nobleman, large landowner, republican leader. Convinced king to give up colony.
- **Peaceful Revolution of 1723**: Sarta cedes control to Padres de Guajicutea. Peaceful, diplomatic.
- **Republic of Guajicutea**: Senate (landowner suffrage) + Cónsul (head of state, international affairs). Unequal: Sartan-Guaji elite in power, natives/Kaftians/Bayani excluded.

## New nations/entities introduced

- **Zong**: Colonial power. War with Volisania in First War of the Isthmus (1711–1716, Balboa). Has colony of Louyang with Commonwealth system (landowner representation).
- **Commonwealth of Louyang**: Representative government in Zong colony of Louyang. Gives landowners equal representation in political process. Model for Guajicutea republicans.
- **First War of the Isthmus**: 1711–1716. Volisania vs Zong. Fought in Balboa region. Companyia Reial expands shipbuilding for war effort, grows wealthy.
- **Padres de Guajicutea**: Founding group of the republic. Sartan-Guaji landowners, merchants, intellectuals.
- **Cónsul**: Head of state title. Roman republican model. Represents senate in international affairs.
- **Don Vicente de la Vega**: Former Sartan nobleman, large landowner, republican leader. Convinced king of Sarta to cede colony.
