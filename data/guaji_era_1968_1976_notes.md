# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1968–1976 (Conservative Interlude and Economic Turmoil)

## Overview

Period from 1968 through 1976. The PPG won the 1968 presidential election, ending two decades of PLG dominance. The conservative government privatised the state-run hospitality industry (Programa de Hospitalidad) to raise funds for manufacturing investment, keeping taxes unchanged for the first four years. The PLG returned in 1972 in a coalition with the Kadarist Party (KdG), attempting to renationalise the airline and cruiseline and launching an inquisition into the privatisation. In 1974, the government raised income tax and passed the Universal Healthcare Act. The Camborián War and oil crises of the 1970s produced an economic downturn that caused flip-flops between the two parties. The era ended in 1976 with the start of the Camborián War.

## Public Mood

- Post-war optimism persisted but was tempered by hesitancy in government trust.
- People had less trust in the government — the PLG's pro-natalist and pro-immigration policies had produced rapid population growth, strain on resources, and tax increases.
- The birth rate was slowing down from the peak of the baby boom as the population stabilised and the initial surge absorbed.
- Unemployment was tough but recovering due to the new industry experiments (manufacturing investment from privatisation funds).

## The 1966–1967 Tax Changes (under PLG)

### 1966 Income Tax Raise
- The PLG government raised income tax as reparations funds started to dry up (reparations spread over ~10 years, 1948–1958; by 1966 winding down).
- The raise generated significant revenue but contributed to PLG unpopularity.
- Income tax was originally established during the Second War of the Isthmus (1893–1899) under PdI war leadership.

### 1967 Sales Tax
- The PLG government introduced a sales tax — a new tax on goods and services.
- Regressive (affects all consumers). Very unpopular.
- Combined with the income tax raise, the sales tax made the PLG increasingly unpopular.

## The 1967 Hospitality Workers Walkout

- December 1967: All hospitality workers walked out — a 2-month strike during the peak travel season before the 1968 election.
- The strike was a negotiation for higher wages. Hospitality workers' wages were low — the PLG government had not enforced labor protections for the hospitality sector.
- The strike caused economic disruption during the peak season — one of the reasons the PPG won the 1968 election.
- The walkout was a catalyst for the political shift: it demonstrated the PLG's lack of care for labor protections in the hospitality industry.

## The Conservative Government (1968–1972)

### Privatisation of Hospitality
- The conservative government broke up the state-run hospitality industry (Programa de Hospitalidad) into multiple private companies:
  - Three hotel chains (privatised)
  - The national airline (privatised)
  - The cruiseline (privatised)
- The privatisation raised funds for manufacturing investment.
- The privatisation was a core conservative policy — reversing the PLG's state-run hospitality model.

### Tax Policy
- The conservative government did not change taxes for the first four years (1968–1972).
- Taxes during this period were pretty low (inherited from the PLG era).
- The privatisation raised funds, reducing the need for tax increases initially.

### Manufacturing Investment
- Funds from privatisation invested in manufacturing.
- The manufacturing conundrum continued: what could Guajicutea make competitively? Focus on food processing, construction materials, textiles/crafts, light assembly.

## The 1972 Election and the PLG-KdG Coalition

- 1972: PLG returned in a coalition with the Kadarist Party (KdG, socialists).
- The coalition attempted to renationalise the airline and cruiseline (reversing the conservative privatisation).
## The 1973 Inquisition

- The PLG-KdG coalition launched an inquisition (investigation) into the privatisation of the hospitality industry.
- The minister of commerce, **Hernán Montes**, was arrested over the sale.
- The inquisition was a political persecution of the previous conservative government's officials.
- The arrest of the minister of commerce was the most prominent action of the inquisition.

## The 1970s: Camborián War and Oil Crises

- The [[Camborián War]] and oil crises of the 1970s produced an economic downturn.
- The downturn caused flip-flops between the PPG and PLG through the decade.

## 1974 Tax Increase and Universal Healthcare

- 1974: The government raised income tax again to support government coffers and increased spending.
- The **Universal Healthcare Act** was passed in 1974, establishing universal healthcare for all residents.
- The tax increase and healthcare act were part of the government's expanding social programmes during this period.
## The 1980s: Switch Back to Labor

- The economic turmoil of the 1970s eventually led to a switch back to labor in the late 1980s.
- The era ends in 1988 with the return of PLG governance.

### Assembly Seat Distribution

- 1 representative per 10,000 residents.
- Seats updated only after each official census (1950, 1960, 1970), applied starting at the November election of the census year.
- Each census takes effect at the November election of its year and lasts 8 years (until the next census takes effect).
- 1950 census (Jan 1950) → 1950–1956 elections (~49 seats). Next election after census = Nov 1950 (same year).
- 1960 census (Jan 1960) → 1960–1968 elections (~68 seats). Next election after census = Nov 1960 (same year).
- 1970 census (Jan 1970) → 1970–1978 elections (~140 seats). Next election after census = Nov 1970 (same year).
- ~1975 partial census (~750k): Mid-decade estimate. Population stats only. Does NOT adjust seats.
- Assembly elections every 2 years. Presidential elections every 4 years. Officials take office November of election year.
