## Plantation economy

- **Location**: Southern Guajicutea
- **Labor**: Enslaved Kaftians (imported since 1543)
- **Main buyer**: Burawa (New Burawa province, wartime economy)
- **Secondary buyer**: Volisania (had own colonies, smaller demand)
- **Beneficiaries**: Southern bikabás (feudal lords who controlled estates)
- **Problem**: Free peasants couldn't compete with slave labor → rebellion

## Economic depression (1573)

**Cause**: New Burawa's agricultural production recovering as Aruanãyára war wound down → reduced purchases of Guaji cash crops → plantation economy contracted

**Effects**:
- Plantations became unprofitable
- Peasant discontent intensified
- Dawit I proclaimed end of slavery
- Kaftian population freed and assimilated

## Kingdom of Murani (1564)

- **Established**: 1564 by diplomatic arrangement of Dawit I
- **Status**: Burawi protectorate (similar to Guajicutea)
- **Territory**: Three Murani islands (Maruba, Dukira, Waturi) — formerly Badweyn Islands
- **Marriage alliance**: Caracuran bikabári's daughters married to Murani king
- **Why Caracuran**: Caracurí tradition = eastern sea people, historical maritime connections to Greater Capuyaquiran
- **Burawi church role**: Wanted a new Christianization project; convinced to accept protectorate instead of direct rule
- **Murani king**: Baptized, took Burawi regnal name, became Boqor under Burawi church authority
- **Political logic**: Dawit I created a buffer state to prevent direct Burawi annexation of eastern islands

## Burawi-Volisania rivalry

| Aspect | Burawa | Volisania |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Port city | Waribá (NE coast) | Barikú (west coast) |
| Investments | Plazas, amenities, mission | Plazas, amenities, fort, Petran church |
| Religious tradition | Burawi Catholic Church | Petran Catholic Church |
| Military presence | Reduced battalion (post-1554) | Fort at Barikú (post-1554) |
| Goal | Court Dawit I's loyalty | Court Dawit I's loyalty |

**Costabella Agreement (1565)**: Reduced Volisanian courting effort. Both powers focused on mainland conquests.

**1580s**: Both powers withdraw attention from Guajicutea. Investments slow. Military presence reduced. Guaji gains autonomy but loses support.

**Late 1580s**: Relations sour. Cooperation breaks down. Strategic positioning begins.

Civic improvements (plazas, markets, amenities) concentrated in these three cities. Outside: feudal agrarian/mining society.

## Southern mining (1570)

- **Northern mines** (Mina Yaruma, eastern slopes of Cerro Caracurí): declining, centuries of panning depleted surface deposits
- **Monte Yukumá**: sacred/forbidden — source of yukubara (516 CE), mountain's domain
- **Taboo broken**: 1570, first mines along southern rivers of Monte Yukumá
- **Type**: Placer gold in river gravels
- **Reason**: Economic pressure (northern gold drying up, depression approaching)
- **Religious authority gone**: Bohíques/priestesses abolished under colonial reforms — no one to enforce the prohibition

## Costabella Agreement (1565)

- **Signed**: Costabella, Volisanian capital of Balboa colony
- **Parties**: Burawa and Volisania
- **Terms**:
  - Volisania aids Burawa in finishing Aruanãyára conquest
  - Burawa supports Volisania in conquering mainland Tapuya (1568–1593)
- **Effect on Guajicutea**: Reduced colonial competition. Both powers focused on mainland.
- **Sphere division**: Burawa = east/Aruanãyára front. Volisania = west/Tapuya front.

## Third Capuyaquiran War (1578–1581)

- **Belligerent**: Burawa
- **Target**: Remaining independent islands in Greater Capuyaquiran
- **Excluded**: Guajicutea and Kingdom of Murani (both protectorates)
- **Result**: Eastern archipelago fully divided between Burawa colonial territories and two protectorate kingdoms
- **Significance**: Last Capuyaquiran War; completes colonial partition of the eastern archipelago

## Island status by 1590

| Power | Territory | Status |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Burawa | Eastern Greater Capuyaquiran islands | Colonial territory |
| Burawa | Guajicutea | Protectorate (Kingdom of Guajicutea) |
| Burawa | Murani islands | Protectorate (Kingdom of Murani) |
| Volisania | Western Lesser Capuyaquiran islands | Colonial territory |
| Volisania | Mainland Tapuya territories | Colonial territory (conquest 1568–1593) |

Only Guajicutea and Murani remain as non-colonial entities. All other islands annexed.

## War of the Mesogean (1590)

- **Belligerents**: Burawa vs Volisania
- **Start**: 1590
- **Cause**: Breakdown of Costabella cooperation; disputes over mainland territories, trade routes, colonial spheres
- **Significance**: First direct conflict between the two colonial powers
- **Impact on Guajicutea**: Ends era of peace. Island caught between two powers. Dawit I's dual loyalties tested.
