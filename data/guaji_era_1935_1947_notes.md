# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1935–1947 (Great Adonian War)

## Overview

Period from 1935 through 1947, covering Guajicutea's involvement in the Great Adonian War. The island began as a staging ground for the Volisanian invasion of Zong Balboa, became a front line, was reduced to a Volisanian military territory, and was liberated when the NVP was overthrown in Volisania. Throughout, the Zong-funded Grupo de Liberación Guajicuteano (GLG) waged a domestic resistance campaign from the volcanic tunnels, growing in popularity as the fascist regime stripped away civil rights, interned the Zong population, and ultimately surrendered sovereignty to Volisania.

## Phase 1: Staging Ground (1935–1936)

- Volisanian and Balboan forces invaded Zong Balboa in 1935, opening the Great Adonian War.
- Guajicutea, with Waribá port already leased to Volisania and a Volisanian military base on its soil since 1930, was a staging ground for the invasion.
- Volisanian forces used Guaji as a stopover point for refueling and hiding ships.
- The island's technical independence (not Volisania, not Sarta) made it a safe harbour for naval operations, just as it had been a safe harbour for war production.

## The Grupo de Liberación Guajicuteano (GLG)

- A secret network funded by Zong as part of Guaji liberal restoration.
- Orchestrated attacks on political and military infrastructure.
- Originally small, then grew over time.
- The government had a hard time figuring out who was part of the GLG — covert, cellular structure.
- The GLG's growth and the government's inability to identify its members led to increasingly harsh crackdowns on civil life.

## Crackdowns on Civil Life (1935–1936)

- Curfews imposed.
- Ban on "unemployment" — effectively forced labor. Anyone without a job was conscripted into war production or military support.
- Government struggled to distinguish between genuine civilians and GLG operatives, leading to broad repression.

## Racial Segregation and Internment (1936)

- First racial segregation laws enacted in 1936.
- All Zong people in Guajicutea interned in a concentration camp (needs name) outside of Waribá.
- Privacy protection laws overturned — police could enter homes with no cause, no warrant.
- The internment of the Zong population was the most extreme racial measure of the fascist regime, contradicting the 1931 memorandum's advice against racial laws. The war changed the calculation — the Zong threat was now real (Zong bombing Guaji), and the regime used the war to justify what it had previously resisted.

## The Slow Stripping of Rights (1936–1941)

- Over the next 5 years, rights were slowly ripped away — incremental authoritarianism.
- Domestic population was still split on the GLG: employment was high, most people were fine economically, and the GLG's attacks were disruptive.
- The split: some saw the GLG as resistance fighters, others as terrorists disturbing a prosperous economy.
- The regime maintained public acquiescence through economic prosperity (war production jobs) while incrementally removing freedoms.

## Zong Bombing Campaign (April 1, 1941)

- April 1, 1941: Zong bombers launched a bombing campaign of the port of Waribá.
- Caused internal panic — the war had come to Guajicutea directly.
- Continued with biweekly raids by Zong aircraft on Volisanian military infrastructure on the island.
- The bombing shattered the illusion that Guajicutea was a safe harbour.

## Protests and the Massacre (1941)

- 1941: Street protests demanded the government stop the war.
- But it was clearly out of Guaji's hands — the island was a Volisanian military staging ground, not an independent actor.
- More crackdowns on protests.
- A massacre (needs name) happened during this time — state forces killed protesters.
- The massacre moved public favour toward the GLG, which became even more popular as the regime's violence turned from political repression to killing its own citizens.

## The Camp Riot (October 1941)

- October 1941: The Zong internment camp had a "riot."
- The riot was probably staged — forced killings of many Zong people by the regime, disguised as a prisoner uprising.
- News of the killings spread to the local community around Waribá.
- A crowd of people forced their way into the camp to free the Zong people.
- Subsequent arrest and trial of those rioters — people who tried to save the internees were prosecuted.
- The staged riot and the community response revealed both the regime's brutality and the public's growing willingness to resist.

## Guaji Becomes the Front Line (1942)

- Zong and Sinchew started pushing back on the northern campaign for Balboa.
- Pushed Balboa and Volisanian forces north towards Tapuya and its islands.
- Guaji became the front line of the war — the island was now directly in the combat zone.
- Hit by even more bombing campaigns.

## Relinquishment of Sovereignty (February 8, 1943)

- February 8, 1943: The Guaji government relinquished control to the Volisanian military.
- The nation was essentially a territory of Volisania — no longer even nominally independent.
- Caused mass rioting, which was squashed by Volisanian military force.
- The last pretense of Guajicutean sovereignty ended. The president and Jefe de la Asamblea were reduced to administrators under Volisanian military command.

## GLG Domestic Bombing Campaign (1943–1944)

- The GLG started their own domestic bombing operation — targeting vehicles, government buildings, and military infrastructure.
- GLG operatives hid in the many volcanic tunnels of Guajicutea — the island's geology provided a natural network of caves and lava tubes.
- The volcanic tunnels were ideal for guerrilla warfare: hidden, defensible, connected, and difficult for conventional forces to assault.
- The Volisanian military sent forces to try to extinguish the GLG.

## Battles of the Caves (1943–1944)

- The Volisanian military's campaign against the GLG in the volcanic tunnel systems.
- A series of engagements in the caves and tunnels of Guajicutea's volcanic highlands.
- The GLG used the tunnel networks for hiding, weapons storage, and as escape routes.
- The Volisanian military, trained for conventional warfare, struggled with the subterranean combat environment.
- The battles eventually led to the capture of the GLG leaders in 1944.
- The capture of the leadership effectively ended the GLG's organised resistance, though the war continued.

## Zong Offensive and the Superfortress (1945)

- 1945: Zong attempted to capture islands along the Tapuyan coast and head toward Guaji.
- This saw an increased concentration of Volisanian forces in Guaji.
- The island became the "superfortress" — the most heavily fortified position in the Capuyaquiran Sea.
- The Battle of Waribá: the city was firebombed by Zong forces. A major air battle fought over and around the city.
- The battle demonstrated that Guajicutea was no longer a safe harbour but a primary target.

## Volisanian Decampment and Famine (1946)

- 1946: Volisanian forces decamped from the island of Guaji.
- The decampment was an effort to move the fighting back to the mainland in the Tapuya campaign.
- The war was shifting away from Guajicutea as Volisania redirected its forces to the mainland front.
- **Famine (1946)**: As Volisanian forces withdrew, Volisanian funding for the war production economy stopped. The factories and infrastructure built to serve the Volisanian military lost their patron overnight. Workers were laid off, food imports ceased, and the economy that had been oriented entirely toward military production collapsed. A famine began as the island could no longer feed itself — agriculture had been neglected during the war production boom in favor of factory work, and the import networks that supplied food were Volisanian military supply chains that vanished with the troops.

## Liberation and Reconstruction (1947–1948)

- 1947: The NVP was overthrown by liberal revolutionaries in Volisania.
- The overthrow of the fascist government in Volisania forced the retreat of all remaining Volisanian forces from Guaji.
- July 3, 1947: Last Volisanian forces left the island.
- Guajicutea was liberated not by domestic forces but by the collapse of the fascist regime in Volisania itself.
- A group of conservative-moderate leadership that had been kept around during the war (the "rump government") took over governance.
- Weeks of domestic unrest followed the liberation — political violence, score-settling, food shortages.
- August 7, 1947: The rump government requested a coalition occupation by Baliscan forces to restore order. Baliscan forces arrived and occupied the island.
- The occupation lasted 6 months (August 1947 – February 1948). Objectives: restore order, release political prisoners (from Campo de Las Minas and other detention), restore food supply.
- During the occupation, Asikyira sent aid — food, medical supplies, reconstruction assistance.
- December 18, 1947: New constitution established — the **Third Republic of Guajicutea**. The modern nation was instituted.
- January 1, 1948: The **Adonian Community** (AC) was created to prevent future conflicts. Guajicutea was invited to be an initial member.
- February 1948: Baliscan occupation ended.
