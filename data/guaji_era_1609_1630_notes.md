# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1609–1630 (Sarta Colony, Early Period)

## Overview

First two decades of Sartan colonial rule. Colony established 1609 after Sarta-Guajicutea War (1608–1609). Two governors: Diego Vela (1609–1618), Alonso Mera (1618–1630). Period marked by trade embargo, famines, rapid population growth, and urbanization.

- Base (1618): ~39,000
- Natural growth (~1.5%/year): ~7,000 over 12 years
- Arrivals (capped at 500/year): ~6,000 over 12 years

- Famine mortality offset: some reduction from 1617 famine aftermath
- Total: ~39,000 + ~7,000 + ~6,000 + ~6,000 ≈ ~58,000–60,000

## Governors

### Diego Vela (1609–1618)
- First colonial governor
- Appointed by Sartan crown upon colony establishment
- Oversaw: purge of bikabás, renaming of territories with Sartan provincial names, church expulsion, language policy, early Bayani migration
- Weathered: trade embargo (1610–1617), famine of 1612
- Recalled 1618 — likely due to ongoing crisis and transition to post-embargo policy

## Distrito Renaming (1609)

The Sartan colonial administration under Diego Vela renamed all 16 feudal bikabá territories with Sartan provincial names — a mix of saint names and geographic descriptors following metropolitan naming conventions. The territorial boundaries were retained; only the names changed. These names persisted through the colonial period and into the republic (1723+).

### Alonso Mera (1618–1630)
- Second colonial governor
- Managed: post-embargo stabilization, trade resumption (1617 Petran Church deal), arrival cap (1618), population growth, urbanization
- Colony achieved stability by 1630: trade flowing, population growing, administration established
- Still financial burden on Sarta but no longer in crisis

## Trade Embargo (1610–1617)

- Burawa and Volisania imposed complete trade embargo on Sartan colony
- Sarta had no other colonies or friendly ports in Capuyaquiran Sea
- Forced to resupply from Breislandic Lurandia (~1500km away, no intermediate ports)
- Every shipment: food, tools, weapons, personnel — full 1500km journey
- Colony became significant financial burden on Sartan crown
- Smuggling through Kingdom of Murani (Burawi protectorate) at margins
- Not total in practice — informal trade continued
- But official trade halted, colonial administration couldn't rely on consistent supply

## Petran Church Intervention (1617)

- Sartan Petran Church shared religious tradition with Volisania's Petran Catholic Church
- This shared tradition provided diplomatic channel political authorities couldn't/wouldn't use
- Church argued humanitarian situation (two famines) demanded relief
- Volisania agreed to provide aid and resume limited trade
- Volisania did NOT recognize Sartan sovereignty over Guajicutea
- Burawa did NOT participate in the arrangement
- Broke the embargo, established practical trade relationship
- Alleviated colony's supply crisis

## Deforestation

- Agricultural expansion to feed growing population + cash crop production
- Cleared forested land on volcanic slopes and southern lowlands
- Accelerated during embargo years (1610–1617) — couldn't import food
- Soil erosion on steeper volcanic terrain → reduced yields → contributed to 1617 famine
- Post-1617: continued expansion but with imported food supplementing domestic production
- Southern lowlands = plantation agriculture center
- Central river valleys + western leeward lowlands = food crops

## Urbanization

### Tabakurí (inland capital)
- Seat of colonial governor
- Administrative center of colony
- New construction: government offices, Sartan Petran Catholic cathedral, military barracks, colonial residences
- Population growth: Sartan administrators + families, Guaji/Bayani migrants for bureaucracy/service economy

### Waribá (NE port)
- Primary port and naval base
- Sartan ships arrive through Waribá
- Port facilities expanded: new docks, warehouses, naval installations
- Primary entry point for Bayani migrants
- Center of trade with Volisania (post-1617) and Breislandic Lurandia
- Population: port labor, military, migrant communities

### Barikú (west port)
- Secondary port and military outpost
- Volisanian fort (post-1554) repurposed by Sartan administration
- Port activity increased post-1617 trade resumption
- Smaller than Waribá
- Growth: military presence, trade with western Capuyaquiran islands

## Key established details

- **Treaty of 1610**: Ends War of the Mesogean (Burawa-Volisania, 1590–1610). Normalizes relations in New World.
- **Trade embargo**: 1610–1617. Burawa + Volisania vs Sartan colony. Non-military undermining.
- **Breislandic Lurandia**: Breislandic territory ~1500km from Guajicutea. Sarta's nearest resupply point. No intermediate ports.
- **Famines**: 1612 (first, less severe), 1617 (second, more severe). Caused by embargo + agricultural insufficiency + deforestation/erosion.
- **Petran Church intervention**: 1617. Sartan Petran Church negotiated with Volisania through shared Petran Catholic tradition. Secured aid + trade resumption.
- **Arrival cap**: 1618. 500/year. Applied to all migrants (Bayani, others). Did not include Sartan colonial personnel.
- **Population**: ~36,500 (1609) → ~60,000 (c. 1630). Nearly doubled in ~20 years despite famines.
- **Governors**: Diego Vela (1609–1618, first, establishment + embargo + first famine), Alonso Mera (1618–1630, stabilization + growth + urbanization).
- **Bayani**: Persecuted in Balisca (Volisanian province). Migrated to Guajicutea for agricultural labor. Spoke Bayani.
- **Kingdom of Murani**: Burawi protectorate (established 1564). Served as smuggling conduit during embargo.
- **Era end**: 1630 tentative. Population milestone. Colony stabilized. User indicated unsure when era ends.
