# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1947–1956 (Third Republic, Reconstruction)

## Overview

Post-war reconstruction era. Waribá was mostly destroyed; Tabakurí and Barikú were largely intact. A unified coalition of non-fascist factions — the Partido de la Restauración Guajicuteana (PRG) — governed the transitional period, assisted by Adonian Community consultants. A new constitution established the Third Republic with broad rights guarantees. The Labor Party won the first election overwhelmingly and invested reparations funds in sustainable economics, reconstruction, and a reorientation of Waribá toward hospitality and specialty agriculture. A baby boom and the planning of a new northwestern city accompanied the reconstruction.

## The Constitution (December 18, 1947)

- The Third Republic of Guajicutea was established.
- The constitution included the following guarantees and provisions:
  1. **Full enfranchisement** — universal suffrage.
  2. **Freedom of speech, protest, right to assembly, press** — fundamental civil liberties.
  3. **Prevention of presidential usurption** — constitutional mechanisms to prevent a repeat of the Jefe de la Asamblea subversion.
  4. **Restriction of foreign ownership** — national institutions, whether public or private, cannot be owned by foreign entities.
  5. **Religious protection** — freedom of religion.
  6. **Language protection** — no national language. Common languages are Guaji and Sartan, both taught to all.
  7. **Right to education** — universal right to education for all.
  8. **Citizenship** — all pre-war residents are granted citizenship, regardless of race or origin.
  9. **Military service** — mandatory 2-year service program for all 18-year-olds.
  10. **District restructuring** — new districts established (see GIS, need names).

## Reconstruction Era

### Waribá Rebuild
- Waribá was mostly destroyed during the war (firebombing, 1945).
- Rebuilt over 8 years (~1948–~1956) to focus on hospitality and specialty agriculture.
- Post-war peace brought people eager to travel and spend — tourism demand.
- Waribá transitioned to hospitality industry and specialty agriculture.
- Nature and history restoration — environmental recovery, historical preservation.
- Creation of large museums — cultural institutions built as part of reconstruction.
- Baby boom — Waribá growing too fast, straining infrastructure.

## The 1954 Recession and Reparations

### Global Postwar Recession (1954)
- A global postwar recession hit in 1954, correlated with the tail-end of the Great Adonian War's economic effects.
- Guajicutea was not severely affected due to being padded by reparations funds from Volisania.
- The reparations were still flowing in 1954 (spread over ~10 years, 1948–1958), providing an economic buffer.
- Guajicutea's economy was reconstruction-driven (high employment for construction) rather than export-dependent, so the global recession had less direct impact.
- The recession may have slowed some trade but did not cause major unemployment because the domestic reconstruction economy was still active.

### Reparations
- The Adonian Community made Volisania pay reparations to Guajicutea for reconstruction.
- The reparations were spread over approximately 10 years (1948–1958), not a lump sum.
- Covered: Waribá reconstruction (8-year project), food imports (famine recovery), industrial rebuilding, infrastructure repair, museum and cultural restoration.
- The Labor Party's strategy was to invest reparations funds in building sustainable economics rather than consuming them on short-term relief.
- The annual amount was substantial — enough to cover a significant portion of the government budget — but not unlimited. It buffered the 1954 recession but did not make Guajicutea wealthy.
- The reparations funded the construction boom, the new northwestern city, and the reorientation of Waribá toward hospitality and specialty agriculture.
