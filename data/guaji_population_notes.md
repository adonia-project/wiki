# Guaji Population Notes

Internal working notes on Guajicutea's estimated population and settlement distribution from 0 CE to 1500 CE.

## Assumptions

- Island area: ~3,278 km²
- Climate: tropical, wet windward east / drier leeward west, rich volcanic soils
- Agriculture: intensive root-crop complex (cassava, yams, makú) with conuco mounds
- Settlement model: most people live in dispersed rural/jungle hamlets and villages; coastal areas concentrate larger trade-oriented settlements; highlands concentrate smaller religious/mining settlements
- No writing system; all figures are estimates based on agricultural carrying capacity and Caribbean analogues

## Population categories

| Category | Description | Typical share of population |
|----------|-------------|----------------------------|
| Rural/jungle | Dispersed farmers in river valleys and lower slopes | ~70–78% |
| Coastal centers | Trade ports and surrounding dependent villages | ~17–24% |
| Religious/mountain | Ceremonial centers, cave sites, mining camps, catacomb caretakers | ~5–8% |

## Key demographic events

### 0–500 CE: Two-tribe formation
- Slow, steady growth as the root-crop agricultural system matures
- Population concentrates in river valleys and along coasts
- Guajicutea Vieja becomes the major ceremonial/residential center in the south/west
- By 500 CE, the island supports ~22,000 people split between Yukurí (west/mountain) and Caracurí (east/sea)

### 516 CE: Yukubara and unification
- The mudflow destroys Guajicutea Vieja and kills or displaces several thousand people in the western/southern region
- Population drops to ~19,000
- Survivors merge into unified Guaji; the Caracurí form becomes dominant
- Coastal trade networks are disrupted but then rebuilt under unified control

### 516–1100 CE: Unified expansion
- Recovery is rapid because the agricultural base is intact
- Barikú grows as the cross-island redistribution port after 516 CE
- Trade with both Lesser and Greater Capuyaquiran expands
- Population reaches ~38,000 by 1100 CE

### 1143 CE: Great hurricane
- Devastating storm kills thousands and destroys coastal villages
- Population drops to ~29,000
- Recovery is slower than after 516 because coastal infrastructure is damaged
- Settlement patterns shift: more cautious coastal development, larger grain reserves

### 1143–1300 CE: Recovery and expansion
- Population recovers to ~35,000 by 1300 CE
- Tributary system established under Kanu Maku (1268–1295 CE)
- Trade networks expand

### 1344–1392 CE: The Kari Wirá (Sea Hurricane)
- Tapuyic Empire clashes with Guajicutea over the Boraku islands and western trade routes
- Raid on Yukubá (1367 CE) destroys port infrastructure
- Population dips to ~30,000 during the worst years (~1370 CE)
- War ends 1392 CE — slow recovery begins
- See `guaji_tapuyic_war_notes.md` for full timeline

### 1392–1500 CE: Post-war recovery and peak
- Population recovers steadily after the war
- Sewn-plank war fleet developed under Bika Kuru
- Settlements fortified, trade routes restored
- **Population reaches ~40,000 by 1500 CE — the pre-contact peak**
- After 1500, colonization brings disease and disruption; population begins to fall

| West coast (Yukurí/Yukubá/Barikú) | 30–35% | Fewer but larger settlements, trade wealth |

| Highlands | <5% | Caves, catacombs, mining camps, sparse permanent settlement |
| South coast | minimal | Exposed to open ocean, few permanent settlements |

## Notes on methodology

- Figures are rough estimates based on agricultural carrying capacity, not census data.
- Pre-contact Caribbean population densities are debated; estimates range from 3–7 people/km² to 20–50 people/km² in intensive agricultural zones.
- Guajicutea's volcanic soils and year-round growing season support higher densities than marginal Caribbean islands, but the mountainous interior reduces usable land.
- The 40,000 peak (~12 people/km² across the whole island) is plausible for an intensive root-crop economy on volcanic soil.
- Population peak is at 1500 CE (pre-contact), not 1400 — the Kari Wirá war (1344–1392 CE) caused a dip to ~30,000, with full recovery and growth to 40,000 only by 1500.
