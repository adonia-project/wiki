# Guajicutea Era Notes: 1824–1853 (Reconstruction and War of Liberation)

## Overview

Period from Guajicutea-Lacashe War (1824–1825) through outbreak of War of Liberation (1853). Virtuous Revolution (July 12, 1824) → Lacashe invasion (Aug 15, 1824) → 6-month Lacashe occupation (same race codes retained) → Sartan Volunteer Army helps overthrow Lacashe → Lacashe withdraws (Feb 15, 1825). First decade: rebuilding, formalized tax code, military modernization, cash crop exports restart. Second Republic formed (Oct 12, 1828). First election (June 12, 1829). Barirí presidency (1829–1837). RG splits into PdI and PdA (May 15, 1832). PdA more popular initially (trauma). Depression (1834–1838). Lacashe-Asikyira War (1838–1840) → demand for imports → PdI ascendant. PdI becomes corrupt. PpLT formed (1851). War of Liberation (1853–1856).

## Guajicutea-Lacashe War (1824–1825)

- **Start**: August 15, 1824 — Lacashe fleet dispatched to Guajicutea
- **Context**: Power vacuum after NC withdrawal (Pastry War). Lacashe supported 1812 NC invasion, allied with occupation government.
- **Occupation**: 6 months (Aug 15, 1824 – Feb 15, 1825). Same race codes, miscegenation laws, antiliberal laws retained.
- **Resistance**: Restored government (led by Javier Barirí) + Guarda Revolutionary fought from jungle interior.
- **Sartan Volunteer Army**: Arrangement of Sartan soldiers, volunteered to support Guaji. Not official Sartan military deployment — facilitated arrangement. Sarta's motivation: countering Lacashe's influence in Capuyaquiran Sea.
- **Liberation**: Sartan Volunteer Army + Guarda Revolutionary (under Yukurí) fought Lacashe garrison at Waribá and Tabakurí. Lacashe withdrew February 15, 1825.
- **Kurí returns**: Mateo Kurí returned from exile in Sarta on March 3, 1825. Symbol of pre-occupation republic. Did not resume presidency.

## Javier Barirí — First President

- **Name**: Javier Barirí. Surname: bari (storm) + -rí = "person of the storm." Storm = upheaval of occupation and revolution.
- **Role**: RG leader (1824–1828) → first president of Second Republic (1829–1837)
- **Terms**: 2 terms (July 1, 1829 – July 1, 1837). 4-year terms, no term limits.
- **Retired**: July 1, 1837. **Died**: August 1, 1837 (one month after retirement).
- **Party alignment**: PdA (isolationist, post-trauma). The PdA was more popular during his presidency.
- **Significance**: Founding father of the Second Republic. Led resistance, transition, and first two terms of elected government.

## Political Parties

### RG (Restored Government) — 1824–1832
- Unified post-revolution movement. Not a formal party initially.
- Included Guarda Revolutionary leadership (Barirí, Yukurí, Nasarí) and PD underground networks.
- Functioned as governing coalition during transitional period.

### Partido del Internacionalismo (PdI) — from May 15, 1832
- **Platform**: Greater integration with emerging international order. General economic liberalization.
- **Base**: Commercial interests, urban populations, pro-trade factions.
- **Trajectory**: Initially less popular (trauma → isolationism). Gained support after Lacashe-Asikyira War (1838–1840) created demand for imports. Became ascendant in post-depression growth period. Became increasingly corrupt — leadership entangled with commercial networks that benefited from liberalization.

### Partido de Aislamiento (PdA) — from May 15, 1832
- **Platform**: Formation of an autarky (self-sufficient economy). Reduction of involvement in foreign affairs.
- **Base**: Post-trauma population, rural communities, those who suffered most from occupation.
- **Trajectory**: Initially more popular (trauma, desire for isolation). Special relationship with Sarta was the exception to isolationist policy (because of Sartan Volunteer Army). Lost ground after Lacas-Asikyira War shifted sentiment toward internationalism.

### Partido por la Tierra (PpLT) — from 1851
- **Platform**: Rural and worker-based. Greater working conditions.
- **Base**: Rural populations, agricultural workers, laborers.
- **Trajectory**: Formed in response to PdI corruption and perception that PdI and PdA both served elite interests. Began challenging seats in Asamblea Nacional. Third force in previously binary PdI-PdA landscape.

## Regional Anti-Slavery Movement

- Guajicutea's anti-slavery position (established under PD, maintained through occupation) aligned with regional shift
- Nations boycotted Lacashe's slave economy → redirected trade benefited Guajicutea's restored economy
- **1836**: Tapuya banned slavery — one of first in region
- **1848**: Balboa banned slavery
- Progressive abolition increased economic and diplomatic pressure on Lacashe (last slave-holding state)
- Boycott movement → War of Liberation

## Lacashe-Asikyira War (1838–1840)

- Conflict between Lacashe and Asikyira
- Guajicutea officially neutral
- Secretly supplied goods to Asikyira using re-established trade networks and maritime infrastructure
- Covert support reflected: anti-Lacashe stance + strategic interest in weakening Lacashe without direct engagement
- Shows Guaji using trade as a tool of foreign policy during reconstruction

## War of Liberation (1853–1856)

- Regional war against Lacashe (last slave-holding state)
- Alliance: Burawa, Volisania, Guajicutea (and possibly others)
- Aim: end Lacashe's slave economy
- **Guajicutea's role**:
  - Small troop assignment dispatched
  - Granted docking rights to Burawa and Volisania
  - Waribá and Barikú used as staging points for allied fleet
  - Drew on island's role as resupply hub + rebuilt naval infrastructure
- War began 1853, continued to 1856 (extends beyond reconstruction period)
- This is the event that ends the 1824–1853 era

## Key Figures

- **Javier Barirí** — RG leader (1824–1828). First president (1829–1837). 2 terms. Retired July 1, 1837. Died August 1, 1837. Surname: bari (storm) + -rí.
- **Tomás Yukurí** — Guarda Revolutionary military commander. Oversaw integration of Guarda fighters into reconstituted military. Led military operations against Lacashe occupation.
- **Diego Nasarí** — Guarda Revolutionary political commander. Transitioned to civilian government role. Bayani.
- **Mateo Kurí** — Exiled president (1804–1812). Exiled to Sarta 1813. Returned March 3, 1825. Symbol of pre-occupation republic. Did not resume presidency.

## Government Structure

- **President**: 4-year terms, no term limits. Directly elected.
- **Asamblea Nacional**: Representative assembly. Seats by population. 2-year terms, no term limits.
- **Second Republic established**: October 12, 1828.
- **First election**: June 12, 1829 (8 months after formation).
- **First inauguration**: July 1, 1829.

## Religion and Church-State Relations

- **1824 separation**: Church and state separated as part of the Restoration. Unlike the 1784 amendment (which expropriated church lands and limited church power punitively), the 1824 separation was pragmatic and amicable.
- **Parallel institutions**: The state built its own secular institutions — civil schools, state registries, government ceremonies — alongside existing church structures. The church was not attacked or dismantled.
- **Parish credibility**: Parish-level clergy earned credibility during NC occupation (parish networks = PD underground + Guarda Revolutionary infrastructure). Institutional hierarchy was more compromised (re-empowered under Leyes Antiliberales) but restored government chose reconciliation over purge.
- **Expropriated land debate**: Asamblea Nacional debated compensating the church for lands expropriated under 1784 amendment. Debated but never resolved. No compensation paid. Remained a point of tension but did not escalate to open conflict.
- **Guaji Catholicism**: Syncretic tradition continued — three powers mapped to Trinity, cremation hybrid practice, lunar calendar rural survival. Deepened as resistance identity during occupation.

## Arts and Culture

### Realism (post-1824)
- Immediate post-occupation movement. Painters, poets, writers who lived through Bloody Decade documented executions, house burnings, guerrilla warfare.
- Documentary character — recording what happened, not interpreting.
- Centred in Tabakurí and Waribá (printing presses, exhibition spaces, literary circles).

### Proto-Symbolism (1840s–1850s)
- Emerged in late 1830s–1840s. Drew on Guaji symbolic vocabulary: Bikumaku glyphs, three powers (sea/mountain/sky), calendar iconography, catacomb carvings.
- Applied inherited symbols to contemporary painting/poetry to represent trauma at symbolic register.
- **First Guajicutean art to achieve international influence** — spread from Guajicutea to Sarta to Illypnia. Reversal of usual cultural flow (Guajicutea historically a receiver of Illypnian influences).

### Key Artists

- **Sebastián Biku** (c. 1808–1862) — Painter. Central figure. Surname: bikú (carved year-mark), plain root, no suffix. Early realist works documenting Bloody Decade. Late 1830s: began incorporating Bikumaku glyphs and three powers into canvases. Travelled to Sarta 1845, exhibited there — the bridge to Illypnia.
- **Ana Flores** (c. 1815–1890) — Painter. Sartan surname, urban-born, mixed Sartan-Guaji heritage. More abstract than Biku. Earth-toned palettes, landscapes as witness. Stayed in Guajicutea, deepened the visual vocabulary.
- **Andrés Guma** (c. 1810–1858) — Poet and essayist. Surname: guma (fire), plain root. The theorist. Argued Guajicutean art should draw from its own symbolic tradition, not imitate Illypnian forms. Essays circulated in Sarta, gave movement intellectual weight abroad.

### Surname pattern note
- Not every Guaji surname ends in -í/-rí. Biku and Guma use plain roots. Flores uses a Sartan surname. Variety in naming patterns reflects the mixed culture of republican-era Guajicutea.

## Demographics

### Population Trajectory

| Year | Population | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| 1824 | ~155,000 | Post-Virtuous Revolution. After 12 years NC occupation + Bloody Decade. |
| 1825 | ~155,000 | After Lacashe war. Minimal change (short occupation). |
| 1835 | ~170,000 | First decade of reconstruction. Growth resumes. |
| 1840 | ~180,000 | Steady growth. Economic recovery. Depression ending. |
| 1853 | ~195,000 | Pre-War of Liberation. ~28 years of growth. |

- Growth from ~155k to ~195k (~40k increase over 29 years, ~0.8%/year) is almost entirely natural increase.
- Immigration very low during this period. Sartan colonial arrival cap (500/year) abolished with end of colony in 1723, not reinstated, but no significant migration occurred.
- Depression (1834–1838) slightly slowed growth rate.

### Bloody Decade Casualties
- Casualties mostly poor rural people — Guaji, Bayani, Kaftian agricultural communities.
- Race codes targeted non-Baliscan/non-Sartan populations. Rural villages in interior = primary reprisal targets.
- Natural growth during 12-year occupation (~18k at 1%/year) roughly offset casualties + disrupted births (~16k). Net: roughly flat (153k → 155k).

## Collective Sentiment

- **Trauma**: Generation grew up under foreign rule — public beheadings, house burnings, guerrilla warfare. Psychological consequences carried into post-occupation period.
- **Compounded experience**: Virtuous Revolution (July 1824) → Lacashe invasion (August 1824). One month of freedom before another occupation.
- **Early reconstruction**: Exhaustion, desire for isolation. PdA's popularity = visceral rejection of foreign involvement, not ideological commitment to autarky.
- **Special relationship with Sarta**: Sole exception to isolationism. Grounded in Sartan Volunteer Army's role, not strategic calculation.
- **Depression (1834–1838)**: Deepened sense of betrayal — liberation hadn't produced prosperity.
- **Shift to PdI**: After Lacashe-Asikyira War (1838–1840), driven by economic necessity, not ideological conversion. Population needed imports and trade revenue.
- **Late 1840s–early 1850s**: Mood shifting. Proto-Symbolism = processing trauma at symbolic level (moving beyond raw documentation). PpLT formation (1851) = political energy moving from PdI-PdA divide toward class/labour questions (looking forward, not backward).
- **War of Liberation (1853–1856)**: Cathartic quality — fighting Lacashe (the 1824–1825 occupier) directly for the first time.

## Internal Notes (not for wiki)

- **RG = Restored Government**: The post-Virtuous Revolution government. Initially military/revolutionary, transitions to civilian rule. RG splits into PdI and PdA on May 15, 1832.
- **Barirí's party**: Aligned with PdA (isolationist). Makes sense — he led through the trauma period and the PdA's isolationism matched the post-occupation sentiment.
- **Special relationship with Sarta**: The Sartan Volunteer Army created a bond. Even the PdA (isolationists) maintained the Sarta relationship. The exception to isolationist policy.
- **PdI corruption**: PdI leadership became entangled with commercial networks. Financial interests drove policy. This created opening for PpLT.
- **PpLT = Partido por la Tierra**: "Party for the Land." Rural/worker base. Working conditions. Formed 1851. Third force.
- **Next era**: Post-1853. War of Liberation (1853–1856) and aftermath. Party conflict (PdI vs PpLT). Mechanization, industrialization. Population growth. Kaftian immigration from Lacashe. Ethnic conflicts from immigration.
