| Year | Population | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|

| 1804 | ~148,000 | First presidential election. Kurí inaugurated. |

- Growth rate: ~1% per year (consistent with previous era's trajectory).
- Post-invasion reduction: ~5,000 lost to combat casualties, displacement, and famine-like conditions during 2-month invasion.
- Internal rebellions post-1812 likely caused further population disruption (displacement, casualties, emigration of NR exiles).

| Guaji | ~85,000 | 59% | Largest group. Rural majority. Natural increase since pre-colonial era. PD's core base. |
| Bayani | ~23,000 | 16% | Descendants of migrants from Balisca (arrived 1609+). Continued migration + natural increase. Agricultural labourers, increasingly urban artisans. |

### Composition notes

- **Guaji**: Consistently the majority. Rural, agricultural. PD's electoral base. The 1803 enfranchisement didn't change their status (already citizens since 1723) but the shift to population-based assembly representation increased their political weight.
- **Bayani**: Second-largest minority. Arrived as agricultural labourers during Sartan colonial period (1609+). 1803 amendment granted them full citizenship for the first time. Previously excluded from political participation despite ~200 years on the island.
- **Sartan/Sartan-Guaji**: The political and economic elite. NR's base. The 1803 reforms reduced their political dominance — population-based representation meant their concentrated urban populations had fewer seats relative to the rural Guaji majority.
- **Kaftians**: The smallest of the four main groups. 1803 amendment granted them full citizenship for the first time. Descendants of enslaved people, freed in 1571, but economically marginalised for ~230 years. The PD's enfranchisement of Kaftians was the movement's most radical act and the most contested element of the reform.
- **Other**: Zong merchant community established in Waribá since 1765. Small but commercially significant.

## Electoral Implications of 1803 Reform

The shift from equal representation (2 senators per distrito) to population-based representation in the Asamblea Nacional had significant demographic consequences:

- **Rural districtos gained seats**: The more populous rural districtos (San Mateo, San Andrés, Costa del Sol, Valle del Sur) received more assembly seats than the less populous urban ones (San Esteban, San Ventura).
- **Guaji political weight increased**: As the rural majority, Guaji voters gained the most from proportional representation. The PD's base was now structurally advantaged.
- **Sartan urban elite disadvantaged**: The Sartan concentration in cities (Tabakurí, Waribá) meant fewer seats per voter under the new system. The old NR strongholds lost relative representation.
- **Kaftian and Bayani enfranchisement**: Newly enfranchised voters were concentrated in rural agricultural districtos and urban labour communities — both PD-leaning constituencies.

## Impact of 1812 Invasion on Demographics

- **Combat casualties**: ~2,000–3,000 estimated (military and civilian). Guajicutean military units and loyalist militias bore the brunt.
- **Displacement**: Rural populations in contested areas fled to interior or to Barikú (western port, less affected by naval invasion).
- **NR exile departure**: Ramón Cifuentes and his supporters returned with the invasion force. Post-invasion, additional PD sympathisers and government officials may have fled or gone into hiding.
- **Post-invasion rebellions**: Sustained insurgency in rural districtos caused ongoing displacement and casualties through the years following 1812.
- **Occupation forces**: Northern Confederation garrison troops stationed in Guajicutea — not counted in civilian population figures.

## Key Demographic Notes

- The 1803 reform was the most significant demographic-political shift since the republic's founding. The enfranchisement of ~35,000 Kaftian and Bayani voters (combined) transformed the electorate.
- The PD's electoral base was structurally reinforced by population-based representation — Guaji rural voters + newly enfranchised Kaftian/Bayani voters = a majority of the electorate.
- The invasion disproportionately affected the Guaji population (rural insurgency areas) and the PD-aligned communities.
- The occupation government under Partido Unidad Guajicuteana represented a minority of the population — installed by force, not electoral mandate.
- All figures are estimates. No census data exists for this period in the worldbuilding. Numbers should be treated as approximate.
